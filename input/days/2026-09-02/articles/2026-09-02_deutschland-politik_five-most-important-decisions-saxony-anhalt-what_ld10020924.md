# The five most important decisions in Saxony-Anhalt: what you need to know before election night

*This Sunday, a new state parliament will be elected in the East German state. For the first time, an AfD politician has a realistic chance of becoming state premier. However, Ulrich Siegmund does not want to govern at any cost.*

## Will Ulrich Siegmund be the first AfD state premier?

![AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund aims for an absolute majority.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2026/08/13/17611c0a-1bd3-4ce8-a20d-b05ddacea709.jpg?crop=5318,3540,x0,y0)
*AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund aims for an absolute majority.* (Sean Gallup / Getty)

All eyes will be on one man on election night. AfD lead candidate Ulrich Siegmund is the first in his party to have a realistic chance of becoming state premier of a federal state.

The AfD is clearly leading in the polls. It currently stands at just over 40% and could double its result from the last state election in 2021. The far-right party is almost 20 percentage points ahead of the second-strongest force, the conservative CDU.

However, Siegmund does not want to govern at any cost. He said during the election campaign that he must be able to rely on a stable team with which he could implement his promises. He maintained that this was only possible in a sole AfD government. To achieve this goal, however, he depends on losses among the other parties. If parties like the SPD, Greens, FDP, and BSW fail to enter the state parliament, a result of around 40% would be enough for the AfD to govern alone.

Such a result would have consequences reaching far beyond Saxony-Anhalt. For the AfD, it would be a historic triumph. The former anti-euro protest party could hardly complete its rise to a major party more clearly. However, after the election, it would also have to prove its ability to govern for the first time. The state chapter in Saxony-Anhalt has not exactly stood out for professional leadership in the past. It is at the center of a cronyism scandal, which, however, is not reflected in the polls.

An absolute majority for the AfD in the state parliament would also likely plunge the other parties into a crisis. It would be a clear signal that the firewall is unsuitable for containing the far-right party. The result of the election in Saxony-Anhalt will certainly lead to discussions about how to deal with the AfD on election night.

## Can the CDU form an anti-AfD coalition?

The CDU's lead candidate, Sven Schulze, faces difficult decisions. He took over the office of state premier from his party colleague Reiner Haseloff in January. However, Schulze cannot match the popularity of his predecessor, who, as a «state father,» was able to unite many voters behind him. If the AfD misses an absolute majority in the state parliament, he faces only bad options for a potential government formation.

## Will the SPD and the Greens make it into the state parliament?

The SPD and Greens could become the kingmakers in Saxony-Anhalt. They are just above the 5% hurdle in the polls. Even a narrow re-entry into the state parliament would have a major impact on possible coalitions. An absolute majority for the AfD in the state parliament would become less likely.

The prospect of being able to prevent a sole AfD government has mobilized the political periphery of the SPD and Greens. The organization Campact, for example, is campaigning with significant resources to encourage «strategic» voting – meaning for the Greens and SPD. According to the polls, it looks like the plan could succeed.

For both parties, however, more is at stake than just their re-entry into the state parliament. The Greens have a difficult standing in East Germany. In 2024, they were already ousted from the Brandenburg state parliament. In the election campaign, they are trying to score points with a focus on proximity to citizens. Party chairman Felix Banaszak, for example, is driving a taxi through the East to find out what «moves» people. This is intended to prevent the party from sinking into insignificance in the East.

For the SPD, the election could even have consequences for the party leadership. In the state elections in Baden-Württemberg and Rhineland-Palatinate, it suffered defeats of historic proportions. Its former core voters, the workers, have turned to the AfD. No improvement is in sight, which is increasingly putting the chairpersons, Labor Minister Bärbel Bas and Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil, under pressure. Losses in Saxony-Anhalt would likely further fuel the debate about a change in leadership.

## Will the FDP succeed in its resurrection?

The FDP must fear that its crisis in Saxony-Anhalt will continue. According to polls, the Liberals could fail to clear the 5% hurdle. They would then only be represented in five state parliaments.

The election in Saxony-Anhalt is also the first since Wolfgang Kubicki took over the party leadership. He stands for a change of course. In the federal «traffic light» coalition, the party still supported many rather left-liberal decisions. Kubicki, however, wants to score points with issues such as freedom of expression, migration, and the free market economy. The result is therefore also an initial indicator of whether his strategy is working.

## Is the BSW's meteoric rise over?

For the BSW, the election in Saxony-Anhalt will decide whether it is more than a political flash in the pan. The party founded by Sahra Wagenknecht is currently just below the 5% hurdle. It is thus far from its former successes in Brandenburg, Thuringia, and Saxony. In West Germany, it is not yet represented in any state parliament.

The state chapter in Saxony-Anhalt is specifically trying to appeal to voters who want a change in policy but do not want to vote for the AfD. The BSW lead candidates spoke out in favor of ending the firewall. If they make it into the state parliament, they are open to working selectively with the far-right party. Specifically, they envision an impartial state premier who, they said, would govern with shifting majorities in the state parliament. However, this course is controversial within the party. During the election campaign, several members left the party, including some in leadership positions.
