# Could the federal government remove an AfD-led state government from power?

*Ahead of the Saxony-Anhalt election: FDP leader Wolfgang Kubicki on federal coercion against a possible AfD state government – coercive measures for legal violations. When would such measures be permissible?*

In view of possible future electoral victories for the AfD, politicians from other parties are discussing steps to prevent the right-wing populists from abusing institutions: The instruments range from a ban on political parties and cutting off AfD state governments from intelligence exchange, all the way to drastic steps such as federal coercion. This was introduced on Monday by Wolfgang Kubicki, the leader of the Liberals (FDP).

If necessary, the federal government could strip a state government of power if it does not comply with federal laws, Wolfgang Kubicki said. The Liberal politician does not actually believe that the AfD truly poses a threat to the Federal Republic of Germany. «It would be bad, but not an operational accident for democracy, if the AfD were to govern in Saxony-Anhalt,» Kubicki said. However, even a far-right government must adhere to the laws, he maintained.

But is this legal instrument, provided for in Article 37 of the Basic Law, truly a sharp sword? Since the strengthening of the AfD in recent years, federal coercion has been increasingly discussed in legal scholarship and the media – though without any practical application: Since the Federal Republic of Germany was founded, it has not been applied a single time.

## What is federal coercion?

If a federal state fails to fulfill its obligations under the Basic Law or a federal law, the federal government, with the consent of the Federal Council, the representation of the German states, can take the «necessary measures.» Article 37 of the Basic Law stipulates that it has the right to issue directives to the state and its authorities. To exercise this right, it can also appoint a «commissioner.» However, there is no jurisprudence on this matter: Federal-state conflicts have always been resolved in court, and coercive measures against a state have never been used.

## When can federal coercion be applied?

A prerequisite is a concrete violation of federal duties, such as the refusal to implement federal laws, or the disregard of a binding judgment by the Federal Constitutional Court. A possible refusal by a state to accept asylum seekers, who are distributed within the Federal Republic according to the so-called «Königstein formula» (which dictates the distribution of asylum seekers within the Federal Republic), serves as a model case in the debate: In 2024, AfD chairman Tino Chrupalla said in an ARD summer interview that his party «will no longer participate» in this.

Below the threshold of such a concrete legal violation, federal coercion is in any case inadmissible. Constitutional law scholar Rupert Scholz also warns in «Kommunal» magazine against overly reckless use of federal coercion. Attempts to undermine a democratic election result politically or administratively risk a «shift to an authoritarian state system,» he warns.

## What measures would be possible?

Federal coercion is primarily conceivable as a so-called substitute performance: The federal government itself carries out what the state unlawfully fails to do. In practice, this could mean that a commissioner from Berlin takes the place of a state official and assumes those duties.

The deployment of the Federal Police or the police of another state on behalf of the federal government is conceivable; fiscal measures such as blocking EU funding are also being discussed. However, such instruments are excluded as purely punitive measures: The intervention must aim to bring the state back to lawful conduct.

## **Who decides on federal coercion?**

The federal government decides on the coercive measure. It has political discretion as to whether to act. However, it requires the approval of the Federal Council, which must decide on the coercive measures by absolute majority – so the hurdle is high. The affected state, for its part, can seek interim legal protection and file a complaint with the Federal Constitutional Court.

## Is the federal government allowed to deploy the military?

The deployment of the German armed forces is excluded from federal coercion, which would be a significant difference from the «Reich execution» (a form of federal enforcement) provided for in the Weimar Constitution, which occurred, for example, in 1919 when the central government in Berlin deployed the Reichswehr against the Munich Soviet Republic. This was a four-week attempt to establish a socialist form of government in Bavaria.

Interventions in judicial independence are also considered inadmissible, as are all steps that endanger the existence of the state or its constitutional bodies. The interventions under federal coercion are in any case temporary in nature. Kubicki’s words about «stripping of power» therefore sound overly absolute: The state government remains in office; federal coercion is solely intended to eliminate an unlawful state of affairs.
