# Tenant runs lucrative subletting business, leading to conviction

*Disability pensioner's illegal subletting for profit*

For at least three and a half years, the scheme operated undetected: The tenant of an apartment in Zurich's District 5 discovered a lucrative sideline, renting out the rooms to three subtenants at a time for profit, rather than living in the apartment himself – all without the landlord's knowledge.

He rented out the apartment for more than he paid for it. The 59-year-old disability pensioner, a Sri Lankan national, was convicted of fraud by a penal order.

According to the final penal order from the Zurich-Limmat prosecutor's office, which the NZZ reviewed, the accused signed a rental agreement for the apartment in Zurich's District 5 in September 2017. The agreement stipulated occupancy by a maximum of two people and explicitly prohibited subletting. The rent was 1439 Swiss francs per month.

## Untruthful reply to landlord's letter

The landlord had long suspected that his tenant was not living in the apartment himself. But it was not until September 2024 that he wrote him a letter. In it, he asked the disability pensioner to inform him within 10 days of receiving the letter who was actually living in the apartment and whether any subtenancy agreements existed. The accused falsely replied that he had been living in the apartment with another man for several years, but that this person was not a subtenant, he said.

However, the subsequent criminal investigation uncovered completely different facts: From approximately April 2018 to April 2021, three of the tenant's compatriots lived in the apartment. Each of them paid him 650 francs. This provided the accused with a monthly income of 1950 francs. He thus made a monthly profit of 511 francs from his undeclared subtenants. This amount was 35.5% above the actual rent.

In a second, shorter period, from September 2022 to March 2023, three subtenants paid him a total of 1650 francs, which was still 14.7% above the actual rent. The penal order stated that the accused generated a total profit of 19,662 francs through his actions, which he was not entitled to, while he himself lived elsewhere.

Through his conduct, the disability pensioner had misled the landlord about the apartment's actual occupancy, the penal order said. This error, which he caused, led the landlord to forgo legally stipulated measures to recover the unlawfully obtained additional earnings over all those years, it added.

## Fraudulent intent affirmed

The accused acted intentionally and with intent to enrich himself, and also deliberately fraudulently through his false reply to the letter, the penal order stated. The actual use of the apartment, moreover, could only have been verified by the landlord with considerable effort. His ability to check was limited, it noted, as the privacy of the main tenant was protected and the landlord could not conduct an apartment viewing or unannounced inspections without specific cause.

The 59-year-old disability pensioner was convicted of fraud and sentenced to a suspended monetary penalty of 120 daily rates at 60 francs each, totaling 7200 francs. The enforcement of the monetary penalty was suspended during a two-year probationary period. However, the accused also had to pay a fine of 1000 francs and 1600 francs in procedural costs, for a total of 2600 francs. Any civil claims were not mentioned in the penal order.
