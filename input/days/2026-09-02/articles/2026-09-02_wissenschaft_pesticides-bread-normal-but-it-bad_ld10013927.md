# Pesticides in bread: It’s normal – but is it bad?

*Not only fruits and vegetables, but also bread often contains pesticide residues. Experts explain how concerning this is for health and how to protect yourself.*

**Reader question: How high are pesticide residues in bread, and are there differences between white bread and whole-grain bread?**

Bread is very popular. In both Switzerland and Germany, it is among the most beloved foods. Around 40 kilograms are consumed per person annually in both countries. In terms of nutritional content, white flour and whole-grain products differ significantly, but neither variant is immune to undesirable contaminants: residues of chemical-synthetic pesticides, sprayed in the field to combat pests, can be detected in many grain products – such as glyphosate, chlormequat, ethephon, and carbofuran.

«In 2023, we evaluated more than 2,000 official samples from various European countries and found a total of 65 pesticides. Every third grain product was contaminated with residues,» says Annemarie Botzki from the consumer protection organization Foodwatch. The frequency of pesticide residues varied among the different types of grain – residues were found more frequently in wheat bread and rolls than in emmer and rye samples. Nevertheless, it cannot be generally stated that there are always differences between whole-grain and white bread.

While it seems obvious that more pesticides enter bread when the entire grain, including the outer layers, is processed – meaning whole-grain bread would be more contaminated – Botzki says: «Many factors play a role in the measurement result, such as grain type, cultivation, origin, storage, and processing.»

In addition, whole-grain bread offers many health benefits, for instance, due to its high content of dietary fiber, complex carbohydrates, and B vitamins. Therefore, those who consume a lot of bread are better served by the whole-grain variety.

## Pesticide residue limits mostly adhered to

Although traces of pesticides can often be detected, the limits set by authorities are rarely exceeded. The European Union sets maximum residue levels for each active ingredient and grain type – for glyphosate, for example, 10 milligrams per kilogram for wheat and rye, but 20 milligrams for barley and oats. In Switzerland, specific limits apply that are based on EU regulations.

«In our study, 1215 out of a total of 2243 samples showed residues, but only 14 of them exceeded the permissible limits,» says Botzki about the situation in Germany. Other tests show similar results. In the latest monitoring by the Swiss Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (FSVO), none of the examined samples exceeded the permissible limits.

The Federal Institute for Risk Assessment classifies traces of pesticides in grain products as «generally harmless to health» as long as the limits are not exceeded. Botzki also does not want to stir up fears. «Nobody drops dead after eating a slice of bread,» she says. However, she does not want to simply ignore the pesticide contamination: «Firstly, we want to draw attention to the high use of pesticides, especially in grain cultivation. Secondly, many consumers want residue-free grain or do not even know that many grain products contain pesticide residues.»

## Another way

Some experts do not take as relaxed a view as many authorities. Ecotoxicologist Carsten Brühl from the Technical University of Kaiserslautern-Landau, for example, says: «Pesticides threaten our environment, animal and plant life, and thus our livelihoods, as well as our health. And their use would not be necessary to the extent currently practiced.»

He refers to examples from conventional agriculture such as Kraichgau Korn in Germany or IP Suisse with its ladybug label in Switzerland. Both have relied on pesticide-free grain for decades. Regarding official limits, Brühl says: «They consider neither the multiple exposure in many products nor the fact that in our daily lives we are exposed to a variety of environmental toxins that accumulate.» For example, anyone who consumes contaminated bread, fruit, and vegetables, lives near a vineyard or field where spraying occurs, and inhales fine dust daily, accumulates a considerable cocktail of pollutants that can potentially harm health, Brühl adds.

Individuals seeking to minimize their risk must take action themselves. For example, by switching to organic products, which are less contaminated because pesticide use is prohibited in organic farming. Regional products also typically show fewer residues, and some conventional suppliers, like those mentioned, also forego pesticides. Beyond that, it is advisable to consume different types of bread and grain, as grain contamination can vary.
