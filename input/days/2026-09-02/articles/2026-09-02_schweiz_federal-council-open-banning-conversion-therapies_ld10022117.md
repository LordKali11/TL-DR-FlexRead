# Federal Council Open to Banning Conversion Therapies

*For years, the Federal Council firmly opposed a ban on conversion therapies. Now it has changed its stance. However, it leaves the question of implementation open.*

**Adrien Woeffray, Bern** · Schweiz · 2026-09-02 · 632 words · [ld.10022117](https://www.nzz.ch/schweiz/der-bundesrat-zeigt-sich-offen-fuer-ein-verbot-von-konversionstherapien-ld.10022117)

So-called conversion therapies are also conducted in Switzerland. The Federal Council has now stated this somewhat trivial, yet long-known fact in black and white in a report published on Wednesday.

According to a study by the University of Bern, 9.5% of homosexual and bisexual individuals and 15.5% of trans people have already been confronted with efforts aimed at changing or suppressing their sexual orientation or gender identity. However, due to the limited data available, it is not possible to quantify how widespread conversion measures are in Switzerland, the study states. An exact calculation is difficult, it adds, especially since the practice often takes place in secret.

These «pseudo-therapies,» predominantly offered in religious institutions, ranged from subtle influence to severe infringements on physical integrity.

Even those who offer such conversion measures have refrained from using the term «therapy» since the 2000s. The criticism of the methods simply became too great. Since then, the offerings have operated in vague terms, such as «support in wrestling with one's sexuality» or under the guise of «depression treatment.»

Figures have shown that conversion therapies also take place in Switzerland since 2019. Since then, the Swiss LGBTIQ+ Panel has been collecting data as part of a longitudinal study. The University of Bern study, on which the Federal Council now relies, also refers to these figures. However, when asked whether this practice should be banned, the Federal Council avoided a clear answer for years.

Already at the end of 2021, the National Council had adopted a postulate by the now-retired Erich von Siebenthal (Swiss People's Party). In it, von Siebenthal called for an examination of «the actual extent of these practices» in Switzerland and an assessment of how current law «responds to this situation.»

## **Mounting Pressure**

The Federal Council «clearly and decisively» rejects conversion therapies. Nevertheless, its reasoning remains contradictory. On the one hand, it considers the existing legal framework to be sufficient. Current criminal, civil, and health professional law already offers extensive protection against conversion therapies, the Federal Council argues. Therefore, a federal legal regulation does not appear «absolutely necessary,» it maintains.

On the other hand, it is nevertheless receptive to a ban. It justifies this, in part, with the growing pressure from politics and society. Indeed, resistance against the «gay healers» is expanding. Last May, the Swiss Bishops' Conference and the Council of the Federation of Swiss Protestant Churches spoke out in favor of a ban in a coordinated action. In addition, the cantons of Neuchâtel, Valais, and Vaud have already introduced a criminal ban on conversion measures. In eleven other cantons, political efforts are underway in the same direction.

The Federal Council also wants to prevent a federal patchwork of regulations. Different cantonal regulations could impair their effectiveness. Last but not least, it is also important to «send a clear signal against conversion measures,» the Council states. The Federal Council still leaves open in what form this will occur.

## Many Possibilities, Many Buts

Although the Federal Council outlines various ways in which a ban could be introduced, it also always points out the limitations of the options.

An extension of the Equality Act does not convince the Federal Council, as it would miss the original purpose of the law, it argues. It considers an expansion of the anti-racism criminal provision unlikely, since parliament explicitly spoke out against including gender identity as a criterion. However, a separate law, analogous to the burqa ban or the draft ban on Nazi symbols, is conceivable, it says. The mandate, however, must come from parliament, the Council maintains.

The next opportunity will arise with the implementation of the motion «Prohibit and criminalize conversion measures against LGBTQ persons» by the Legal Affairs Committee of the National Council, the Council suggests. The responsible Legal Affairs Committee of the Council of States had suspended deliberations on the motion until the Federal Council's report was available. Now the way is clear for a new beginning.
