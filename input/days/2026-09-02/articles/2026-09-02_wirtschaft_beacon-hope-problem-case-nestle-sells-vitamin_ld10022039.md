# From a beacon of hope to a problem case: Nestlé sells vitamin brands at a loss of billions

*Nestlé once pursued ambitious plans in the health business. CEO Philipp Navratil is scaling them back.*

Exactly one year after taking office, CEO Philipp Navratil is streamlining Nestlé's vitamin portfolio. The food giant found a buyer for its mainstream vitamin, mineral, and dietary supplement business, it announced Monday evening. The division was sold to the American private equity firm Yellow Wood Partners for about 800 million Swiss francs. Seven brands, primarily sold in the U.S., including Nature’s Bounty, are being divested.

The mainstream vitamin business, with sales of 1 billion Swiss francs, is relatively small by Nestlé standards (group sales: 89.5 billion Swiss francs). Furthermore, Navratil had already announced in February that he intended to sell it. Nevertheless, the sale marks a «line drawn under a painful episode of value destruction,» as Vontobel analyst Jean-Philippe Bertschy writes.

## Departure from previous ambitions

Nestlé once had ambitious plans in the health business. Under former CEO Mark Schneider, the group acquired various vitamin and supplement providers. For example, in 2021, it bought The Bountiful Company for about 5.2 billion Swiss francs, which included most of the brands now being sold again.

Schneider wanted to increasingly position Nestlé as a health company that sells products at the intersection of nutrition and health. Under his predecessors Paul Bulcke and Peter Brabeck-Letmathe, the Nestlé Health Science division had been specifically established for this purpose.

However, hopes for the vitamin business were not fulfilled. The market is competitive: Nestlé is likely making less revenue from mainstream vitamins today than it did at the time of purchase in 2021. In addition, Nestlé had acquired many brands too hastily. IT problems arose during integration, which led to the group being unable to deliver products for months.

## Significant write-down

The sale is an admission that Nestlé bet on the wrong horse with mainstream vitamins. It is also associated with a significant write-down. When presenting its half-year figures for 2026, Nestlé announced that a write-down of 1.3 billion Swiss francs was to be expected. The fact that it is not more is partly due to the retention of certain vitamin brands.

Despite the sale, Nestlé will continue to be involved in the health business. However, CEO Navratil wants to concentrate on products where Nestlé potentially has a competitive advantage. For example, the group is retaining the high-priced vitamin brands Solgar and Pure Encapsulations, where Nestlé aims to leverage its in-house research. In addition, health activities are being more closely integrated with the rest of the business.

But one thing is clear: Navratil is abandoning plans to build an independent pillar in Nestlé's health business. In doing so, he is undoing the legacy of his predecessors. He already dissolved the Nestlé Health Science division in the spring.
