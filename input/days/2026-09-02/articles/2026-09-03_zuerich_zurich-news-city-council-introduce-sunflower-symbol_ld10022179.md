## City of Zurich to introduce sunflower lanyard system for people with invisible disabilities

_glu._ In the Zurich City Council on Wednesday (9/2), a postulate by Jehuda Spielman (FDP) and David Garcia Nuñez (AL) received a majority of 100 to 15 votes and was referred to the City Council, the Swiss news agency Keystone-SDA reported.

The Sunflower Lanyard System aims to make invisible disabilities such as ADHD, autism, dementia, or epilepsy recognizable. The sunflower symbol can be worn around the neck on a lanyard. The company's website states this is intended to signal that the person might need additional support, time, or understanding.

The postulate from June 2025 states that the sunflower is an internationally established symbol. Companies such as SBB and Swiss International Air Lines already use the Sunflower Lanyard System. The postulate calls for an introduction to be examined in municipal operations such as administration, schools, and playgrounds. Furthermore, it should be determined how the public can be informed about this symbol and how city employees can be trained, the postulate added.

## After headscarf ban in schools: Youth party mobilizes against two Green cantonal parliament members

_lip._ The headscarf ban in Zurich schools, which the cantonal parliament adopted on Monday, is causing political waves. On Wednesday (9/2), the Young Greens launched a petition to urge Gabi Petri and Daniel Heierli, two long-standing members of the Green parliamentary group, to resign.

Petri and Heierli were among the 87 council members who approved the SVP motion two days ago. The decision passed narrowly with 82 opposing votes and 6 abstentions. The motion aims to «fundamentally prohibit the wearing of clothing that covers the heads of women and girls for religious or ideological reasons in all public schools and kindergartens.»

The Young Greens describe the motion as «clearly Islamophobic,» as they write on their website. They said it was particularly disappointing that Green cantonal parliament members Gabi Petri and Daniel Heierli also approved it and thus helped it pass. The Young Greens stated that one expects Green politicians to stand behind the principles of religious freedom, anti-racism, and self-determination. Since this was not guaranteed, the youth party is calling for Petri and Heierli to resign.

While the Young Greens apparently tolerate no dissenting opinions on the headscarf issue, the cantonal government already expressed a positive view on the matter prior to the debate. Education Director Silvia Steiner (Centre) said earlier this week that the cantonal government would now work out a proposal for the implementation of the matter within two years.
