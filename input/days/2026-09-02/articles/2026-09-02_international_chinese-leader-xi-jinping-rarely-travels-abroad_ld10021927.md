# Chinese leader Xi Jinping rarely travels abroad. Why then Egypt, of all places?

*Egypt has become central for China: Beijing is investing billions, training with the Egyptian air force – and securing influence over one of the world's most important trade routes.*

Xi Jinping makes himself scarce. He spends little time abroad, just over two weeks a year. Before the coronavirus pandemic, it was closer to a month. He visited an average of thirteen countries per year then; today, it is only six.

While Xi Jinping is regularly sought out by heads of state and government from around the world, he himself honors only a few with a visit. When Xi travels, it is often to international summits. This makes state visits, when he does undertake them, all the more significant.

On Tuesday evening, Xi landed in Egypt for a two-day visit with President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi. What role does the country on the Nile play in China's global strategy?

Officially, the 70th anniversary of bilateral relations is the occasion for the visit. Ten years ago, for the 60th anniversary, Xi was last in Cairo. The deeper reason is that Egypt, with its 110 million inhabitants, is an important hub for China's interests in the region. Egypt has a unique location, connecting the Arab world with the African continent, Xi Jinping writes in his article published in Egyptian state media before his visit.

Additionally, the Suez Canal is one of China's most important trade routes to Europe. At the same time, Egypt is a central mediator in the Gaza war and a political heavyweight between Africa, the Middle East, and the Gulf states, with Cairo as the seat of the Arab League.

## Egypt: New focus of the Silk Road

In recent years, Egypt has become one of the most important countries in China's «New Silk Road» initiative. Xi Jinping's gigantic geopolitical project lost significant momentum during the pandemic. Many recipient countries incurred high debts. However, as economist Christoph Nedopil of the University of Queensland in Australia has found, the structure of the New Silk Road has changed since then. Private Chinese companies are playing an increasingly larger role. Their share of total Chinese engagement has risen from 12.5% in 2020 to almost 48% in the first half of 2026.

The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) is now experiencing a significant comeback. Combining investments and construction contracts, Chinese engagement in the first half of 2026 was as high as in any first half since the initiative's launch in 2013. For Egypt alone, Nedopil recorded investment announcements totaling 12.2 billion dollars. Of this, 10 billion dollars is for the construction of a new steel plant by a Chinese company.

«The data show very close interdependencies between China and Egypt that have grown in recent years,» Nedopil says. «For China, Egypt is a stable country with manageable risks.» In addition to steel, China is also investing heavily in energy, chemicals, and technology, he adds.

A Chinese-Egyptian industrial zone has existed in the Suez Canal since 2008. Chinese companies increasingly see Egypt as a location to outsource production capacities. The products are labeled «made in Egypt» instead of «made in China» — and if they meet the corresponding rules of origin, the companies can benefit from Egypt's free trade agreements, for example with the EU. «Egypt is a gateway for Africa, the Middle East, and Europe,» Nedopil explains. China states that about 60% of Chinese exports to Europe are transported via the Suez Canal.

The EU has become aware of China's «Made in Egypt» strategy. In August, the EU Commission extended countervailing duties on imports from two Chinese-controlled fiber optic manufacturers in Egypt. The factories were specifically set up to supply the European market, the EU Commission stated.

## Closer military cooperation

China and Egypt are also moving closer together in terms of security policy. Immediately before Xi's visit, the air forces of the two countries began joint maneuvers for the second time. During the first exercise in 2025, China sent fighter jets, reconnaissance aircraft, and tanker aircraft to Egypt, among others. The Chinese aircraft covered nearly 6,000 kilometers. The Defense Ministry described it as the first time China's air force had deployed such a formation to Africa for joint training. The exercise had also been a test of China's ability to deploy troops and aircraft over long distances, it added.

This year, cooperation is expanding. Chinese J-16 fighter jets trained with French Rafales of the Egyptian air force. A Chinese tanker aircraft even refueled a Rafale in the air. Such exercises offer the People's Liberation Army a rare opportunity to practice with Western-developed combat aircraft.

This is noteworthy because Egypt has been one of America's most important security partners in the Arab world for decades. The country receives around 1.3 billion dollars in military aid from the U.S. annually. Nevertheless, Cairo is expanding its military relations with China. In 2024, for example, two Chinese warships visited Alexandria, then trained with the Egyptian navy.

Egypt does not want to replace Washington with Beijing. Rather, the government is trying to broaden its partnerships. «Egypt and China have become partners who jointly contribute to strengthening the role of the Global South in the international system,» President Sisi wrote in state media. Since 2022, Egypt has been a dialogue partner of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, and since January 2024, it has belonged to the BRICS states.

## Xi: Conserving his strength

Xi Jinping turned 73 in June. His increasing age is probably one of the reasons why he travels less. Beijing does not provide an official explanation. However, the reduced travel activity is hardly a sign of political weakness; rather, the opposite is true: Xi can afford to send Premier Li Qiang to summits in his place — and to have heads of state from all over the world come to Beijing.

Xi Jinping probably wants to remain at the top for many more years and is therefore more consciously conserving his strength, writes Neil Thomas, a researcher on Chinese elite politics at the Asia Society Policy Institute.

If Xi travels personally, his interests must therefore be great. Egypt, in any case, seems to have been worth the effort for him.
