# Pension or Capital: How to Avoid Risks and Misadvice in Retirement

*More and more retirees choose a capital payment instead of a pension when drawing their pension fund assets. This decision carries risks. The pension fund association reacts.*

Should one draw the accumulated assets in the pension fund as a pension or as a capital payment? For many prospective retirees in Switzerland, this is one of the most important financial decisions of their lives. After all, it determines whether one has a regular monthly income in retirement or whether one has to manage the money oneself.

In the past, many here favored the monthly pension, but in recent years there has been a clear trend reversal.

According to the new pension statistics from the Federal Statistical Office (FSO), 45% of individuals who received new benefits from their pension fund in 2024 opted exclusively for a capital payment. Another 36% chose exclusively a pension, and 19% opted for a mix of pension and capital.

## **Flexibility for retirees**

Several reasons are likely responsible for the increase in capital withdrawals. According to a study by the investment company Swisscanto from April of this year, almost half of capital recipients cite greater financial flexibility as the reason. A third of prospective retirees want to invest the money themselves, and about a third also see advantages in inheritance. Another factor often cited is the decreased conversion rates at pension funds. However, these play a minor role in the decision, the Swisscanto study found.

Representatives of the financial industry are reluctant to talk about another reason that is likely responsible for the increase in capital withdrawals: conflicts of interest and misadvice in financial institutions. Banks and asset managers are not welfare institutions and therefore have an interest in their clients withdrawing pension fund assets and investing them with them.

«When a financial company conducts consultations with clients and simultaneously operates asset management, the threshold for a conflict of interest is quickly reached,» says Lukas Müller-Brunner, Director of the pension fund association Asip.

## Pension funds hesitant to advise

Many employees approaching retirement are desperately looking for reliable advice. However, they often do not know who to turn to. From Müller-Brunner's perspective, pension funds are obvious points of contact for prospective retirees because they are independent. «However, many pension funds hesitate to give their insured members individual assessments on retirement planning beyond basic information,» says the Asip Director.

Many foundation board members of pension funds were concerned about potential legal consequences of any advice and wanted to rule out liability, Müller-Brunner says. Moreover, many also had no training as financial advisors or planners and consequently did not feel able to give advice here — especially since the decision was so important, he adds.

## New tool for prospective retirees

Asip now wants to strengthen pension funds in advising on this decision. That is why it is launching the «Pension Compass» for prospective retirees. This online tool includes a calculator with which various scenarios can be individually simulated. In addition, frequently asked questions about the different options are answered simply and understandably.

Pension fund representatives can refer to the tool when asked about this decision and discuss the results with insured members. «They may still need advice from a financial institution, but they will not go there empty-handed and will be familiar with the most important facts and any pitfalls regarding their situation,» says Müller-Brunner.

## **No blanket answer for pension or capital**

Meanwhile, it should be noted that there is no blanket right or wrong answer when deciding between «pension or capital.» «The pension finances a long life; capital finances freedom,» says Reto Spring, CEO of the Pension Forum platform and a financial planning expert.

«Many people think: What I have in hand, I have,» he says, explaining the increase in capital withdrawals. They wanted to remain flexible, and tax advantages were only a secondary concern, he adds. Many also wanted to pay off their property or intended to bequeath something to their descendants later. However, not a few ignored the fact that their life expectancy could be very long.

This is also where advice from financial institutions comes into play again. In many consultations — or rather sales pitches — assumptions about life expectancy were made in such a way that capital withdrawal appeared more advantageous than a pension, Spring says. However, if one were to live to 90 and the advice was only geared toward a life expectancy of 80 years, the concept would not work out, he adds.

## Pension fund and AHV pensions for basic needs

Müller-Brunner has a rule of thumb for this: the pension fund pension as a secured income after retirement should, together with the AHV (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance), cover at least the basic needs of living expenses in old age.

Spring cites the often-quoted rule that many people needed about 80% of their pre-retirement income in retirement. However, one must look at this individually, he says. At least 60% of pre-retirement income should be achieved through AHV and pension fund pensions. For many high earners, however, this was not the case, and the question arose whether they had saved enough to maintain their standard of living in old age. «Anyone who takes stock only at 55 is potentially too late,» Spring says.

## Risk of a stock market crash

When withdrawing capital and subsequently investing the money, the risk of a stock market crash must also be considered. While a stock market decline will eventually recover, many retirees do not have the time for this because they need the money to live. Therefore, anyone who manages the money themselves or has it managed takes on an investment risk.

In addition, it becomes difficult for many to manage their money themselves in their old age. Here, a trusted person is needed to take over — and thus there is potential for abuse.

An advantage of the pension is also often overlooked: survivors are protected. Especially if there was a significant age difference between two partners, this could be extremely relevant, Spring says. The protection benefit was then all the more important, he adds.

Many people had in mind that capital withdrawal was always more tax-efficient than a pension, Müller-Brunner says. «But that's not true when calculated precisely; in many places, it's exactly the opposite.» This was partly because many insured persons did not account for or underestimated wealth tax, he adds.

## Capital withdrawal sensible when living expenses covered

Meanwhile, there are certainly examples where capital withdrawal makes sense. This applies, for example, in cases where enough money is available and living expenses in retirement are covered. Then one can withdraw the remaining pension fund assets as capital without hesitation.

There were also other cases where capital withdrawal was the better option than a pension, Spring says. This applied, for example, if one could assume they did not have a high life expectancy, or if one was already seriously ill when retiring. Health condition played a very important role in the decision, he adds.
