# Crans-Montana burn victims gradually return to everyday life

*Of the 42 people with severe burn injuries who were treated in Suva's two rehabilitation clinics after the Crans-Montana disaster, 10 are still receiving inpatient treatment.*

For the burn victims of Crans-Montana, rehabilitation at Suva's two clinics in Sion (VS) and Bellikon (AG) plays an important role in their return to everyday life. Since the fire disaster on New Year's Day, the Suva clinics have treated 42 patients as inpatients. Currently, ten of them are still receiving inpatient care, and two more will be admitted soon. The Crans-Montana burn victims are, on average, 20 years old, and an average of 51% of their body surface was burned.

## Integrated care

The care for the injured was provided from the outset through close national and international cooperation. University hospitals in Zurich and Lausanne, specializing in burn injuries, played a crucial role in acute care. Some patients were admitted to hospitals in Germany, Belgium, France, and Italy. However, rehabilitation was considered from the very beginning.

Integrated care, focused on the entire treatment pathway, was crucial, Gianni Roberto Rossi, CEO of the two Suva clinics, told the media in Sion on Wednesday. «Rehabilitation must be included early so that the transitions from acute medicine to inpatient rehabilitation and outpatient aftercare are as seamless as possible.»

Rehabilitation measures are individually tailored to the health status, healing process, and needs of the injured. The goal of rehabilitation is to maintain or regain functions, promote independence, and reduce the impact of injuries on daily life as much as possible.

## Dealing with 'strange' looks

Depending on the situation, pain management, wound care, scar treatment, and mobility are initially prioritized. The path back to an everyday life that is as independent as possible can take months or even years.

Initially, everyday activities such as washing, eating, dressing, or mobility can pose a major challenge. «The rehabilitation of severely burned patients is not a sprint, but a marathon,» explained Chief Physician Maria Iakova of the Western Switzerland Rehabilitation Clinic in Sion.

Severely burned individuals can, however, remain impaired for life. The Suva clinics also have teams of psychiatrists and psychologists who support traumatized patients. For example, they help those affected cope when people look «strange» because the injured may be disfigured by scars. People who have undergone amputations also needed such support, Rossi said.

## Compression garments, masks, and mouth retractors

Interprofessional collaboration among nursing, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and technical orthopedics is considered key to rehabilitation at the Suva clinics. Depending on the medical indication, individually adapted aids such as compression garments, gloves, or face masks with silicone inlays are also used; silicone keeps scars moist.

The compression suits on burned skin were not comfortable, said Patrick Meier, a burn specialist at the Bellikon Rehabilitation Clinic. For this reason, the compression was gradually increased. It was also important that patients could put on the compression suits themselves over time. Sometimes more zippers were needed for this.

New solutions must always be found, Meier said. In Crans-Montana, there were many facial injuries. His team therefore developed a mouth retractor, for example. The highest priority, however, is to gain the trust of the patients and, above all, to listen to them.
