# Neutrality is essential for Swiss peace policy, both domestically and internationally

*The Swiss government in Bern is increasingly pushing for closer ties with the EU and NATO. The neutrality initiative aims to set guidelines for the Federal Council and Parliament.*

Neutrality in Switzerland continues to be supported by a large majority of the population. However, it is only rudimentarily enshrined in the Federal Constitution. Until a few decades ago, it was still implicitly clear to the population, as well as to the Federal Council and Parliament, what neutrality entailed.

Neutrality is essential for Swiss peace policy, both domestically and internationally. This was also conveyed in education and schooling. However, this historical awareness is increasingly eroding, partly due to the removal of history as a subject in primary schools and the neglect of civics.

At the same time, the federal government in Bern is increasingly pushing for further steps toward rapprochement with the EU and NATO. These were the reasons why a cross-party working group launched the Neutrality Initiative about four and a half years ago. The group was convinced that a more precise definition of neutrality was now needed in the Federal Constitution.

## Sovereignty and internal cohesion

The initiative text recalls the legal foundations of neutrality and links them with a sensible neutrality policy. This provides the Federal Council and Parliament with the necessary guardrails to conduct domestic and foreign policy for the benefit of the country's sovereignty and peaceful development.

Immediately after the initiative was launched, its opponents took a stand. They insist that Switzerland would be forced into a «straitjacket» by enshrining a more precise definition of neutrality and that neutrality had always been interpreted «flexibly.» A closer look at the historical derivation of Swiss neutrality makes it clear that the opponents' arguments ignore the entire ethical dimension of neutrality.

The network of alliances of the Old Swiss Confederacy developed from the 13th to the 17th century as part of the Holy Roman Empire. All parties involved soon realized that the cooperatively organized confederation of sovereign members could only survive if disputes could be resolved through its own mediation.

After the Thirty Years' War, the Peace of Westphalia in 1648 set important foreign policy accents, including the principles of amnesty for those involved in the war and religious tolerance. These were also central elements of an early Enlightenment, which was later continued with the development of international law (including Emer de Vattel as a representative of the Western Swiss natural law school) and with the human rights discourse.

Such elements were also important for the neutral Confederacy. It was confirmed as a sovereign country in the context of the Peace of Westphalia, in line with its own state formation. Switzerland, which had gained its territory independently and developed it cooperatively, achieved its neutral position outside the territory of the parties to the conflict in the Thirty Years' War.

In 1674, the Federal Diet officially referred to Switzerland for the first time as a «neutral country.» However, defensive alliances were still permissible, and the Confederacy was entangled in numerous alliances. Nevertheless, the declared neutrality increasingly brought the desired unity to multicultural and multi-religious Switzerland. The Confederacy then also succeeded in staying out of the European wars of religion, conquest, and succession in the early modern period.

It was also important that in 1647, with the Defensionale of Wil, the first federal military ordinance, armed neutrality had increasingly taken shape. Mercenary services also faced increasing criticism and were finally banned. Consequently, thanks to its neutrality, the Confederacy repeatedly initiated arbitration proceedings within the framework of foreign policy and made a name for itself as a mediator. The Good Offices took shape.

## The foundation for humanitarian Switzerland

The Congress of Vienna in 1815 and the founding of the federal state in 1848 were other important milestones that Switzerland set on its own initiative. With its independence, developed over centuries, it strengthened its neutrality nationally and internationally.

On this foundation, neutral Switzerland developed its comprehensive humanitarian commitment alongside the Good Offices. With the founding of the Red Cross in 1863 and especially thanks to the ICRC, Switzerland succeeded in applying its wealth of experience in dealing with neutrality externally in the context of international law. With international humanitarian law and the Geneva Conventions, Switzerland, as a depositary state, was increasingly able to act as a peacemaker.

Finally, the Hague Peace Conventions of 1907 decisively supplemented the neutral foundation of the Confederacy. Even though it is over a hundred years old, this peace order still applies today as customary international law. It sets out, among other things, the rights and duties of a neutral country. The Neutrality Initiative aims to recall this once again. In this sense, the initiative will not herald the end of a development, but a new beginning.
