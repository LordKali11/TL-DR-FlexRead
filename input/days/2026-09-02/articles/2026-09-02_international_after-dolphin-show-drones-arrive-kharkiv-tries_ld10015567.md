## Maritime holiday feeling

The eastern Ukrainian metropolis of Kharkiv is located just 30 kilometers from the front. Russia attacks almost daily with drones and glide bombs. Numerous buildings are damaged by war, others are protected with sandbags. Soldiers are a common sight in the streets. The war is omnipresent.

The Nemo Hotel with its integrated dolphinarium offers the maximum contrast to this. In a prime location, in the middle of the city park, there is a maritime holiday atmosphere here – even though the sea is 350 kilometers away.

The building is modeled after a ship. Several rooms have portholes as windows, and an annex houses an aquarium with exotic fish. On the rooftop terrace, there is a pool with a bar and a magnificent view over the city, surrounded by gentle hills. Visitors drink cocktails or smoke hookah.

The trademark of the establishment, however, is the dolphin program. There is an offer for individual clients like Lyudmila and Irina, and a show for a larger audience three times a day. For an hour, the animals perform somersaults to dramatic music, shoot synchronously out of the water, or hurl large balls with their tail fins into the audience, who cheer and throw them back into the pool.

One dolphin even paints a picture with a brush in the national colors of blue and yellow. To conclude, an attendant rides along the edge of the pool, standing on the snouts of two animals. In his hand, he holds a large Ukrainian flag.

«The program now has a patriotic touch,» says Vladislav Litnevsky, who has been working with the animals for eleven years and plays the main role in the flag routine. The war, however, has not changed much else, he says. Operations were only suspended in the first months after the major Russian attack, he adds.

«But already in the summer of 2022, we reopened. The demand was always there,» Litnevsky says. «People in this city want to live, not just survive.» He has no understanding for animal welfare criticism of the show. The animals, he argues, live significantly longer with them than in the open sea.

The spectacular evacuation of the two beluga whales also shows the importance of animal welfare, Litnevsky points out. The animals were formerly also part of the Kharkiv dolphinarium's collection but could no longer be optimally cared for during the war. Therefore, they were brought to Valencia, Spain, in June 2024. In addition to his work as a dolphin trainer, Litnevsky has been studying veterinary medicine for several semesters.

## Local pride in the livable city

Kharkiv’s cityscape is characterized by a mixture of Stalinist «wedding cake» style, much late-Soviet concrete, and unrestrained construction activity thereafter. This may not be beautiful in the classical sense. However, the metropolis of eastern Ukraine was always considered livable: thanks to its large parks, wide squares, and vibrant gastronomic and cultural scene.

The residents of Kharkiv defend this sense of life, which is quite identity-forming, even in wartime – be it in a dolphinarium. It is not for nothing that the city has carried the epithet «the Unbreakable» since the thwarted Russian attempt to conquer it in 2022.

Irina and Lyudmila, the two women from the dolphin pool, actually live in an apartment in the city center. Nevertheless, they have checked into the Nemo Hotel for one night. «From our room, you can see our apartment,» Lyudmila says, laughing. «We wanted to treat ourselves to something special at the end,» she adds, explaining. The two are traveling to France the next day, where Lyudmila’s brother lives with his family. They spend some time with him every year.

With room prices starting at 80 Swiss francs, the Nemo is not an expensive hotel by international standards. Lyudmila’s handbag from a French luxury brand probably cost many times that. Nevertheless, not everyone can afford this extravagant form of everyday escape. The average income in Ukraine is just over 500 francs.

## Free admission to the zoo

«All people need space to breathe. We offer that space,» says Karina Detjuk. The tall woman is the deputy director of the Kharkiv city zoo, which directly adjoins the Nemo Hotel and the large city park.

The zoo is the oldest and at the same time most modern animal park in Ukraine. The facility was extensively renovated a few years ago. The work was only completed in August 2021, just a few months before the major Russian attack. Like public transport in Kharkiv, admission to the animal park is free for all visitors.

On a tour, Detjuk proudly shows off the new enclosures. «The bears have so much space that you sometimes can’t even see them.» On beautiful summer days, up to 17,000 people visit the animal park, Detjuk says. «If all these people can spend a few carefree hours with us, that is worth a lot.»

## «Our children only know this life»

Carefreeness, however, is a relative concept in Ukraine, especially in close proximity to the front. During the tour, an air raid alarm suddenly sounds. Russian drones are approaching. For several weeks, Russia has been deliberately destroying gas stations in Ukraine, with the Kharkiv region being particularly hard hit. It is revenge for the fuel shortage caused by Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries.

Over the loudspeakers, zoo visitors are urged to go to the nearest air raid shelter, a subway station not far from the entrance. Only a few people comply. In front of the crocodile enclosure, a young family stands watching the animal. The children are clearly more impressed by the reptile’s large teeth than by the siren. «The attacks are part of our everyday life,» the mother says. «If we ran to the bunker every time, we would no longer have a life.»

Deputy Director Detjuk observes the scene from a distance. Her son is five years old, she says. He recently asked her where she had hidden from the bombs as a child, she adds. «Our children only know this life.»

After some time, the all-clear signal sounds. This time, the drones have flown past Kharkiv; this time, there are no explosions. Most animals have gotten used to the sounds of war, the sirens, and the bombs, Detjuk says. However, the youngest chimpanzee in the monkey enclosure lost its fur at the beginning of the war and has been bald ever since, she adds.

A man introduces himself as Slavik, lying on a deck chair. The cocktail on the small table next to him is clearly not his first. When Slavik reaches for his phone, he knocks over the glass. It is the last day of his leave from the front, he says apologetically. «Tomorrow I have to report back. And I have problems with my knees.» When the waitress comes to wipe away the shards, he picks up his bag and walks with unsteady steps to the cloakroom.

A little later, Irina and Lyudmila come onto the terrace. They treated themselves to a massage after their dolphin swim and then had dinner at the best Georgian restaurant in the city. Now they want to enjoy a drink with a view of the city.

Again, an air raid alarm sounds. This time, an explosion is heard a few minutes later. Black smoke mixes into the red glow of the sky. Somewhere outside the city, there was an impact – perhaps another gas station.

Mother and daughter look at the plume of smoke on the horizon and then towards their apartment. Then they toast each other: «Our house is still standing!»
