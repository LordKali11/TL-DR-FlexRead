# Kompass Initiative: Who really waited too long?

*The initiators of the Compass initiative are accused of submitting their proposal too late. The retroactive application to the vote on the EU treaties, enshrined in the initiative text, is therefore legally problematic. However, what actually arrived very late was the message from the Federal Council.*

In the debate surrounding the Kompass Initiative, critics have raised an accusation that cannot go unchallenged: that Kompass/Europa submitted the initiative too late, ensuring the popular vote could not occur before the ratification of the EU treaty package. This, however, is factually incorrect. It also distracts from an uncomfortable question: What has the Federal Council itself done in the past 12 months?

## Controversial cantonal majority

The Kompass Initiative's central goal is for a double majority of the people and cantons to apply to the vote on new agreements with the EU, its initiators state. The Federal Council, however, wants the vote to proceed with a simple popular majority. In this case, a retroactive clause in the text of the Kompass Initiative demands that the ballot on the EU treaties be annulled if accepted.

These are the reasons why proponents of the EU treaties and the Federal Council complain that the Kompass Initiative was simply submitted too late.

The decision to launch the Kompass Initiative was made in winter 2023, after the Common Understanding between the EU and Switzerland became known. Official negotiations with the EU began on March 18, 2024. On Oct. 1, 2024, signature collection for the initiative started. The substantive conclusion of negotiations only followed on Dec. 20, 2024, two and a half months after collection began. Even then, only fact sheets were published, not the full treaty texts.

The unsatisfactory negotiation result — 1,889 pages of treaty text — was only published on June 13, 2025. Three months later, 140,347 signatures had been collected, and the initiative was submitted. Eleven of the legally available 18 months were needed for collection and submission — six months before the signing in Brussels and three months before the consultation report. Kompass/Europa acted as soon as it was factually justifiable and at a pace exceptional for a popular initiative.

What actually took a long time, however, was the Federal Council's dispatch to parliament. It was adopted on Aug. 12, 2026 — 17 days before the expiry of the statutory annual deadline. The Federal Council allowed nine months to pass. Had the dispatch been submitted earlier, parliament would have had the opportunity to deliberate on the initiative quickly.

## Not politically neutral

This exhaustion of the statutory maximum period is not politically neutral. It significantly reduces parliamentary leeway and makes a vote on the constitutional fundamental question of the required double majority more difficult before the vote on the EU package. This represents obvious political calculation.

And this is precisely where it becomes grotesque: Federal Councillor Jans warns of the legal uncertainty that would arise if the Kompass Initiative were accepted after a vote on the EU package. But it is precisely he who, by delaying the dispatch, prevents the people from creating clarity in time. He significantly contributed to the uncertainty he warns against through his own delaying tactics.

The Kompass Initiative was not too late. It is too late to answer the fundamental question of the cantonal majority in institutional integration only after its first major application.

_**Alfred Gantner** is a co-founder of Partners Group and a co-initiator of the Kompass Initiative._
