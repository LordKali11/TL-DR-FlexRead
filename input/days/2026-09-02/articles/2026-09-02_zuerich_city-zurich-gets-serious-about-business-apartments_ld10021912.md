# The City of Zurich gets serious about business apartments and accepts complaints

*From Sept. 1, providers of short-term rental apartments must submit building applications. To process these, the city is creating five full-time positions.*

A few months ago, the Federal Supreme Court ruled that the City of Zurich can restrict business apartments. Specifically, apartments rented for less than one year at a time will no longer be counted toward the minimum proportion of residential units required in residential zones. This ended a years-long legal dispute between the city and four companies that operate short-term rental businesses.

Now, the City of Zurich is getting serious. From Sept. 1, providers of short-term rental apartments will have to submit a building application to request a change of use from residential to business apartment. As the Department of Building Construction wrote in a statement on Tuesday, this also applies even if no structural changes are made.

However, the city will not only act when such a building application has been received, but also when it receives information that apartments are being rented out commercially for short periods. The city could not answer how many such complaints had already been received when asked by the NZZ. The recording of complaints and building applications is only just beginning.

City officials further stated that due to the great public interest in the business apartment issue, it was to be assumed that a large number of complaints would be received, especially at the beginning. They added that this would be the case, for example, if entire buildings were rented out for short periods by commercial providers.

To enable the Office for Building Permits to manage this work, which is in addition to its daily operations, the City Council approved an urgent supplementary credit. This enables five new full-time positions for three years.

How long an individual procedure would take was difficult to predict, the Department of Building Construction wrote. «Extensive case-by-case reviews with comprehensive balancing of interests will certainly have to be carried out,» it added.

City officials said that if an application was rejected, the city would carry out checks to ensure that the apartment, once rented short-term, would be offered regularly. The exact procedure is currently being developed, the Department of Building Construction wrote.

They also noted that one possibility for this would be, for example, that the owner would provide proof of booking cancellations or the conclusion of long-term rental agreements. Should owners not comply with the instructions of the Office for Building Permits, they added, they would be reported to the district governor, and a fine would be imposed.

Individuals who occasionally rent out their own apartment, for example while they are on vacation themselves, are not affected by the regulation.

However, the issue of short-term rentals is not off the table in the City of Zurich. An initiative launched by the SP together with the Greens and the AL to restrict platforms like Airbnb is still pending. The City of Lucerne serves as a model for this. There, voters decided three years ago that apartments may only be rented out for short periods for a maximum of 90 days per year.

The initiative is expected to come to a vote next year. The City Council is working on a counter-proposal.
