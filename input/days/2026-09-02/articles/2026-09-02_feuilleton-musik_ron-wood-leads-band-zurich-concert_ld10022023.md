# Ron Wood leads his band at Zurich concert

*The Stones guitarist gives a soulful but limited concert at the Volkshaus.*

«No, Ronnie,» Keith Richards once told Ron Wood. «We don’t want to be happy all the time.» Rolling Stones guitarist Ron Wood is blessed with an untamable zest for life, a warm-hearted man from a Roma background. In his 79 years, he has survived two serious cancer diagnoses, undergone several alcohol detoxes, and battled addictions to cocaine and nicotine.

Wood almost lost his life, but never his humor. With it, he managed to win over even difficult artists like Bob Dylan. «Bob Dylan gave me that song,» Ron Wood explained on Tuesday evening during his performance at Zurich’s Volkshaus, before launching into the magnificent song «Seven Days,» which is about a man who can barely wait for his beloved at the train station.

## Broad English Channel laugh

It is probably impossible not to like Ronnie Wood. His arms flutter like butterflies, his voice rasps in a deep Cockney, his laugh is almost as wide as the English Channel. His charm, his subtle guitar playing, and his passion for music carried the Zurich audience through a two-hour concert that primarily demonstrates one thing: that Ron Wood, like all Rolling Stones musicians, has excellent musical taste.

The audience heard not only some songs from his solo career, but also «Chain of Fools» by Aretha Franklin, «River Deep, Mountain High» by Ike and Tina Turner, and the blues classics «Spoonful» and «Baby What You Want Me to Do.» However, a sympathetic demeanor and an attractive repertoire are not enough for a convincing concert. One also needs to be able to play the material.

And in that regard, problems arose. Ronnie Wood brought a great band – with the exception of the drummer, who operated his kit with the finesse of a cement mixer. One thought wistfully of Stones drummer Charlie Watts, the cool-swinging jazz man with the Buster Keaton face, who died of cancer five years ago. And if rock’n’roll doesn’t swing, as happened on Tuesday, it sounds dead.

Compounding the problem was the fact that while Wood, as a guitarist, knows how to create atmosphere and tension, as a singer he is often hopelessly overwhelmed. Because he is at least aware of this, he brought Irish singer Imelda May on tour with him, who compensates for her bandleader’s gaping deficiencies with fervent gospel singing.

As a guitarist for the Faces and the Rolling Stones, Ron Wood was never more than a sideman, lacking what Stones singer Mick Jagger possesses in abundance to be a bandleader: vocal strength, authority, and the ability to draw all eyes to himself. The fact that the concert, with ticket prices between 177 and 267 Swiss francs, was not sold out for a long time demonstrates in its own way that a nice guy like Ron Wood is not enough to command the center of the stage.

## Happy accompaniment

When Wood last performed in Zurich nine years ago, he stood with the Rolling Stones on the stage of the sold-out Letzigrund Stadium. The band then delivered a concert of burning intensity. One remembers that now, as Ron Wood provided the highlight of the evening with «Can’t You Hear Me Knocking» from the now-classic Stones album «Sticky Fingers» (1971).

On the original recording, Mick Taylor still played lead guitar. He did not have Ronnie Wood’s humor, but he was far superior to him on the instrument. Nevertheless, one now hopes that the sympathetic Ron Wood will soon return to where he fits best: with the Rolling Stones, the best rock band, as a backing musician, but happy all the time.
