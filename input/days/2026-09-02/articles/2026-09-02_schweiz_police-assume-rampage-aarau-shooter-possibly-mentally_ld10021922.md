# Police assume rampage: Aarau shooter possibly mentally ill, stumbled upon rave by «chance»

*Two and a half days after the suspect's arrest, police provided an update on the investigation. The motive remains unclear.*

The suspect, who reportedly killed an Italian woman after a techno festival in Aarau, is a 43-year-old «Swiss without a migration background» who lived in the canton of Jura and suffered from psychological problems. The Aargau Cantonal Police announced this at a press conference on Wednesday afternoon.

According to cantonal police commander Michael Leupold, the accused turned himself in on Monday night: he drove his car to the police station in the Jura capital of Delémont, confessed to the crime, but then refused to make a statement. Public prosecutor Christoph Rüedi said: «I have been in poor health for some time.»

## Witness statement on getaway vehicle

In the man's trunk, police found three weapons: two pistols and an AK-74 assault rifle, a Kalashnikov. Police explained the sequence of events as follows: On Sunday night, the man parked his car in a parking lot on Schachenstrasse. He then took the weapons from the trunk, moved toward the techno festival on the horse racing track, before firing more than 40 shots indiscriminately at the well over 100 people who were still on the premises after the festival on Sunday night.

The suspect legally acquired the weapons about 20 years ago, and they were registered, Leupold said. «Today, the licensing requirements would be significantly stricter,» he added. After the act, the man reportedly ran back to the parking lot, where he shot at a moving car, among other things. He then fled in his vehicle.

Police indicated they would have caught the perpetrator if he had not turned himself in. Thanks to the «statements of one person,» they were able to determine the type of getaway car – «with license plates from the canton of Jura,» police said. Police soon found out the suspect's name.

But why does a man drive to Aarau in the middle of the night to shoot at the remaining guests of a rave? The suspect exercised his «right to refuse to testify» during the interrogation, Leupold said on Wednesday. So, for now, police cannot answer the question of «Why?» They assumed it was a coincidence that he stumbled upon the event, adding that there are currently «no indications of ideological motives,» meaning «neither right-wing nor left-wing extremist nor Islamist motives.» This is what is unsettling about this crime: that it still has no motive. Police classify it as a rampage.

## Reserved communication

Around 60 journalists sat in the police headquarters in Schafisheim in the canton of Aargau on Wednesday to ask police further questions. The press conference had been eagerly awaited, as little was known until then. On Sunday evening, police had stated that they had few clues. They did not know where the perpetrators were, nor what motive was behind the crime.

A few hours later, on Monday night, the suspect was apprehended. In the corresponding communiqué, the Aargau Cantonal Police wrote about a 43-year-old Swiss man who was «under urgent suspicion of the crime.» They assumed he was a lone perpetrator. Police did not release any more information; they did not name a motive, nor did they describe the manhunt or the escape. Instead, they referred to Wednesday's press conference.

Police explained on Wednesday that they first wanted to wait until «there was no longer any danger.» And when Leupold read out what work the Cantonal Police had to do after the crime, it sounded a bit like a defense speech before the international press: they had to evaluate the DNA samples, which «took a certain amount of time due to the technical process,» had «checked all cameras in public spaces,» had «followed up on well over 100 tips» from the public, and had to stop and check cars.

## Two people in hospital

The sparse information over the past two days, however, left room for speculation, especially in Italy, where the victim came from. Several media professionals traveled to Aarau, made live broadcasts, and investigated on their own. The daily newspapers «Corriere della Sera» and «La Repubblica» indicated that the suspect moved in military circles. Leupold debunked this theory, saying that although the accused had completed recruit school and had been a troop cook, he had left the army in 2013. He had handed in his service weapon.

Further information about the man is currently unavailable. The authorities did not provide any details about his family background, whether the man had a job, or how he lived. What is certain is that he was not known to the police and had, «apart from minor traffic offenses,» no previous convictions, as police commander Leupold said.

Police have ended the «special situation» and finally given the all-clear, stating that there are «no indications of accomplices or helpers.» The injured are not in life-threatening condition, although police corrected the number from five to six, saying one woman was injured by a «ricochet or stray bullet» but only reported it on Monday. Two people are still in the hospital.
