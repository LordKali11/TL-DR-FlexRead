# German-Russian relations at an all-time low: Putin speaks of a «grave mistake» after German sanctions

*Kremlin denies responsibility for Leipzig drone incident; German accusations called desperate; Moscow's countermeasures*

The reaction from Russia was not long in coming. «Moscow will respond to all anti-Russian decisions,» said Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova shortly after the German government announced punitive measures against Russia on Tuesday evening. Berlin considers it proven that the Kremlin was behind the failed drone attack at Leipzig Airport.

This response would come very quickly, Deputy Foreign Minister Alexander Grushko later reiterated on the sidelines of an economic forum in distant Vladivostok. Earlier, at a press conference following the Shanghai Cooperation Organization meeting, President Vladimir Putin had already spoken of «a grave mistake» that Germany had made with the sanctions.

## Fewer representations

What the countermeasures will specifically entail is not yet known. However, since the Kremlin speaks of a «mirror-image» reaction, the closure of the German Consulate General in St. Petersburg and the Goethe-Institut in Moscow is to be expected. The German government, among other things, announced that it would close the Russian Consulate General in Bonn and the Russian House in Berlin.

A debate has been ongoing in Germany for weeks about the Russian House, which officially functions as a cultural institution but is also said to serve the Kremlin for intelligence purposes. The Kremlin had already threatened before the latest escalation that a closure of the institution would affect the Goethe-Institut.

The German Consulate General in St. Petersburg is Germany's last diplomatic representation in Russia, besides the embassy in Moscow. The consulates in Kaliningrad, Yekaterinburg, and Novosibirsk had to cease operations at the end of 2023. At the same time, the Russian consulates in Leipzig, Munich, Frankfurt, and Hamburg were closed.

The consulate in St. Petersburg opened in 1972 and, alongside the embassy in Moscow, is the only West German representation in Russia that existed during Soviet times. With its closure, bilateral diplomatic relations would definitively fall back to a level last seen before Federal Chancellor Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik.

A new low point would also be reached in cultural relations. The Goethe-Institut is tasked with promoting the German language and culture. The states of the former Soviet Union form a focus of the cultural institution's international activities.

However, since the major Russian attack on Ukraine, the institute's activities in Russia have been severely restricted. Now it could be closed entirely. The German Historical Institute in Moscow, another institution designed to promote mutual cultural understanding, had already had to cease operations in June 2024 as an «undesirable organization.»

## Sign of weakness

The Kremlin attributes all responsibility for this development to Germany. Berlin had once again brought about an escalation of bilateral relations under a completely fabricated pretext, said Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Zakharova. Putin portrayed the latest punitive measures as an act of desperation by a government that had its back against the wall. There was no evidence of Russian involvement in the drone incident in Leipzig, he added.

«The Chancellor's situation is difficult. He does not enjoy the trust of the population because he does not protect national interests. Companies have to close, the standard of living is falling,» the Russian President said at the press conference in Bishkek. With his actions against Russia, Merz wanted to distract from this, also because of the upcoming state elections. Especially in Saxony-Anhalt, the governing parties must expect a bitter defeat.

## Denial and disclaiming

The Russian President added that Russia had already been groundlessly accused in the Nord Stream attack. Ukrainian authorship is now considered the most plausible explanation for the bombing of the gas pipelines in the Baltic Sea.

Of course, the two incidents cannot be compared. While there were indeed speculations about Russian perpetrators after the Nord Stream attack, the German government never made official accusations against Moscow due to lack of evidence. No sanctions were imposed in that matter either.

The situation is now much clearer. The failed attack in Leipzig resembles other sabotage actions attributed to Russia in NATO territory, not least because it was directed against Western support for Ukraine. A drone found at Leipzig Airport was near Ukrainian cargo planes transporting military equipment.

That Moscow disclaims all responsibility for the attack and portrays it as a figment of an anti-Russian West's imagination should surprise no one. The Kremlin has much practice in denying its misdeeds: The nerve agent attacks on Sergei Skripal and Alexei Navalny are part of this, as is the downing of a Malaysia Airlines passenger plane over Donbas, the bombing of a theater in the Ukrainian city of Mariupol where hundreds of children had sought refuge, and countless other crimes committed by the Russian armed forces during this war.

## Rejection of negotiations

At the press conference on Tuesday evening, Putin announced renewed massive attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure and rejected negotiations with the «terrorist regime in Kyiv.» At the same time, incidents in NATO territory, such as the failed drone attack in Leipzig, have been increasing for some time. Nevertheless, in the Kremlin's portrayal, it is the West and Ukraine that seek escalation.
