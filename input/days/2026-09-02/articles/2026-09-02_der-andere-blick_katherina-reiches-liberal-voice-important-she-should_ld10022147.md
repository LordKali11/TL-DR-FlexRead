As a counterpoint to the statism all too prevalent, even within the governing coalition, a voice of economic reason is urgently needed. It has been heard clearly and distinctly from Minister of Economic Affairs Katherina Reiche time and again. Business representatives therefore trust that their minister takes their concerns seriously. Especially since this was rather less the case with Reiche’s predecessor, Robert Habeck.

The minister thus plays an enormously important role. However, she jeopardizes it by repeatedly overstepping. Politics largely consists of compromises, and once these are negotiated, they should endure. Reiche can obviously only accept this to a limited extent. This may be necessary in some cases. But anyone who consistently behaves this way eventually risks being ousted from politics.

## Right in substance, wrong in form

The latest example of this is a letter that a State Secretary from her department sent to a colleague in the Ministry of Finance, which is led by SPD leader Lars Klingbeil. The State Secretary criticized above all that the tax reform passed this Wednesday brought no relief. The main criticism, he said, was that the government did not reduce «bracket creep.» Through this, the purchasing power of employees decreases even when their wages rise in lockstep with inflation.

In their coalition agreement, the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) and the Social Democratic Party (SPD) had agreed to relieve primarily small and medium incomes. There can be no talk of that now. That is why Reiche’s Ministry is right in substance.

That the government has failed to relieve middle incomes in particular is a serious oversight. That it also wants to conceal this failure is worthy of all criticism. However, when this criticism comes from within the government itself one day before the vote, it is not an expression of upright stance. It is a disruptive maneuver that creates nothing but unrest and reinforces the already existing doubts about the coalition's functionality. The form and timing of the action therefore harm the cause more than they benefit it.

## Attack at an inopportune time

Already in April, the Minister of Economic Affairs had attacked her cabinet colleague Klingbeil so vehemently that Chancellor Merz called her to order. The fact that she is once again launching an attack at an opportune time raises the question of whether she simply lacks intuition or whether she might even want to provoke her own dismissal. Is she ultimately already concerned with interpretive authority over a possible premature end to her term? After all, Reiche was also considered a candidate for departure when Merz reshuffled the cabinet in the summer.

As a successor, Merz would probably hardly install another manager from industry. After the experiences with Reiche, the choice is likely to fall on a reliable force with ample political experience, who integrates into coalition discipline as smoothly as possible. And who does not disturb the harmony, even when it would be appropriate not only in terms of content but also timing. The liberal voice would fall silent. Katherina Reiche would then be missed. She would have only herself to blame for that.
