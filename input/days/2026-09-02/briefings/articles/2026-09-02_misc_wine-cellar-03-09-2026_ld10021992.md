by Peter Keller

Wine editor

For several years now, I've been accompanying the NZZ's wine tours. Bordeaux and Tuscany are sure bets, each appealing to a large readership. River cruises offer a wonderful way to slow down. Famous wine regions are situated along waterways. Think of the Rhine or Moselle in Germany, the Danube, home to the Wachau region in Austria, or the Loire in France. This year, my trip also took me to France, to the Rhône and Saône rivers. Along the route between St. Jean de Losne and Avignon, there's the opportunity to visit four regions: the Mâconnais in southern Burgundy, the adjacent Beaujolais, and the northern and southern Rhône Valley with such renowned appellations as Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, and Châteauneuf-du-Pape. In addition to visiting fine wineries, I served selected wines from these regions each evening on board the ship. You can read here which wines were particularly well-received and memorable on the last trip.

I will be accompanying one or more NZZ reader trips again next year. The program will be published in January. I would be delighted to welcome you on board again, or for the first time.

Peter Keller

Anyone traveling by boat on the Saône and Rhône rivers will pass by four different French wine regions. I enjoyed some wine highlights in southern Burgundy, Beaujolais, and the northern and southern Rhône valleys. These are my five favorites.

Maria P. from L. writes that Spain is one of the most diverse wine-growing countries and asks: Which wines should one absolutely have in their portfolio? What tips do you recommend?

The Mont Vully wine region on Lake Murten offers many oenological experiences. You can discover exciting wines, ambitious restaurants, and a historic château that now houses a stylish boutique hotel.
