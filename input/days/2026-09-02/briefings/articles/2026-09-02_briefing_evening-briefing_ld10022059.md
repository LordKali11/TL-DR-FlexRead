**What happened:** Investigators from the Federal Criminal Police Office apparently identified two suspects in the attempted attack on Leipzig Airport. The logistics provider and instructor for the attack was a Russian-born man with a Latvian passport, NDR, WDR, and the «Süddeutsche Zeitung» reported. He had connections to the Russian intelligence service, the outlets said. The second suspect was a Belarusian, they added. He also possessed a Russian passport and had previously attracted attention for criminal offenses in the Baltics, the outlets said. To the latest developments

**How the EU is reacting:** EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said that the incident in Leipzig represented a new escalation on European soil. She added that it would further strengthen the EU's resolve to increase pressure on Russia. To the report

**How Russia is reacting: **The Kremlin categorically denies responsibility for the incident in Leipzig and portrays the German accusations as an act of desperation by a weak government. The sanctions imposed by Germany were a «serious mistake,» Vladimir Putin said. Moscow has already announced countermeasures. To the report

**Background:** On Aug. 4, security personnel at Leipzig Airport discovered a drone rigged with explosives and a detonator. More drones were found later. The Office of the Attorney General considers the incident a serious attack on Germany's transport and logistics infrastructure. The German government holds the Russian intelligence service responsible for it.

**What happened:** Following the disturbance at a substation near Cologne, investigators found six launch devices in a cornfield. It was to be assumed that they were built to guide a wire over the power lines using pyrotechnical objects, the Senior Criminal Director said. From the perspective of the investigating chief public prosecutor, the discovered devices were a «very strong indication of an intentional criminal offense.» To the report

**Situation in Brandenburg:** On Tuesday morning, unknown individuals attempted to trigger short circuits on high-voltage lines at a substation in Brandenburg. Authorities are now investigating on suspicion of forming a terrorist organization. To the report

**Background:** The incidents in Brandenburg and North Rhine-Westphalia highlight how difficult it is to secure critical infrastructure across the board. Attacks on it are likely to increase in the future. To the analysis
