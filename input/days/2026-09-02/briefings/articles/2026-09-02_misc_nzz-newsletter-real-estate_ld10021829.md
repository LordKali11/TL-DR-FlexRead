In many cities, entire neighborhoods are currently changing their appearance within a short period of time. Construction cranes stand close together. And yet, despite extensive investments, city dwellers are surprised that housing is not becoming cheaper.

Interpretations vary widely. The accusation of «profit-driven thinking» targets investors and developers. The counter-argument cites migration and an open labor market as key causes. And a third view places the problem in tenancy law, which it considers too lenient.

The internationally renowned real estate economist Christian Hilber examines which theories can be substantiated. He conducts research at the London School of Economics and teaches at CUREM at the University of Zurich. Two years ago, I attended his inaugural lecture on housing, affordability, and spatial planning. It remained analytical down to the last detail, yet still captured the essence of the experience that accompanies every home purchase and every apartment search.

Where a city offers jobs and opportunities, but the supply of housing doesn't keep pace, prices rise. This is true worldwide. We imagine Australia as vast and boundless, yet Sydney is experiencing one of the steepest price increases anywhere. The explanation lies less in the well-intentioned plans of the responsible politicians than in the regulations governing construction.

Anyone who truly grapples with this issue must also examine theories that contradict common intuition. It's quite possible that the very laws intended to protect tenants will ultimately make housing unaffordable.

Some groups are calling for lower rents while simultaneously arguing that modern societies must be able to manage without additional housing supply. This calculation doesn't add up, and it disadvantages those looking for an apartment.

Hilber's research focuses on the flaws in housing policy, which often fails to recognize the underlying issues. Despite strong population growth, per capita construction activity is declining in many countries. «Previously, we rezoned and developed greenfield land on the outskirts of communities. This was a straightforward, inexpensive, and politically relatively smooth process,» he says.

Effective September 1, **Christian Sturm** will assume leadership of the listed Swiss real estate funds within Real Estate DACH at UBS. He will thus be responsible for real estate assets of approximately CHF 38 billion. Christian Sturm has been with Real Estate Switzerland since 2005 and, prior to moving into portfolio management, worked in Accounting & Controlling. For the past ten years, he has managed the UBS (CH) Property Fund – Direct Residential, a role he will continue in his expanded position. He is an expert in accounting and controlling and holds a Master of Advanced Studies (MAS) in Real Estate from the University of Zurich (UZH).

Sturm succeeds **Urs Fäs** , who is taking early retirement after 30 years at UBS. Fäs's career dates back to the 1990s, when he began working in corporate banking at what was then the Swiss Bank Corporation. Fäs is also a well-known figure in the industry outside of UBS and has significantly shaped the development of real estate investments.

On August 29, the participating partners and guests from politics and business celebrated the completion of the **Konnex** building complex in Baden. Formerly known primarily for offices and events, Konnex now unites living, working, and gastronomy under one roof on the historic industrial site. The project combines diverse uses across an area of ​​42,000 square meters. Swiss pension funds are co-owners of Konnex. Following a comprehensive condition assessment, the building complex underwent extensive renovation and repositioning.

The building was designed and constructed by **Theo Hotz AG** between 1992 and 1996. In 2019 and 2020, initial feasibility studies were conducted under the direction of **Brandenberger+Ruosch AG (B+R)** , and a strategy for repositioning the property was formulated in collaboration with the co-owners.

With flexible office spaces, 119 furnished apartments, and a green courtyard, an urban micro-system has been created just minutes from Baden train station. According to the press release, **Konnex** sees itself as a location that «promotes encounters and gives people a reason» to come together on-site. This is because offices and workplaces today have different requirements than simply focused work, which is just as easily possible from a home office. The architecture is designed accordingly, featuring, among other things, generous glass surfaces, light-filled courtyards, and open floor plans.

Established and successful Swiss real estate companies such as **Swiss Prime Site, Hiag, and Allreal** reported strong financial results this summer. Hiag alone, for example, recorded portfolio revaluations of over 50 million Swiss francs. **Swiss Prime Site AG** , with a portfolio exceeding 14 billion Swiss francs, also stood out as the industry leader. In addition, SPS is successfully positioning itself in asset management for third parties.

**SPS shares** were trading on the stock exchange at a premium of 50 to 60 percent, although the value of the third-party asset management business is not included in the net asset value (NAV). For this reason, the premium is comparatively higher than for pure real estate owners. Analysts estimate the value of the third-party asset management division at over one billion Swiss francs.

The business magazine «Finanz und Wirtschaft» speaks of a «unique stock market story.» But why have the share prices of Swiss real estate stocks tended to move sideways?

There are primarily two explanations for the discrepancy between strong operational results and a lack of enthusiasm for further share price increases.

The first reason lies in the valuations themselves. Stock prices had reached a level that left no room for disappointment. With the slight rebound in medium-term capital market interest rates, the historically high premiums came under pressure. **Markus Waeber** , real estate analyst at Bank Julius Baer, ​​sees this as a healthy consolidation. «For a while, real estate stocks were even valued higher than real estate funds. That was very unusual and an indicator that these stocks were rather expensive.» The fact that valuations are now returning to normal is not unexpected, says Waeber. For companies that primarily invest in commercial properties, this corresponds to the longer-term average.

The second explanation is the shrinking yield advantage. **Tobias Kistler** , an analyst at St. Galler Kantonalbank, also doesn't see the cause in the company's operations. «Generally speaking, these companies remain richly valued. While the dividend yield of around 3 percent is attractive, it has been higher in the past.» Moreover, there are also very attractive dividend stocks in other sectors, such as Swiss insurance companies. Since a scenario with negative interest rates is currently considered very unlikely, the previously high premiums can no longer be justified. The correction is therefore less a warning signal than a return to normality.
