**What happened:** The Aargau Cantonal Police provided an update on the investigation two and a half days after the arrest of the suspect in Aarau. The police spoke of a rampage that had cost one person their life and injured six others. Two of them were still hospitalized. The police received 80 emergency calls, and in the first twelve hours of the operation, they questioned over 130 people. Read the live updates

**What is known about the suspect:** The 43-year-old Swiss national turned himself in to the police in Delémont in the canton of Jura. According to prosecutor Christoph Rüedi, he had been in poor health for some time and suffered from psychological problems. His motive remained unclear and was still the subject of investigations, he added. Read the report

**How the act unfolded:** On Sunday morning, the suspect drove to the parking lot on Schachenstrasse, from there he continued towards the horse racing track. Then, at the rave, he fired over 40 shots indiscriminately into the crowd with an assault rifle and fled in a vehicle towards the city of Aarau.

**What happened:** In Germany, investigators from the Federal Criminal Police Office have apparently identified two suspects in the attempted attack on Leipzig Airport. NDR, WDR and «Süddeutsche Zeitung» reported that a Russian-born man with a Latvian passport had been the logistics coordinator and instructor for the attack. He had connections to the Russian secret service, they said. The second suspect was a Belarusian, the outlets added. He also possessed a Russian passport and had already attracted attention for criminal offenses in the Baltics, according to the reports. Read the latest developments

**How the EU is reacting:** EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said that the incident in Leipzig represented a new escalation on European soil. It would further strengthen the EU's resolve to increase pressure on Russia, she added. Read the report

**How Russia is reacting: **The Kremlin categorically denies responsibility for the incident in Leipzig and portrays the German accusations as an act of desperation by a weak government. The sanctions that Germany had imposed were a «grave mistake,» said President Vladimir Putin. Moscow has already announced countermeasures. Read the report

**Background:** On Aug. 4, security personnel at Leipzig Airport discovered a drone rigged with explosives and a detonator. More drones were found later. The Office of the Attorney General considers the incident a serious attack on Germany's transport and logistics infrastructure. The German government holds the Russian intelligence service responsible for it.
