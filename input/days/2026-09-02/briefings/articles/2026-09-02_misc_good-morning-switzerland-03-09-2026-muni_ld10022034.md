A year ago, the Swiss National Wrestling and Alpine Festival (ESAF) took place in Mollis (GL). Many Swiss people remember the event not primarily for the sporting competition, but for the bull Max.

Thousands of visitors wanted to see the 182-ton wooden sculpture outside the sawdust ring, a sculpture that even made headlines beyond the state's borders. But now there's controversy surrounding Max the Uristier, as the sculpture is now known.

The way the secret star of the Esaf found a new home already drew criticism in fall 2026. Glarus residents, for instance, had wanted to keep Max, and other cantons also showed great interest in the high-profile landmark. However, the bull eventually left Eastern Switzerland.

The canton of Uri won the bid among numerous competitors. It was able to play two trump cards at once: first, its cantonal coat of arms features a bull's head; second, the Gotthard canton has an influential string-puller in investor Samih Sawiris. The entrepreneur, who built a tourism resort with multiple hotels, vacation homes, and apartments in Andermatt, was not the main financier but pulled many strings behind the scenes.

Within just a few days, the association «Max the Ural Bull» was founded. The organization, comprised of well-known local politicians and businesspeople, provided 1.3 million Swiss francs for the purchase of the wooden colossus. Project managers estimate the total cost for reconstruction and operation at the new location at approximately 1.85 million Swiss francs.

Max the Aurochs is to be permanently installed in the Nätschen area above Andermatt. There, he is intended to become a tourist attraction. The 21-meter-high and 36-meter-wide sculpture will house sparkling crystals and other exhibits on four levels. In addition, the top level will be transformed into a sun terrace with mountain views.

But whether these plans will ever become reality is currently uncertain. While the transport, involving 32 trucks, went smoothly in September 2025, the project has stalled since then. The promoters' original intention to erect the wooden bull before the winter of 2026 proved far too optimistic.

The necessary building permit was not issued by the Andermatt authorities via a fast-track procedure, as the Max the Uri Bull association had envisioned. Max faces headwinds. Resistance comes primarily from various environmental organizations, which are demanding a legally correct and transparent procedure for the project.

Not all residents of Andermatt are happy about the idea of ​​placing a giant bull practically on their doorstep. The Ursern Valley (Bärental) actually features a bear in its coat of arms, while the bull is the heraldic animal of the canton of Uri (Unterland). There is a healthy rivalry between the two parts of the canton.

Besides these identity issues, the financial aspect is also causing a stir: In April of this year, the Uri cantonal government, together with the federal government, approved a total of 100,000 Swiss francs in funding for the planning concept as part of the New Regional Policy (NRP). The Social Democratic Party (SP) and the Green Party described the canton's financial contribution as «incomprehensible.» They criticize the fact that the Andermatt destination does not need to improve its marketing.

All of this has consequences. It is now clear that Bull Max will have to remain stored in individual parts in Erstfeld for another winter. On Wednesday, the Max the Uri Bull association announced that the start of construction would be postponed until spring 2027. The reasons for this are the ongoing procedures for the building permit and clarification of the neighborhood development plan.

These proceedings are being hampered by several objections. Among others, the Central Swiss Heritage Society is opposing the association's plans. Given these delays, the society writes that a subsequent construction phase before winter is no longer realistic. After all, the planned site is located at almost 2000 meters above sea level.

Given the broad and prominent resistance in Max the Uri Bull's new home, it seems doubtful whether the legal requirements can be met quickly enough for the bull to experience its second spring anytime soon.
