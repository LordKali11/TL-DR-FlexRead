**What happened:** The cabinet came together for a two-day cabinet retreat. The meeting of the coalition partners from CDU, CSU, and SPD focused primarily on strengthening the competitiveness of the German economy. Security and climate protection were also on the agenda for the deliberations at Neuhardenberg Palace in Brandenburg. To the report

**Why it matters:** Chancellor Friedrich Merz said that the cabinet meeting would prepare what the coalition still intended to accomplish during the year. With the end of the parliamentary summer break, the «autumn of reforms» was then set to begin. Various economic actors had previously put pressure on the government to ensure reforms of pensions, taxes, or the healthcare system, for example, would succeed. These actors agreed that things could not continue as they had until then.

**How economic representatives reacted:** Marie-Christine Ostermann, President of the Family Businesses, demanded that the federal government present a «coherent growth strategy.» Wolfgang Grosse Entrup, Managing Director of the German Chemical Industry Association, believed that Germany needed «the courage to 'Schröderize'.» Grosse Entrup therefore called for an «Agenda 2030,» echoing Agenda 2010 under the then-Social Democratic Federal Chancellor Gerhard Schröder.

**What happened:** The U.S. tightened economic pressure on Iran and also threatened its trading partners with sanctions. Tehran remained unimpressed and, in turn, threatened to restrict the flow of oil in the Persian Gulf. Washington faced a dilemma: Iran was difficult to isolate as long as China remained its most important buyer of Iranian oil. Beijing signaled that it would not be deterred from trading with Iran by U.S. threats. To the analysis

**Why it matters:** If it were up to the U.S. government, all countries were to cut their economic ties with Iran. Last week, American President Donald Trump had spoken of «measures of historical proportions.» However, the steps presented on Monday fell short of this announcement.

**What the expert said:** «Even if sanctions pressure is further increased, a short-term change in Tehran's strategic calculus in the war is unlikely,» said Iran expert Ali Fathollah-Nejad. He added that, in the medium to long term, targeted sanctions against the regime could pave the way for Iran to re-evaluate its strategic calculus and develop a greater willingness for understanding and concessions.
