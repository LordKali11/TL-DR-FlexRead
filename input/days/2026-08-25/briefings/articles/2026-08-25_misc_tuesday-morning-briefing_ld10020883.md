## The Most Important News This Morning

## Canada breaks off tariff negotiations with the US – would that be an option for Switzerland too?

![](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2026/08/24/e3c565a1-c535-4c8b-a9d7-337a323bddf2.webp?width=1024)
** (Carlos Osorio / Reuters)

**What happened:** Canada was the first country in a long time to leave the negotiating table with the U.S. and suspended ongoing negotiations for a tariff agreement. After Prime Minister Mark Carney announced this over the weekend, U.S. President Donald Trump announced on Monday that he would increase punitive tariffs on cars, car parts, and steel from Canada. Read the report

**Why it matters:** Canada’s approach was remarkable. Most governments had so far tried to remain in talks with the Trump administration to reach a trade agreement. Carney also surprised with his choice of words, stating that they were then «at war» because Canada had been attacked.

**The question for Switzerland:** Should Switzerland also have broken off tariff negotiations with the U.S.? At the time, no one in Switzerland was advocating for an end to the talks. However, there were certainly ideas for a tougher negotiating stance. These included selling U.S. stocks held by the National Bank, restricting gold trade with the U.S., or stopping deliveries of critical components.

## On Ukraine's Independence Day, London reaffirms support

**What happened:** Ukraine celebrated the 35th anniversary of its independence yesterday. In his speech, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy emphasized his country's will to resist: «Ukraine wants peace, but is not ready to simply surrender.» He called on partners for more support, especially for air defense. The most important pledge of the day concerned offensive weapons. Britain gave the green light for the transfer of important defense technology. Read the report

**Why it matters:** Russia sharply condemned that the Storm Shadow/Scalp cruise missile, based on British and French technology, could soon be produced in Ukraine. The guided missile had a range of 250 kilometers. «Britain is part of the war and regularly pours oil on the fire,» a Kremlin spokesman said.

**The background:** After the official celebrations, the so-called Coalition of the Willing held a hybrid meeting. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who made his first foreign trip since taking office in Kyiv, chaired the meeting. French President Emmanuel Macron, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, and many others participated via video. The U.S. was not represented. Read the live ticker

One day, when he had had enough, he wrote on his mailbox: «Solo cartas de amor» (only love letters). Indeed, people began to drop only love letters into it. The Count died three years ago. This was more than a gap in the arsenal: against an adversary like Russia, which possessed a large arsenal of ballistic missiles, Europe lacked precisely those conventional options that lay between merely intercepting an attack and a significantly higher level of escalation.
