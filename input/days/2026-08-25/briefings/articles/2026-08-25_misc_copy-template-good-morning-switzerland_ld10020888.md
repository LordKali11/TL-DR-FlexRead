Are you looking forward to winter? After the endless, hot and dry summer, you might be tempted by the idea of ​​getting your skis out of the basement in a few months and leaving the first tracks in the snow.

However, this will no longer be possible in Braunwald (GL). At least, not if the management of the Braunwald ski lifts has its way.

In mid-June, the ski lift operators unexpectedly announced that traditional skiing operations in the Glarus mountain village would be discontinued for the upcoming winter season. The operators fear bankruptcy if the costly facilities have to continue running despite the increasing lack of snow. Therefore, the ski lifts and snow cannons will not be activated again after their summer hibernation.

However, the final word on this matter has not yet been spoken. In recent weeks, widespread resistance has formed in Braunwald. Local business owners and second-home owners fear that Braunwald will quickly fade into insignificance if the ski resort is closed.

On Wednesday afternoon, the showdown will take place at the snow line. The general meeting of the Braunwald AG ski lift company will be held on the Grotzenbüel, at 1559 meters above sea level. The remarkable thing is that the sweeping changes facing the traditional winter sports resort are officially not on the agenda.

However, emotions are expected to run high. Whether the many small shareholders will express their confidence in the board of directors at the eagerly awaited general meeting is entirely uncertain. It is much more likely that they will criticize the board vehemently and put it on the defensive from the outset.

Three years ago, I experienced firsthand the emotions that the closure of a traditional winter sports area can trigger. In the summer of 2023, the Sattel-Hochstuckli destination in the canton of Schwyz faced the same decision. There, too, the operators had decided, without consulting the minority shareholders, to discontinue traditional skiing operations due to the effects of climate change.

The general meeting saw a large turnout of shareholders, who discussed the strategic realignment in a tense atmosphere. Those in charge had clearly underestimated the emotional factor. For the locals, their financial commitment is also an expression of their connection to «their» ski resort.

At the end of the event, there was general disappointment. The proposal to continue ski operations was not even put to a vote. The decision regarding the strategic direction of the ski resort lies solely within the purview of the board of directors.

It's quite possible that the small shareholders of Braunwald will face the same fate on Wednesday. In the Glarus region, too, the facts are stacked against the people who are so passionately committed to their ski resort. Braunwald would thus join the ever-growing list of winter sports destinations that must find new ways to survive.

Since Monday, Anna Giacometti has been on trial at the Maloja Regional Court in Pontresina (Grisons). The former mayor of Bregaglia is accused of failing to close a hiking trail in a timely manner in 2017. The prosecution alleges that she is partly responsible for the deaths of eight hikers who were buried in the Bondo landslide. Giacometti's lawyer, who now serves in the National Council for the FDP (Liberal Party), stated at the court hearing: «My client had to rely on expert recommendations.» He is demanding her acquittal. The court is expected to deliver its verdict against Giacometti and other defendants on September 4.
