# They devour everything and turn dry-stone walls into scree slopes: Goat plague on Capri

*A public prosecutor, a star director, and ninety goats are the main characters in a summer story set on the dream island in the Gulf of Naples.*

Capri, the large rock out there in the Gulf of Naples, looks not unlike a primeval creature from a distance: two massive humps, a furrowed surface, jagged edges. Perhaps a giant fish from prehistoric times? A sea monster?

The story that has been preoccupying hikers and residents of this mythical island for days concerns less threatening, but particularly stubborn creatures. It's about goats, «capre» in Italian. These are the animals whose presence on the island has been documented since antiquity and to which it probably owes its name. Of all things!

## The Public Prosecutor's Indictment

On Monte Solaro, one of the humps, the highest point of Capri at almost 600 meters above sea level, they cause significant damage: «Their traces are clearly visible everywhere: grazed vegetation, stripped shrubs, gorse, young plants that can no longer grow, dry-stone walls that have turned into scree slopes, loose or dislodged rocks that could roll into the valley,» the local edition of «Repubblica» recently reported.

The author of the indictment: none other than the Public Prosecutor General of Naples, Aldo Policastro. He is considered one of the country's foremost experts in the fight against organized crime.

This time, under the impression of a summer excursion to the Santa Maria a Cetrella hermitage located on Monte Solaro, he dedicated himself to other delinquents – petty criminals, considering his usual clientele.

«Nobody wants to demonize these animals,» Policastro wrote, «but if their population grows so much that the ecological balance is endangered, a solution must be found.» As Public Prosecutor General, he knows: «Ignoring the problem is never a good choice.»

In Capri, he was pushing at an open door. Questioned by journalists who became aware of the problem thanks to Policastro, the mayor of Anacapri, the responsible municipality, pointed to his long-standing efforts to get the plague under control.

He said that already in 2023, he had ordered the animals to be caught and brought to the mainland. However, the operation failed due to resistance from animal welfare activists, who collected thousands of signatures to protect the goats from exile and their alleged processing into goat roast.

## Poisoning or Lightning Strike?

At the time, news that 22 dead goats had been found under a tree caused outrage. The rumor circulated that the animals had been poisoned. The news later proved to be false. It turned out that the goats had been struck by lightning while seeking shelter under a tree during a thunderstorm.

In any case, the matter preoccupies the islanders. And some of them have identified a culprit: Mario Martone, a renowned film director, one of the greats in Italian cinema, author of blockbusters such as the historical drama «Noi credevamo» about the Italian Risorgimento. In 2011, Martone received the David di Donatello, the country's most important national film award, for it.

## «Martone's Goats»

In 2018, Martone filmed «Capri-Revolution» on the island, about a young goat herder. According to reports, he specifically brought goats of a different breed to Capri for this purpose. «The goats came for the filming in 2018 and were then left behind,» the media quote the mayor of Anacapri.

Martone dismisses this. He wants to bear no responsibility for the overpopulation and voracity of the goats: «During the inspections before filming, the goats were already present in Anacapri; if their population has grown, it is certainly not due to the animals our production brought,» the director told «Repubblica.»

His former assistant director clarifies. At the time, 25 animals from Cilento, an area in southern Campania, had been brought to Capri, Luca Federico said. «We filmed at two different locations with two herds that had to look identical,» Federico told the media these days. The animals had remained in their enclosure and were returned to the mainland after filming was completed, he added. «But the legend of ‹Martone's goats› has been spreading for years, and that amuses us a little,» he said.

So what to do? Apparently, sterilization of the males is now being considered on the island. Animal welfare activists also deem this approach suitable.

One thing is certain: Capri will have to deal with its animals for a while longer.
