# Osman Kavala case: Human Rights Court condemns Turkey

*More than seven years ago, the Strasbourg court demanded the release of Turkish cultural patron Kavala. Now the court finds further human rights violations.*

_(dpa)_ The European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) condemned Turkey over the criminal proceedings and detention of Turkish intellectual Osman Kavala. The Strasbourg judges concluded that the country wanted to silence Kavala and demanded the activist's immediate release due to serious human rights violations.

His conviction was therefore to be considered invalid, the court said. It added that, among other things, Kavala's right to a fair trial had been violated, and in this case, there were justified doubts about the independence of the Turkish courts.

## Human rights activist's life sentence

Kavala has been held in Silivri high-security prison since 2017 and was sentenced in April 2022 to life imprisonment for attempting to overthrow the government in connection with the anti-government Gezi protests in 2013. The verdict provoked sharp international criticism.

The judges found that the human rights activist had merely exercised his right to freedom of expression and peaceful assembly. These very rights had not been respected due to an arbitrary interpretation of a Turkish law and the resulting conviction.

According to the ECHR, Turkey also violates the prohibition of inhuman treatment, as Kavala has no real prospect of release or a legal review of his life sentence.

## ECHR ruling against Turkey in 2019

Already in a December 2019 ruling, the Strasbourg court had found human rights violations and at that time demanded Kavala's immediate release from pre-trial detention. Ankara did not comply. In his new complaint, the cultural patron refers to the period after the first 2019 ruling and is challenging his detention, the criminal proceedings initiated against him, and his conviction.

ECHR rulings are generally binding for Turkey as a member of the Council of Europe. After judges in Istanbul had decided that Kavala had to remain in pre-trial detention, the Council of Europe initiated infringement proceedings against Turkey. The Strasbourg organization, with 46 member states, monitors human rights compliance and is not an EU institution. Turkey has been a member of the Council of Europe since 1950.

## Turkey ordered to pay 7,500 euros in compensation

In another 2022 ruling, the ECHR ordered Turkey to pay a fine for disregarding the 2019 decision. Turkey therefore had to pay 7,500 euros to Kavala. According to his own statements, the imprisoned activist refused Turkey's payment in protest.

Kavala had founded the cultural institute Anadolu Kültür, which also collaborates with several German institutions such as the Goethe-Institut in Istanbul. In 2023, the activist was awarded the prestigious Vaclav Havel Prize of the Council of Europe, which honors commitment to human rights.
