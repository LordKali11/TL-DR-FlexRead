## The proposal

Two years ago, the Zurich City Council presented its draft for new high-rise guidelines. This includes various areas where buildings up to 40, 60, and 80 meters high may be constructed. These are primarily located in Zurich West and Zurich North.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 67ce4c17538bee4a128c65667bd4a064]

The City emphasizes that the creation of these high-rise areas is not about increasing density, but about establishing binding rules for developers and granting them more flexibility in how a plot of land can be developed. For example, if 1,000 square meters of living space can be created on a plot and distributed over 10 floors of 100 square meters each, more space remains for green areas around it than if only five floors of 200 square meters each were built.

In other cases, where a high-rise aims for higher utilization, this requires rezoning or a planning instrument, such as a development plan. If that is the case, the Parliament has the final say.

In addition to the high-rise areas, the guidelines also specify the requirements that projects must meet if they include tall buildings.

In the City Parliament, there was particular debate about the areas where the City Council wants to permit buildings up to 40 meters high. These include large parts of Altstetten, Albisrieden, and Wiedikon, as well as Affoltern, Oerlikon, and Schwamendingen.

Together with the Swiss People's Party (SVP), the left-wing parties pushed through the removal of these areas from the high-rise regulations. They also argued that a development plan must be drawn up for buildings 60 meters and taller.

The Liberals (FDP), Green Liberal Party (GLP), and The Centre (Mitte) launched a referendum against these high-rise guidelines, which had been significantly revised by the City Parliament. This is why a vote will take place.

On Sept. 27, voters can decide not only on the version of the guidelines that found a majority in the City Parliament, but also choose between this and the original version from the City Council.

## Proponents of the Parliament's proposal

Behind the City Parliament's proposal is an unusual alliance of left-wing parties and the SVP. Their reasons for arguing that the city should largely forgo areas for 40-meter towers vary accordingly.

The left-wing parties primarily argued in the City Parliament with social and ecological concerns. According to the Social Democrats (SP), for example, the high-rise guidelines revised by them are intended to prevent «profit-driven constructions.» They contend that tall buildings contribute to the displacement of poorer sections of the population from the city. The party also noted that the construction of tall buildings is expensive, and to create space for them, buildings with affordable housing must be removed.

The SVP, however, fears that more high-rises would stimulate immigration. It argues that the city cannot continue to grow unchecked. The party also believes high-rises are not a suitable means for increasing density. Instead, it maintains, they would destroy proven neighborhood structures and fuel the rise in rents.

## Proponents of the counter-proposal

The FDP, GLP, and Mitte support the counter-proposal with the high-rise guidelines originally defined by the City Council. The FDP and GLP had launched the referendum against the City Parliament's revised version.

They complain that the parliamentary majority's proposal hinders «qualitative urban development.»

For example, the GLP writes that the City Council, with its proposal, created a «reasonable, pragmatic, and future-oriented foundation.» The party adds that with areas for buildings up to 40 meters high, «significantly more urban planning flexibility is made possible in the neighborhoods.» It also states that additional living space can be created without the need for large-scale area developments. «This opens up scope for structural renewal, the preservation of existing structures, and better open spaces in the surrounding environment,» it says.

The FDP also criticizes that the City Parliament «unobjectively» complicated the high-rise guidelines and did so «contrary to the technical assessments of the SP-led Department of Building Construction.» The party adds that construction upwards would thereby be «massively hindered or even made impossible.»

The FDP argues that for a growing city, it is indispensable to be able to build upwards. It contends that sustainable urban development is not feasible without high-rises.

## The NZZ's position

The NZZ recommends a yes to the City Council's counter-proposal. The city of Zurich is growing, and available land is increasingly scarce. Therefore, various options are needed for developing plots of land – including with buildings up to 40 meters high. Waiving this option, as envisioned in the Municipal Council's proposal, restricts developers and negatively impacts construction activity.
