## Latest developments:

## VW's general situation

The German automotive industry is in crisis. Competition from China, U.S. tariffs, and a shrinking sales market are leading to job cuts at major automakers. VW is also affected by this.

CEO Oliver Blume recently described the situation as «more than critical.» On Friday, Sept. 4, he plans to again ask the supervisory board for approval of his austerity plans. The management aims to massively cut costs and improve competitiveness.

Therefore, the workforce is concerned about jobs and the sites in Emden, Hanover, Zwickau, and Neckarsulm. To provide more clarity about the situation, employee representatives convened company meetings. 9 extraordinary meetings are scheduled between Aug. 25 and Aug. 31.

## Group's need for savings

According to Blume, VW earns too little to finance its future. Previous cuts were not enough. «We are oversized. That often makes us too slow and too complicated,» the VW CEO described the problem in an interview published on the company's intranet.

Blume referred to the serious situation facing the German automotive industry. «It is not a VW crisis, but a crisis of the entire automotive sector,» he said. He cited U.S. tariffs, the collapse of the Chinese market with massive price declines, geopolitical conflicts such as in the Persian Gulf, increasingly fierce competition in Europe, and massive regulations as major challenges.

## Expected measures

Concerns among the workforce are high. «For Emden, Hanover, Zwickau, and Neckarsulm, we cannot present a competitive allocation for the 2030s today,» Blume said recently. «Additionally, we must eliminate overcapacities of more than 500,000 vehicles per year in Europe,» he added. A decision on specific plant closures has not yet been made, according to Blume.

It is also unclear how many jobs will be lost. However, massive cuts are expected. Blume pointed out that there had been an agreement since the end of 2024 to cut around 50,000 jobs in Germany at Volkswagen, Audi, Porsche, and Cariad by 2030. However, the already planned savings were not enough. «The frequently mentioned figure of around 50,000 jobs worldwide is not a fixed target,» Blume said.

## Key decision-makers for VW's future

Like every German stock corporation, VW also has a supervisory board. This can be roughly divided into four groups. The largest group consists of employee representatives. The spokeswomen are the chairwoman of the VW works council and the chairwoman of the IG Metall industrial union.

The other groups consist of the Lower Saxony state government, the Porsche and Piëch owner families, and Qatar as the third-largest shareholder. The supervisory board is completed by an individual who stands outside the four interest groups. This seat is currently vacant.

## Likelihood of plant closures

Blume is unlikely to push measures such as plant closures through the supervisory board for now. According to the «VW Law,» decisions on the «establishment and relocation of production facilities» require a two-thirds majority on the supervisory board.

The works council and IG Metall, as well as the Lower Saxony state government and the mayors of the VW sites, largely share a uniform position: drastic steps such as plant closures must be avoided in any case, they maintain.

## Upcoming developments

In a recent online sentiment survey initiated by the works council, the VW workforce criticized the board's communication. To provide more clarity about the current situation, employee representatives convened company meetings. The board is expected to answer questions from the workforce there.

9 events are planned through the end of August, including meetings in Hanover, Braunschweig, Salzgitter, Dresden, Chemnitz, and Kassel-Baunatal. The first company meeting was scheduled for Tuesday, Aug. 25, in Wolfsburg. Emden and Zwickau followed the next day, with Hanover concluding on Aug. 31.

Meanwhile, Blume invoked solidarity among the workforce. «Everyone must pull together,» he told «Bild am Sonntag.» «We have launched the largest transformation plan in the history of the Volkswagen Group,» he said. The next few years are decisive for «who remains in the race and who drives to the top,» he added.
