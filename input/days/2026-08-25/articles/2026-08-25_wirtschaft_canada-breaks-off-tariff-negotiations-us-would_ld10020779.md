# Canada breaks off tariff negotiations with the US – would that be an option for Switzerland too?

*Canada is the first country in a long time to walk away from tariff negotiations with the U.S. Now the two countries are heading for a trade war. The most important questions and answers.*

Signs point to escalation between Canada and the U.S. The latest announcement came from U.S. President Donald Trump. He would increase punitive tariffs on cars, auto parts and steel from Canada, he announced on his social media service Truth Social.

The conflict broke out over the weekend. Canada suspended ongoing negotiations for a customs agreement with the U.S., as Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced in an address to his compatriots. Thereupon, U.S. President Donald Trump put punitive tariffs of 50% on certain Canadian goods into effect. Carney, in turn, announced that he would retaliate against these with his own tariffs, «dollar for dollar,» starting Sept. 8. This provoked Trump's next response at the beginning of the week.

## Significance of the escalation

It is not yet clear whether the conflict will lead to a full-blown trade war – with a sequence of tariffs and counter-tariffs. But Canada's approach is remarkable for two reasons. It is the first country in a long time to leave the negotiating table of its own accord. Most governments have so far tried to remain in talks with the Trump administration to reach a trade agreement. In addition, Carney surprised with his choice of words, saying that they were now «at war» because Canada had been attacked.

However, if one looks at the real economic significance for both countries, the escalation is still rather small. The new American punitive tariffs, which have been in effect since the weekend, affect goods with a trade value of around $20 billion. That is only about 5% of Canadian exports to the U.S. The tariffs are levied on furniture, wine and hockey sticks, for example.

The effective tariff rate that importers at the American border must pay for Canadian goods is likely to rise from an average of 5.3% to 7.6% as a result of the measure, the Yale Budget Lab calculated.

The effective tariff burden for Canadian goods is still relatively low. Although Trump already imposed various punitive tariffs since spring 2025 – such as those on vehicles, steel and aluminum from Canada, which he now wants to increase – many of these products are still effectively exempt from tariffs if they are traded according to the rules of the USMCA free trade agreement. Trump signed this agreement between the U.S., Canada and Mexico during his first term.

## Prospect of a trade war

In negotiations, it is not uncommon for one party to leave the table. This can signal that it does not accept certain conditions. Canadian Prime Minister Carney probably also wanted to show his compatriots that Canada would not tolerate everything.

However, there did not seem to have been a fundamental disagreement between the American and Canadian negotiators, Canadian media reported on Monday. Rather, they had not agreed on various detailed issues, the media noted.

In addition, the Canadians have probably learned to better assess Trump's behavior. The American president repeatedly threatens in harsh words but then backs down. Trump's new threat on Monday could also be seen in this context. It could be an attempt to exert pressure to get the Canadians to return to the negotiating table.

Both sides are likely aware that a full-blown trade war could cause significant damage. Not only would the Canadian economy, for which the U.S. is the most important sales market, suffer. Companies and consumers in the U.S. would also feel the consequences. Economists warned, for example, that tariffs on Canadian products could further drive up inflation in the U.S.

## Should Switzerland break off tariff negotiations with the U.S.?

![Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney explained the termination of tariff negotiations in a speech over the weekend.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2026/08/24/e130382e-6c40-4368-9728-116e6afefea3.jpg?crop=5500,3875,x0,y0)

No one in Switzerland is currently advocating for an end to talks with the U.S. However, there are certainly ideas for tougher negotiating tactics. These include, for example, selling U.S. shares held by the National Bank, restricting gold trade with the U.S. or stopping deliveries of critical components. «However, I consider it unrealistic that such measures would achieve anything,» said Rahul Sahgal, head of the Swiss-American Chamber of Commerce (Swiss Amcham).

Sahgal generally considered a more aggressive stance by Switzerland to be the wrong approach. «We have neither the power nor the means to confront them like Canada,» he added. For key products, for example, it had been shown that customers in the U.S. would bear the tariffs themselves so that they could import urgently needed goods from Switzerland, he noted. If the Federal Council wanted to stop such exports, it would have to impose an explicit delivery ban on domestic companies. «That might work in China, but hardly in Switzerland's free market,» Sahgal said.

According to Sahgal, Switzerland was currently doing exactly the right thing: It was fulfilling its part of the so-called Joint Statement signed last November, step by step. In this statement, the two countries agreed on a tariff ceiling of 15%. The pragmatic approach had proven successful. «Since the Joint Statement was agreed upon, the Americans had proven reliable,» he observed. Unlike other U.S. trading partners, Switzerland had been spared new, arbitrary tariffs.

## Switzerland's reaction to additional U.S. tariffs

The Swiss economy only conditionally trusted the current calm situation. The reason: Trump recently sharply criticized Switzerland for its low-interest rate policy, which he said disadvantaged the American export industry. Washington was currently preparing a report on this topic and on other market distortions controlled by foreign states. Depending on the result, Donald Trump could impose additional tariffs on Switzerland.

However, Sahgal did not attach excessive importance to Trump's rhetoric. The U.S. President had long called for lower interest rates in his own country and used Switzerland as a hook for his domestic political debate, Sahgal explained. «Despite the strong words, it did not necessarily mean that Switzerland was threatened with an even significantly higher tariff rate,» he added.

And even if the U.S. were to introduce additional tariffs to the currently applicable rate of 12.5%, Sahgal advocated for prudence: «If that were to happen, Switzerland would intervene as before and explain its situation to the U.S., instead of going for confrontation,» he said. His expectation remained that Washington would continue to respect the 15% ceiling promised in the Joint Statement, he concluded.
