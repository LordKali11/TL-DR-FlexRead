# A man rages in a cocaine frenzy at the «Baur au Lac» and «Storchen» hotels – and causes 167,000 Swiss francs in damage

*Luxury hotel rooms booked by electrician, claims of persecution delusion*

The «Baur au Lac» hotel is 182 years old, once built as Zurich’s first luxury hotel offering lake access. Throughout its history, it hosted a wide variety of guests. Empress Elisabeth of Austria, known as Sisi, resided here, as did pop star Elton John. Rarely, however, would hotel staff have encountered such a scene after a guest’s departure as they did on Oct. 20, 2024, in Room 310.

The curtains were torn down. Broken paintings lay on the floor. The carpet was destroyed, the wallpaper scratched and dented, and the television screen ripped off. The minibar furniture, the stool in the bathroom, and the floor and table lamps were smashed. Furnishings, thrown haphazardly, piled up in the bathtub.

The culprit was quickly identified, as he had properly checked in a week earlier: a then 32-year-old man from canton Aargau. He later stated that he had acted in a cocaine frenzy.

## Flying crockery in a luxury hotel

Hotels regularly have problems with undesirable guests. Such matters are usually settled out of court by establishments keen on discretion. However, it became known, for example, that former Raiffeisen CEO Pierin Vincenz caused 3,700 Swiss francs in damage in a suite at the «Park Hyatt» in Zurich and wrote off the bill as expenses.

Or the case of a 58-year-old Ukrainian who threw crockery around in the dining room of a Zurich five-star hotel, causing 12,000 francs in damage, apparently out of anger that he had to wait too long for his food.

The property damage in the present case, however, is on a completely different scale: here, the total damage amounts to no less than 167,000 francs.

The man, who stands before the District Court of Zurich on Tuesday, has little in common with the image of the uninhibited party animal from the jeunesse dorée commonly associated with cocaine users. He wears sneakers and a T-shirt, is unkempt, with a scraggly beard. His parents accompany him.

The 34-year-old still lives at home, is employed in a work integration program, and has applied for support from disability insurance. He presents the excessive property damage entirely differently from a drug-fueled orgy: He claims it was the result of psychological problems.

He says in court: «Yes, I destroyed the hotel rooms. But I heard voices and felt persecuted by the television.» The judge asks what he did the day after. «I changed hotels,» the man replies.

And indeed: just one day after the incident at the «Baur au Lac,» he caused similar devastation at the «Storchen» hotel, another traditional luxury hotel less than a kilometer away in the Old Town. Here, he ripped ceiling lights from their sockets, threw glass bottles against televisions and room walls, and scratched the wallpaper. In the hotel’s entrance area, he knocked over a large flower vase.

He says he is sorry, the man states at the trial. His defense counsel puts it in a broad Aargau dialect: «He made more than just a blunder.» But he argues that the man should be credited for admitting himself to a psychiatric clinic some time after the incidents at the hotels. He has been undergoing treatment ever since.

Within this framework, it is also being checked whether he is truly drug-free – and this is the case, his counsel states. The man himself says he had never heard voices before. He sees the delusions as a consequence of his cocaine use.

The lawyer’s problem is that his client did not undergo a forensic examination. He says this is not customary for property damage. Although specialists diagnosed the man with a schizophrenic illness, such a diagnosis does not carry the same weight as an expert opinion. Nevertheless, the lawyer demands an acquittal, arguing that his client was not criminally responsible at the time of the offense.

The court, however, does not agree, sentencing the man to a suspended monetary penalty of 4,500 francs and a fine of 200 francs for the property damage, as well as for drug possession. He was, after all, able to check into another hotel after the first excessive night without being noticed, the sole judge says.

Nevertheless, the court fundamentally believes the accused – and reduces the sentence accordingly. If someone had «committed these acts willfully and with joy,» the sentence would have been set at 1.5 years in prison, the sole judge said. So, it remained a monetary penalty.

## Hotels' claims for lost revenue

The reduced culpability attributed to him could also benefit the man regarding the civil proceedings that await him. Both hotels and their insurance companies have made claims. His lawyer criticized at the trial that the two hotels had not specified the damages precisely enough. However, his client is aware that «something will stick to him,» he added.

The pure property damage amounts to 75,500 francs. However, the majority of the total sum of 167,000 francs consists of the 91,500 francs that the hotels are claiming for lost profits. This is because they had to renovate the hotel rooms for weeks, the hotels say.

These details also show the price range of the rooms the man had chosen: 900 and 980 francs per night, respectively. It remains unclear at the hearing whether he at least paid for the overnight stays, and what prompted the man, an electrician, to stay in Zurich’s luxury hotels for over a week in the first place.
