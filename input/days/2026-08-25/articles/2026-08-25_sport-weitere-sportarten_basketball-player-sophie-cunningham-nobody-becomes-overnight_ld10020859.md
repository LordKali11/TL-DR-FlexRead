# Basketball player Sophie Cunningham, a nobody, becomes an overnight star for the American right through anti-trans rhetoric

Sophie Cunningham is a largely irrelevant basketball player, in sporting terms. She is 30, a shooting guard for the Indiana Fever, a role player. But her jersey has the second-highest sales of all players in the North American Women’s National Basketball Association (WNBA). Her own shoe, released in July, sold out within hours. She has become a poster girl for the American right, the MAGA movement, since she said in an ESPN interview a few weeks ago that «young girls in locker rooms» should be protected by not allowing them to compete «against biological males.»

Transgender athletes have been a topic the right has grappled with for years. The Trump administration enacted a series of laws that, for example, prohibit transgender and non-binary people from joining the military and from competing with girls in school sports. There is not a single transgender player in the WNBA. And among the more than 500,000 athletes in American college sports, fewer than 10 people identified as transgender, said Charlie Baker, the president of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA).

## 116 million dollars for anti-trans athlete ads

Although their proportion among all college athletes is only 0.002%, the outrage machine is cranked up at every opportunity. John Oliver, the British TV show host, noted this as well when he extensively covered the topic in his show «Last Week Tonight» in April 2025.

Republicans reportedly spent $116 million within a year on TV spots about transgender athletes. Joanna Harper, a sports scientist, said: «Transgender women do not transition for sports. Nobody has ever said: ‹Oh yes, I think I would like to be a woman so I can succeed in women’s sports.› When you go through a gender transition, you lose so much in life. My own mother said she would never speak to me again; I lost friends and family, got divorced . . . Would you take all that on just to win a medal? No. Transgender people transition because it is the only way for us to lead a happy life.»

It is a perspective that is of little interest to people like Riley Gaines. The swimmer finished fifth in the 200-yard freestyle at the NCAA final in 2022, at the same time as transgender athlete Lia Thomas. The fact that the award for fifth place was presented to Thomas on site and Gaines only received hers later by mail angered her so much that she ended her swimming career and has since reinvented herself as a right-wing political activist. It remains unclear what injustice might have befallen her: she would have finished fifth even without Thomas’s participation.

Gaines recently sat demonstratively in the front row at an appearance by Cunningham. The Indiana Fever games have become happenings where the culture war rages unfiltered. It is about interpretive authority and the comfortable feeling of being on the right side of history. Protests and marches by both ideological camps repeatedly occur outside the stadiums.

For the WNBA, this is good for business: when Indiana met Atlanta on Aug. 16, ESPN reached 2.6 million viewers, more than ever before in the league’s history. Conservative circles, whose interest in women’s sports was previously even lower than the current dollar-Swiss franc exchange rate, are discovering the league for themselves. Republican politicians even officially complained to the league that Caitlin Clark, Cunningham’s popular teammate, was not sufficiently protected by the referees. In part, this fixation seems to be unsettling even to some within their own camp. TV host Jesse Watters said on Fox News: «Why are we talking about the WNBA? Nobody cares.»

Even Cunningham herself recently seemed to find the excitement too much; in addition to approval from the right, she also received plenty of backlash and hate messages. At the beginning of the month, she complained that it should be about sports again, managing to include a media scolding in the process. Reporting too rarely focuses on the athletic aspect, Cunningham said – of all people. She is a devout Christian who loves to talk about everything but her sport; among other things, she hosts a podcast that deals, not least, with pop culture.

## Dildos on the court; timely World Cup break

Her supporters, among others, have contributed to the traditionally progressively oriented WNBA currently resembling a circus. The Swiss-born former NBA player Enes Kanter Freedom was ejected from the arena in Chicago over the weekend after donning a T-shirt with a message of support for Cunningham and getting into an argument with a Chicago player. Kanter, 34, has shown a penchant for controversial views and attention-grabbing stunts since his retirement. He recently said he now identifies as a woman and wants to register for the 2027 WNBA draft.

On a similar level of humor, men recently again threw dildos onto the court during a WNBA game. It was not the first of these bizarre incidents, but this time it happened explicitly in solidarity with Cunningham.

League play continues until Sunday, then there is a World Cup break. The championships will take place in Berlin from Sept. 4-13. Sophie Cunningham will not be there; she is not even a regular player in Indiana. However, her fervent wish that it should finally be about basketball again is likely to come true in Germany.
