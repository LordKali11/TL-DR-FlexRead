# Berlin crime: The cleaner is unfazed by her employer’s corpse

*In «The Oyster,» author Yael Inokai not only tells a gripping story, but her crime novel also proves to be a highly literary puzzle.*

One might call it fate. The most dispassionate person imaginable falls victim to a crime of passion. Wrapped in his elegant dressing gown, he lies dead in his villa, which until recently was clinically clean but is now no longer clean. Blood everywhere. Then the cleaner arrives and begins to clean. Yael Inokai, the author born in Basel and living in Berlin, begins her novel «The Oyster» with sharply etched scenes. What follows is a crime story unfolding beneath Berlin's gray winter sky, and a highly literary puzzle at the same time.

Joachim Bronstett was, until his unfortunate death, the managing director of a famous Berlin department store, clearly modeled after the KaDeWe. During the attack, the not-so-young man’s hands were severed with his own bread slicer. One of these hands is found among the oysters in the department store's fish buffet. The other, already gnawed by wild boars, is in the Grunewald forest.

## Beyond the usual scheme

«The Oyster» is a novel of brilliant reflections. It is literally about surfaces, but also about the depths of criminal depravity. Nobody knows more about surfaces than the cleaner Leonora Bloch. She has been cleaning for decades. She notices the smallest deviations from the normal state. The biggest deviation, however – the murder of her employer – she initially takes calmly. Because she only calls the police after finishing her work, she might be considered a suspect. But not for the Berlin detectives, not in a crime novel that refuses to follow the usual scheme of misleading the audience.

«The Oyster» consists of life stories tragically intertwined, whose beating heart is a cleaning specialist working long past retirement age. In the life of 73-year-old Leonora Bloch, there must once have been a man, but only a son remained from that relationship.

This Sebastian has long been dead, having died of AIDS. His friend Friedhelm has somehow remained attached to Leonora and is impossible to shake off. He calls at odd hours of the night. A kind of grieving process, still ongoing after many years. Friedhelm is the most incompetent store detective imaginable, but he seems to have had something on Dr. Bronstett that protected him from dismissal.

## Suspense in the retail environment

As uneventful as the managing director's life had been – a mere web of figures and quarterly reports – there must have been brief lapses in judgment. Two daughters were conceived at the same time with two different mothers. The two daughters were similar to each other, down to their reddish-blond hair. When they took a trip together, one died in a ferry accident. Did the other assume her identity? Then the one who calls herself Amande Bronstett and handles the funeral formalities would not be the legitimate daughter at all.

«The Oyster» is suspense in the retail environment. Almost all characters are directly connected to the famous department store on Kurfürstendamm. Geographically, the plot also revolves around it. Leonora herself has repeatedly cleaned in the grand retail emporium. She knows the saleswomen there, the smells, and the abundance of offerings. She knows that the staff in the household department have a different character than those in perfumery.

Already in her first two novels, «Mahlstrom» and «Ein simpler Eingriff,» Yael Inokai showed what good literature is made of: a microsociology of things and gestures. Nothing in the new novel seems superfluous. The distrustful relationship between the Bronstett daughter and Leonora becomes palpable in tiny gestures. Here, everyone prefers to be alone. Even the taciturn detective.

## A surprising ending

The novel describes the social strata of loneliness very precisely and uses laconic language to do so. Leonora's gaze over Dr. Bronstett's belongings: «His umbrellas with horn handles, his shoehorn, his shelf with the drawer for shoe polish, the wax, the brushes, the polishing cloth. Once, when they had met at the door, he had murmured _my father_. _My father and I used to clean shoes together every Saturday._ She never learned much more about this man.»

With her subtle novel «The Oyster,» Yael Inokai has written a double crime story. It is about clues – the traces people leave behind as they live their lives, and of course, the traces left by a murder. Readers follow both with great anticipation and are ultimately surprised by how things unfold.
