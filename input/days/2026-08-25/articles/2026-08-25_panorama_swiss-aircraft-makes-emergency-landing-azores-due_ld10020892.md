# Swiss aircraft makes emergency landing in Azores due to chemical smell

_ehj._ On Tuesday morning, Swiss flight LX9 from Chicago to Zurich sent a distress call over the Atlantic. The aircraft then landed on the island of Terceira around 6:30 a.m. local time, Swiss confirmed to the NZZ. The airline later announced the reason for the distress call: a pungent chemical smell in the Economy Class cabin. The smell could not be precisely identified.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 38eb3c6b76ee3897ce09f60436baf3e0]

Swiss said that in such a situation, the crew takes no risks and decided as a precautionary measure to land at a suitable airport. The airline added that in consultation with air traffic control, an emergency situation was also declared so the aircraft would receive priority in the airspace.

According to Swiss, all 222 passengers and crew left the aircraft via the normal exit. In addition to the passengers, 16 crew members were on board, including two pilots and 14 cabin crew members. About five passengers and one crew member sought medical examination as a precautionary measure after landing, the airline said. They had briefly experienced symptoms such as irritated eyes, nausea, or headaches, it added. The passengers would continue to receive the best possible care, and arrangements would be made for their prompt onward journey to Zurich, Swiss said.

The emergency was first reported by a Flightradar post on X. The report indicated that Code 7700, signaling a general in-flight emergency, was transmitted. Tracking data showed the aircraft then began its descent over the Azores.
