# Out-of-touch legislation: Too often, the EU forgets Switzerland and other third countries

*Many EU laws aim to improve the world. Too often recently, however, this has alienated trading partners. This should not happen with the next major project, which is due in the autumn.*

**Daniel Imwinkelried, Brussels** · Meinung · 2026-08-25 · 602 words · [ld.10020932](https://www.nzz.ch/meinung/weltfremde-gesetzgebung-zu-oft-hat-die-eu-juengst-die-schweiz-und-andere-drittstaaten-vergessen-ld.10020932)

Swiss companies have recently experienced firsthand how much EU legislation has at times missed the mark. Sometimes it even seemed as if the EU Parliament, in particular, remained unaware that the bloc is globally integrated, repeatedly overlooking relations with third countries such as Switzerland and others.

## EU also needs partners

For example, the EU recently sought to prevent member countries from shipping waste to distant African and Asian states through a regulation. However, the regulation would also have affected Switzerland and its neighboring regions. Many municipalities in cross-border areas are glad to send waste to Switzerland: they do not own incineration plants themselves, while Swiss plants have free capacity and readily accept the waste. Fortunately, the EU Commission adjusted the regulation at the last moment – partly because politicians in Germany exerted pressure.

There are other examples where the EU operated in a vacuum. For instance, with the deforestation regulation, it aims to prevent further deforestation for agricultural purposes. Here, too, producers from third countries were overlooked, such as Swiss chocolate manufacturers or American paper producers. The EU created a database to trace the origin of cocoa, wood, or soy. However, easy access to this database for third countries was overlooked in the legislative procedure. A non-tariff trade barrier loomed. In this case, too, the Commission had to painstakingly rectify the situation.

The latest example concerns the packaging regulation. It also serves a laudable purpose: less packaging waste. But it was also designed in such a complex way that not only EU manufacturers currently do not know the applicable rules. Traders and producers from third countries are also left entirely uncertain.

## Shocking images, hasty laws

One can imagine how the regulations came about. Politicians were confronted with shocking images of conditions: for example, mountains of waste in African states, or cleared forest areas used to produce more meat, which at the same time exacerbate climate change. Given the indignation about this, however, Brussels failed to consider involving third countries.

This oversight could recur this autumn. The EU will then debate the Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA). A key question will be whether states closely intertwined with the EU will once again be overlooked.

One goal of the IAA is to make the EU more independent from third countries for key products. The initiative is primarily aimed at China, but not exclusively. For example, there are proposals within the EU to restrict public procurement to a narrow circle: specifically, to the EU and the EEA members Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein.

French politicians, in particular, advocate this approach. German representatives, however, want to widen the circle, believing that Switzerland should be included. But what about other EU trading partners worldwide? Here, the German perspective is currently narrowing, partly because the industry is performing so poorly.

## No more bureaucracy

It is hoped that the EU Commission and the member states will learn from failed legislative processes. It is true that China is causing Europe's industry significant problems. But the EU can best address this bilaterally with China.

Overlooking third countries again would be costly for Europe: it would not only alienate friendly trading partners but also harm its own consumers and companies. They would be confronted with higher prices and more bureaucracy. This does not have to be the case – the EU should demonstrate such a capacity for learning.
