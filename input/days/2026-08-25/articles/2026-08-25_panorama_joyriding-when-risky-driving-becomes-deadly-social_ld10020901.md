# Joyriding: When risky driving becomes a deadly social media trend

*In recent weeks, twelve people have died in traffic accidents in Great Britain and Ireland, several of them involving young wrong-way drivers who film dangerous joyrides for social media.*

They drive in the oncoming lane, accelerate, and film themselves doing it. In the worst-case scenario, they collide head-on with an approaching vehicle. In Great Britain and Ireland, concern is growing about a dangerous social media trend that reportedly claimed twelve lives in recent weeks.

In Middlesbrough, England, two police officers and five teenagers died last weekend in a head-on collision between two vehicles. The teenagers were driving against the flow of traffic on a two-lane expressway. The accident was apparently preceded by a nighttime police chase. The officers killed were in a different patrol car that was not involved in the pursuit, as British media reported.

A week earlier, a similar fatal accident had occurred in Ireland. Five teenagers sped at night in a stolen BMW on the M9 motorway in County Kildare, driving on the wrong side of the road. The car crashed into a vehicle carrying a family on their way to Dublin Airport. The four family members sustained serious injuries. The five teenagers, aged between 15 and 18, did not survive.

According to the police, this accident was also preceded by a chase. However, the officers had already called off the pursuit due to the dangerous driving of the teenagers when the collision with the family's car occurred. In another similar accident recently, nine people were injured in Dublin.

## «They are playing Russian roulette»

British and Irish media link the cases to joyriding. The term refers to risky car journeys in which young people steal or use cars without authorization. They speed down streets, film themselves, and deliberately provoke police chases. They often intentionally drive in the oncoming lane. But what drives these young people to such dangerous driving?

The thrill plays a role. Apparently, the prospect of likes on social networks also counts. The riskier the footage, the greater the attention seems to be. According to media reports, videos of illegal and dangerous car journeys were found on the accounts of some of the deceased. These included footage of police chases.

Ireland's Police Chief Justin Kelly called the accident in Kildare an «appalling incident.» He said it showed «how irresponsible and dangerous driving on the wrong side of the motorway is.» Kelly found it particularly outrageous that «in some cases, it's all about likes on social media.»

James Morrisroe, the Deputy General Secretary of the Irish police union GRA, expressed his disbelief. He said he did not know what was going through the minds of teenagers who would drive in the wrong direction on a motorway. «They are playing Russian roulette with their own lives and Russian roulette with the lives of other people,» he added.

At the same time, the Irish police are registering significantly more wrong-way driving incidents. So far in 2026, they counted 62 cases. In the same period last year, there were 47. This corresponds to an increase of around 32%. «These figures show that these perpetrators are out of control,» Morrisroe said. «This is no longer a trend, but a clear and present danger to every road user in the country,» he added.

## Teenagers previously investigated by police

In Ireland, investigators are now examining whether the joyriding trend is truly the reason for the fatal drive. According to Irish media, at least three of the deceased were among the country's most active and problematic joyriders. The police had already investigated all of them for serious traffic offenses and multiple car thefts before the fatal accident.

Investigators had already obtained a court order against one of the teenagers. This allowed them to secure data from his numerous TikTok accounts. There, he published videos of his risky car journeys. According to the investigators, he filmed himself during dangerous driving maneuvers. These also included wrong-way driving on motorways. On several occasions, other road users narrowly avoided a head-on collision.

In the case of Middlesbrough, the police paint a different picture. The Cleveland Police found no evidence that those involved had recorded videos for TikTok. According to the police, the accident vehicle was linked to a second car that had attracted attention for dangerous driving. Both vehicles bore fake license plates. Investigators arrested nine men and three women aged between 19 and 61.

## Political calls for TikTok accountability

The debate has nevertheless reached the British government. «Anyone glorifying dangerous driving is doing something truly reprehensible,» Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on Monday during a visit to the Ukrainian capital, Kyiv. Luke Pollard, the Minister of State for the Armed Forces, announced that the government had urged social media companies to delete such videos from platforms like TikTok.

In Ireland, political pressure is also growing. The relevant media committee summoned TikTok representatives. The committee said the company was supposed to explain why such videos sometimes remained online for a long time. However, TikTok declined the invitation. The company stated that the platform prohibits content that could cause physical harm, including dangerous driving. According to its own statements, TikTok removes 99% of such videos before users report them.

The Irish government is now planning tougher penalties for wrong-way drivers. In addition, it intends to draft legislation to protect police officers «who conduct vehicle pursuits.» Justice Minister Jim O’Callaghan urgently warned wrong-way drivers of the consequences: «They end up in prison. Or in the grave.»
