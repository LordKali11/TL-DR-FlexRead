# All inhibitions fall at anti-Israel demonstrations; escalation now threatens in Basel

*Unauthorized demonstration announced for Saturday; open threats of violence*

**Sebastian Briellmann** · Schweiz · 2026-08-25 · 833 words · [ld.10020794](https://www.nzz.ch/schweiz/bei-anti-israel-demonstrationen-fallen-alle-hemmungen-hamas-terror-wird-glorifiziert-judenfeindliche-parolen-sind-standard-nun-droht-in-basel-die-eskalation-ld.10020794)

On Monday, the Jewish Community of Basel (IGB) sent a «special newsletter» to its members. The reason: a demonstration announced for Saturday in Basel, operating under the motto «Death to Zionism.» The posters show red triangles, which Hamas typically uses to mark its targets.

The IGB perceives this as «intimidation» and «threats of violence» against Jewish life. The demonstration's timing on the anniversary of the First Zionist Congress of 1897, which took place in Basel, was a «conscious» choice, the IGB says. The community condemns this «attack on Jewish identity and belonging.»

This is only the latest stage of escalation. The sense of security among Jews has been declining for years, intensifying since Oct. 7, 2023. One in three considers emigrating. The number of antisemitically motivated attacks also rose sharply in Switzerland after the Hamas terror attack. The worst occurred in March 2024, when a 15-year-old Swiss with Tunisian roots stabbed an Orthodox Jew. The father of the family survived only by luck. He was a random victim. The teenager simply wanted to kill Jews.

This is the result of an aggressive atmosphere, which is also maintained by weekly demonstrations – and a well-organized online presence. Officially for Gaza and the suffering of Palestinians. Increasingly, however, it is simply against Israel, often garnished with antisemitic messages. One only needs to think of the «Shame of Bern,» when a demonstration last year escalated into violence. But even at smaller demonstrations, protests against «Zionism» and an alleged «genocide,» accompanied by at least antisemitically tinged slogans, have become standard.

Philip Bessermann, managing director of the Foundation against Racism and Antisemitism, told the NZZ last year: «Antisemitic narratives are being normalized, relativized, or presented as a legitimate part of political debates. This lowers the inhibition threshold for exclusion and provides fertile ground for violence.» And Jonathan Kreutner, Secretary General of the Swiss Federation of Jewish Communities (SIG), said that the boundaries of what could be said had shifted, he contended. Now also on the anniversary of the first Zionist Congress, when the demonstration will take place.

## Little criticism from politicians

Erik Petry, a professor at the Center for Jewish Studies at the University of Basel, views this shift critically: «This demonstration call uses language that no longer advocates for a cause, such as for the Palestinians. Instead, it is clearly against something. Against Israel, and also against Jewish people. There are no more subtleties in it, for example, specific criticism of the Israeli government. It is much more. It is a call to murder.» Anyone who promotes with Hamas triangles and slogans like «Death to Zionism» or «Basel zio-free» is endorsing this violence, Petry argues. For Petry, it is clear: «This is unacceptable.»

Petry says the Jewish population is concerned, even frightened. He also states that societal developments are alarming. He poses the crucial question: «When do such calls for violence become part of general discourse? When does no one get upset anymore?»

If these potentially criminal statements are only seen as a trivial provocation, it will become dangerous, Petry warns. «This is no longer a discourse; a line is clearly being crossed.»

But society is increasingly getting used to the violent language. The core group of anti-Israel agitators may be small, but their messages are so loud that they reach a large audience, especially online. Although the upcoming demonstration has been reported internationally, so far only the editor-in-chief of the «Basellandschaftliche Zeitung» – apart from Jewish representatives – has criticized the «antisemitic demo» and the silence of «official Basel» in Switzerland.

Upon inquiry, however, Conradin Cramer, the liberal cantonal president of the city-canton, now also says that he condemns the slogans and symbols, stating: «The calls in connection with this demonstration cause great concern and feelings of intimidation and threat among Jewish residents. This is unacceptable.»

Cramer advocates for criticism of Israel's government («must be possible»), but this must not turn into «antisemitism, the blanket condemnation of Jews, or questioning their right to live in security,» he contends.

## Director of Security «repulsed»

That hardly seems to bother the activists. They did not have their demonstration authorized. How the police will react will become clear by Saturday at the latest. In an email to concerned citizens, which the NZZ has seen, Basel's Director of Security Stephanie Eymann writes that she feels «repulsed» by the slogans used.

The situation is being observed with «the greatest attention» and the protection of Jewish institutions is being strengthened, the Basel security directorate states. It is therefore likely to be expected that the police will give the demonstrators little leeway.

For the activists, however, the call to demonstrate could already have consequences. The Basel prosecutor's office announced on Monday that no criminal complaint regarding the demonstration had yet been received, it said. But that is likely to change. The NZZ has several (planned) complaints.

However, the prosecutor's office could also act on its own initiative. Possible criminal offenses such as violating the anti-racism criminal code, as well as incitement to violence, are official offenses. The Switzerland-Israel Society is already demanding such a criminal investigation in a letter addressed to the Basel government.
