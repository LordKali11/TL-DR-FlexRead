# Zurich maintains fire ban: Neighboring cantons bolder, despite less rain there

*The cantonal building authority has reassessed the situation. Anyone wanting to light a fire outdoors will have to be patient – ​​unlike in Zug or St. Gallen.*

For barbecue enthusiasts and fire romantics in the canton of Zurich, impatience is slowly but surely growing. It rained for four days within a week recently, and more rain is expected toward the end of the week. Are these now those «heavy, widespread precipitation events» that were always cited as a condition for lifting the current fire ban?

This absolute ban, which even applies in private gardens, risks being overlooked as lawns turn green again. But it remains in effect in many Zurich municipalities. And for the time being, it will remain so – unlike in several neighboring cantons.

The Zurich Department of Construction reassessed the situation. As the department announced Thursday morning, the fire ban in and near forests continued to apply. The department justified this by stating that the Zurich forest remained very dry. Although it had rained widely in recent days, the topsoil and deadwood had not yet been re-moistened, it added. Therefore, a high risk of forest fires remained, it said. It therefore remains forbidden to light fires in forests and within 50 meters of forest edges. This ban also applies to fixed official fire pits as well as charcoal fires and grills. Gas and electric grills are exempt from the fire ban.

Further heavy precipitation was required to ease the situation and lift the ban, authorities said. If the Department of Construction’s forestry division concludes that the fire ban can be lifted, it will provide information.

The canton initially issued a fire ban for forests and forest edges at the end of June. The municipalities then went further in July by introducing a general ban. Lifting this again is generally a municipal responsibility.

But the municipal authorities did not want to make arbitrary decisions and thus prevent a patchwork of rules from arising between municipalities. Therefore, they also relied on assessments from expert groups that converged at the canton. Everything ultimately depended on its decision. And this decision upheld the fire ban.

## Fires in forests allowed again in Zug

At the canton borders, however, uniformity ended. This showed that risk analysis was not an exact science and there was room for discretion. In the neighboring cantons, bans had already been relaxed days ago, even though precipitation there was sometimes less than in neighboring Zurich municipalities.

For example, in Wädenswil on Lake Zurich, about 120 millimeters of rain were measured in the past seven days, almost three-quarters of the long-term norm for August. In the Zug municipality of Cham, only just under 80 millimeters fell, and slightly over 100 millimeters in Rapperswil-Jona.

Nevertheless, authorities in Rapperswil-Jona announced last weekend that fires outside forests could be lit again. They reasoned that precipitation in the past few days had reduced the risk of forest fires. The canton of St. Gallen had therefore partially lifted the ban, they added.

The canton of Zug went even further, where the forest fire risk had been lowered to the third of five levels. It was now considered only «considerable,» while in Schwyz and St. Gallen it was considered «high,» and on Zurich soil still «very high.» The consequence: In one place, fires in forests are still taboo, while in another, across the canton border, they have been possible again for a few days, at least in fixed fire pits.

That similar situations were assessed differently depending on the canton was already evident at the beginning of August. Around the Rapperswil-Jona Lake Festival, a controversy arose at that time because organizers were allowed to set off large fireworks from the water despite the fire ban. St. Gallen authorities considered this permissible due to the pyrotechnic system's large distance from the shore.
