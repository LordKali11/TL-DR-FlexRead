# Ayyoub Bouaddi is the mathematician among the midfield playmakers – can he replace Spaniard Rodri at Manchester City?

What can be expected of an 18-year-old footballer without it becoming too much for him? When it comes to Ayyoub Bouaddi, the Moroccan international, the confidence in his abilities seems almost boundless. This is evident from several indicators, which are remarkable in themselves.

Bouaddi plays for OSC Lille and is on the verge of transferring to Manchester City for around 100 million euros – it would be the highest sum ever paid for a footballer playing for a national team from the African Football Confederation, and certainly the highest amount for a player who has just come of age.

## Distance Learning Alongside His Career

The other factor is the trust that is apparently placed in him. Bouaddi is a player of great strategic skill. During his school years, he stood out not only as a gifted mathematician – he achieved his university entrance qualification a year earlier than usual; he is also considered exceptionally gifted rhetorically. The fact that he continues a distance learning degree in mathematics alongside his football career truly makes him an exceptional case. To better understand the game – that is also the goal of his mathematical studies, Ayyoub Bouaddi told «Le Parisien.»

Presumably less algebraic, but with a trigonometric focus: anticipating the opponent's moves, positioning oneself at the right moment – that is an extremely rare gift that distinguishes very good midfielders from exceptional ones. No one currently masters this better than Spain’s Rodri, a World and European champion with Spain. He has signed with FC Barcelona after a long, successful period in England. Bouaddi is reportedly set to take his place at Manchester City.

## Deschamps's Rejection

A fairytale ascent? Certainly – yet despite his young age, he has already had to contend with adversity. For at its core, the story of this exceptional talent is also one of inner conflict. Bouaddi has Moroccan parents but was born in France; he could just as easily have played for the French Football Federation. As recently as March, he captained the French U21 team onto the field.

Didier Deschamps, coach of the Équipe Tricolore for fourteen years, however, did not consider it necessary to rely on the young man – which is why Bouaddi ultimately opted for Morocco. It is not at all impossible that Deschamps will one day regret this step, as the end of his career as national coach was foreseeable. But Bouaddi’s ambition was great enough that he absolutely wanted to travel to the World Cup, ideally with a starting spot.

That Deschamps ignored him can actually only be explained by hubris: Apparently, it was enough for him that the ball somehow reached the fabulous attacking trio Ousmane Dembélé, Kylian Mbappé, and Michael Olise. Then, the national coach probably calculated that something would work out. That was enough until the World Cup semi-final, and also in the quarter-final against Morocco, in a match where Bouaddi was overwhelmed.

Critics later argued that Deschamps had acted correctly – that the young Moroccan was simply not up to the level he himself demanded. However, this perspective ignores one thing: his midfield partner, the offensively oriented Ismael Saibari, was injured in that match – which meant Bouaddi too rarely received relief.

Deschamps probably regretted his decision against Bouaddi when he witnessed how his team was utterly outclassed in the semi-final against Spain. Attack or no attack: Once again, it proved true that big games are usually decided in the center. And there, Fabián Ruiz and Rodri – Bouaddi's predecessor in Manchester – reigned.

## Guardiola's Manchester Exit

So, is everything already set for Bouaddi’s global career? The indicators suggest so, yet caution is advised. After all, there is a reason why Rodri ended his time in Manchester and moved on to FC Barcelona: Pep Guardiola, the man who recognized the genius in Rodri that he truly is, has stepped down as coach at Manchester City. Although his successor Enzo Maresca is visually something of a doppelgänger for the Catalan, it would be wrong to assume the players will not notice the difference. Thus, it is more than just one variable that will decide whether Ayyoub Bouaddi becomes a global star.
