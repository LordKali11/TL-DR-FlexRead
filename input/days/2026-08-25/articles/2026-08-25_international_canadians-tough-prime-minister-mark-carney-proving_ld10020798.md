# Canadians are tough. Prime Minister Mark Carney is proving that in the trade dispute with the US

*Of all people, the former banker wants to show Donald Trump that money isn't everything.*

Perhaps he really needed the vacation. The timing of Mark Carney's mid-August trip to Italy had drawn much criticism. Donald Trump's ultimatum for 50% punitive tariffs was set to expire. While negotiations entered a critical phase, the Canadian prime minister was under the Tuscan sun. But he apparently felt strengthened afterward.

He returned with a clear sense of purpose. He refused Trump, let the talks collapse, and announced retaliatory tariffs. While the Americans targeted Canadian exports like hockey sticks, furniture, and wine, Carney retaliated with tariffs on milk, steel, agricultural equipment, and electrical appliances.

«Canada will reciprocate Washington’s new tariffs dollar for dollar,» he said. Carney maintained that it was necessary to protect Canada’s farmers, families, and businesses from American harassment. The conditions proposed by Trump, he argued, had «called into question the reliability of any agreement.»

Or as Ontario Premier Doug Ford, a Conservative, put it: «President Trump is the type of person who steals your lunch money on the first day. He steals your hat off your head on the second day, and your running shoes on the third day.» Ford said Trump could not be trusted, and Carney thinks so too. Most Canadians share this view.

## Canadians fed up with Trump

In an Angus Reid Institute survey, more than 75% of respondents stated that the prime minister was right to remain stubborn. A majority had already indicated that they would henceforth avoid American products, regardless of how the negotiations turned out. In short, Canadians are fed up with Trump.

No wonder, because the National Review writes that since James Madison, who launched several invasions in the early 19th century, no American president had been so anti-Canadian. Trump’s constant invectives, wanting to make Canada the 51st U.S. state, played into Carney’s hands – even if they were hollow rhetoric.

Why would the Republican want to acquire an additional state that would certainly lean Democrat? At the same time, it is clear that Canada is in a weaker position in the trade dispute. «We don’t need anything Canada has,» Trump said, «but they need everything we have.»

That, of course, is not entirely true. Canadians, for example, obviously don’t need bourbon. A ban on U.S.-made whiskey, which came into effect in March 2025, is already being called «Canada’s Strait of Hormuz»: a small choke point that inflicts economic pain on the Americans.

But bourbon abstinence only offers a taste of what Carney yearns for. He promises to rebalance the American-Canadian power dynamic. Canada’s economic relations with the U.S. were a «weakness that we must correct,» he said in a speech in June.

## The big appearance at the WEF

After taking over the leadership of the Liberal Party and the office of head of government from Justin Trudeau in March 2025, Carney has mantra-like emphasized Canadian sovereignty. The former central banker, who had never held political office before his rapid ascent, is pushing for economic and security policy independence from the United States.

Of course, he is not an isolationist national conservative. He laid out his geopolitical guiding principle at the last World Economic Forum (WEF) in Davos, as he took on Trump without even naming him. He said that in a world where «the strong can do what they can, and the weak suffer what they must,» it was necessary to unite with like-minded people. «If we are not at the table, we are on the menu,» he added.

The speech went viral. The global citizen with Canadian, Irish, and British passports had «stood up to Trump,» as the British Guardian jubilated. But not only in Switzerland; he had repeatedly pushed back against American great power posturing even before that. No agreement was better than a bad one, he said repeatedly. «We will not allow any nation to determine our future,» he also declared after the failed negotiations on Friday.

«Friday, August 21, could go down in history as Canada’s Liberation Day,» wrote Canadian author Stephen Marche in The New York Times: «It was the day America wanted to take us, and we said no.»

## Shaped by the wilderness

For Marche, a Canadian peculiarity is expressed in Carney’s steadfastness. Americans, he believes, prefer to project and gain power. «In Canada, the defining virtue is toughness.»

Marche explained that even the «Coureurs des bois» (French fur traders) who ventured into the inhospitable north by canoe had measured themselves by their ability to survive with the bare necessities in the rough wilderness. This mentality is now also evident in the rebuff Carney delivered to Trump.

Enduring things is typically Canadian. However, Carney’s gesture of resistance will resonate far beyond the country’s borders, Marche writes. He added that a «gray-haired banker,» of all people, had risen up and made it clear that there was more to life than a bit of money. «Trump’s world – a world of transactions and subjugation – is not the only world we all have to live in.»

While Mark Carney, well-rested from his vacation, seems prepared for the arduous fight against Trump, it remains to be seen how long the Canadian population is willing to endure economic hardship.
