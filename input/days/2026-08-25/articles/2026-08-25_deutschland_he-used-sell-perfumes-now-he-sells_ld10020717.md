# He used to sell perfumes, now he sells the AfD program: Ulrich Siegmund's triumphant procession

*On his campaign tour, the AfD's lead candidate sparks genuine euphoria. But voters' expectations for a first AfD government are high.*

The midday sun beats down mercilessly on the square in the small town of Zahna-Elster. The air is hot and dry, yet a queue of people has formed. It leads to a man in a white polo shirt. He smiles broadly, his teeth gleaming. With his athletic build, he quickly stands out.

«Hello, I’m Ulli,» he says, extending his hand to a gentleman in a faded dark tank top with sunglasses pushed up on his head – warmly, as if they had known each other for years. He doesn't need to add his name. Ulrich Siegmund. Everyone here already knows him.

He is the man who could shake the republic on Sept. 6. On that day, the eastern German federal state of Saxony-Anhalt elects a new state parliament. Since 2022, Siegmund has led the AfD parliamentary group there as co-chairman. The current opposition leader has a real chance of becoming state premier.

## A jolt through this country!

The people he meets on his campaign tour across Saxony-Anhalt also assume this. Wherever he goes, Ulrich Siegmund is celebrated like a pop star. At larger events, chants of his first name erupt: «Ulli, Ulli, Ulli!»

Sometimes he rides a sky-blue Simson Schwalbe («It’s just awesome! A jolt is going through this country!») – the legendary mode of transport for East German youth – sometimes he opens family festivals («Hello Merseburg, hello common sense!»), sometimes he grabs a drill to help a craftsman renovate an old building («Side by side with the backbone of our country!»).

An older woman with reddish hair waits patiently at her rollator, her hands tightly gripping the handles. The sun above her is at its highest point. Siegmund spots her. And reacts immediately. His voice cuts through the onlookers: «Do you want a photo? Then you can go back into the shade.» The next moment, the lady stands next to Siegmund. Both grin into the camera. Done.

In the afternoon, he has a moment’s break. Someone has brought him a soft-serve ice cream. The tall chestnut tree provides shade. Instead of using the quiet minute to catch his breath, he approaches the reporter: «Well, you’re still here. Oh, now you don’t even have ice cream. Shall I get you one?» Perhaps this explains part of his success: Siegmund’s warmth is not a role he slips into – like many other politicians – for the stage. It is his first impulse.

A television reporter later asks Siegmund if he is already looking forward to his first beer. At an evening festival with the motto «Politics with Beer and Bratwurst,» Siegmund therefore preventively declares on stage that he is not drinking beer today, but only once they have won. A tour like this cannot be sustained without some sacrifices.

Because such evenings often run longer. «Finally an end to this unregulated migration,» he shouts to the crowd. «So that we can finally abide by our own laws and distinguish between those who want to participate and lend a hand, and those who exploit our system. They can go home. They are not welcome here.» This is met with thunderous applause.

He pauses, looking into their faces. «I want each of you to go home today with a good feeling. That we are on the right side. We are the future, dear friends. Here in Saxony-Anhalt, we are making history right now. Not only the East is looking at this election. All of Europe is looking at it.»

Even the competition amuses him. To a handful of journalists crowding at the edge of the small rush for the autograph session in the paved market square in Lutherstadt Wittenberg, he says: «The CDU has now become our best campaigner.» And then adds: «There are people who think we don’t need to do anything anymore. But I want to win this election because of our own positions and content.»

The lead candidate has already made one thing clear: He only wants to lead a single-party government. Coalitions with the Christian Democratic Union, The Left, Social Democratic Party, The Greens, or The Liberals are out of the question for the others anyway. Siegmund also rejects the prospect of being elected in the third ballot through the Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht’s abstention. He wants a stable majority, and he says he would not have that with the BSW. The idea that someone would want to return the goods Siegmund has sold due to disappointment, he maintains, is out of the question for him.

## Disarming friendliness

A few days ago, the Hamburg magazine «Der Spiegel» declared him «Germany’s most dangerous man.» Hardly had the cover been made public, when a video circulated showing Siegmund calmly signing a stack of the magazine adorned with his face. No sign of outrage or anger. That is typical Siegmund: smilingly one-upping them.

Especially for the AfD, whose members and officials are sometimes prone to quick offense, this is remarkable. Where others react with withdrawal, icy coldness, or aggression, he has adopted a different approach: disarming friendliness.

He seeks dialogue where others slam the door. In their four-hour conversation, he told podcaster Ben Berndt that he had gotten into the habit of «going over to these leftists.» His rival Sven Schulze, the incumbent state premier and Christian Democratic Union lead candidate, was also invited to the format. The Christian Democratic Union is polling between 22 and 26%, far behind the AfD. Schulze declined.

After one week, the podcast reached five million clicks and over 60,000 comments, most of them completely enthusiastic. The salesman had the store all to himself.

Siegmund was born in Havelberg a year after the fall of the Berlin Wall. He grew up in picturesque Tangermünde, a town of 10,000 inhabitants with a medieval castle, located directly on the Elbe River. In the podcast «Ungeskriptet,» he described it as «simply an idyllic world,» a town «like something out of a picture book, as if you open a book and everything has stood still.»

## Once he sold room fragrances, now the AfD program

This is precisely the image he promises his voters: the good old Germany, where children could still play unsupervised and asylum homes were at most a distant big-city problem. Nostalgia and promises of a new beginning all in one. «Germany has many Tangermündes,» he recently put it.

At «18 or 19,» Siegmund first joined the Christian Democratic Union. He says today that the party events were always the same. That frustrated him. In 2014, he joined the AfD; the trigger, he says, was the euro rescue policy. After his commercial training, he became self-employed.

He sold room fragrances: calming lavender to dental practices, orange or bergamot to fitness studios. Most people, he said, left «with a positive feeling.» Then he knew: «What you’re doing is good.» He is no longer involved in his company.

But the sales talent has remained. Only now, instead of perfumes, Siegmund sells the AfD program. And it works. Whether in Wittenberg, Teicha, or Querfurt: wherever he appears, his fans are already waiting, predominantly male, between 30 and 70. A T-shirt reads «Stand Up for Germany.»

But families, teenage couples, and older women also queue up for a selfie. Siegmund himself is married and a father to two daughters; his wife brought one of them into the marriage.

A woman around 60 with frizzy hair and purple-tinted glasses squeezes his hand: «I hope you win.» A younger woman in Capri pants and a floral top says to her daughter: «Now my dream is fulfilled, now we’re going home.» However, there are not only selfie requests, but also concerns are raised. The high energy and fuel prices are repeatedly mentioned, as are expensive nursing homes and the school system, which is perceived as indoctrinating.

## «With love for the country, anything is possible»

A gentleman with a mustache, a shop owner, stands before him in sandals. He had accepted Corona aid and now had to pay it back, he says. He could not help that his shop had to close, the man says: «That was the state.» What would the AfD do about it? The man hopes for amnesty.

Siegmund calls that a «political disgrace,» but then plays it down. He says one must adhere to the legal situation. He adds that he cannot clear up the mess left by the state government with a snap of his fingers. Expectations for the first AfD government are extremely high. Sometimes he seems to want to lower them a little.

At the end of such a day, he has taken over 500 photos and videos. Does he not run out of steam? «No, why? It gives me strength,» he tells the NZZ. 35 degrees Celsius? «With love for the country, anything is possible. There’s no such thing as too hot or too cold.»

A reporter asks if he understands that some people are afraid of an AfD government. «No, not at all, there is no reason to be afraid here,» Siegmund says. He explains the reservations, saying, «It is often emotionally based, what the supposed concerns are.» He adds that with a conversation and a «look at the facts,» that could be «very quickly debunked.»

## Shady environment?

This is the story Siegmund knows how to sell best. It is not complete. Because in his inner circle there are men who once maintained concrete connections to right-wing extremism. Like his press spokesman Patrick Harr, a friendly man whom he trusts.

Harr was once head of department at the Heimattreue Deutsche Jugend (HDJ). Laurens Nothdurft, the parliamentary group’s legal adviser, was also active in the association, according to «Der Spiegel» investigations, as «Second Federal Leader.» The association, founded in 1990, was banned in 2009 for «essential kinship with National Socialism, particularly with the former Hitler Youth.»

According to MDR investigations, four former HDJ officials work for the AfD in Saxony-Anhalt. The HDJ is on the party’s incompatibility list. Siegmund knows about the past. But what happened 20 or 30 years ago is irrelevant to him, he says. «I look forward,» he told «Der Spiegel.»

## Participation in Potsdam meeting has consequences

Siegmund gained nationwide recognition through his participation in the Potsdam meeting in November 2023, where neo-right activist Martin Sellner spoke about remigration. The investigation by the medium «Correctiv» caused great outrage. Today, Siegmund amusedly speaks of the «Potsdam coffee klatch.»

At the time, however, the consequences for him were less amusing. At the end of February 2024, he was removed as chairman of the Committee for Labor, Social Affairs, Health, and Equality in the Saxony-Anhalt state parliament. A companion says today that Siegmund had fallen into a hole at the time.

In February 2026, Siegmund was caught up in a nepotism affair. Several family members of AfD parliamentarians, including his own father, are employed by party colleagues in Magdeburg and Berlin. The so-called cross-appointments are legal, but they hit the party at a sore spot because it accuses established parties of exactly such cronyism. Siegmund played down the allegations, while criticism was voiced even from within his own ranks.

## Program: «Deportation Offensive» and «Birth Rate Turnaround»

While scandals flare up around him, Siegmund does not lose sight of his goal: to bring the AfD into government. Hans-Thomas Tillschneider, an Islamic studies scholar and deputy parliamentary group leader, is largely behind the 256-page government program. Siegmund and he know each other well.

Tillschneider wants to bring about a «patriotic turnaround» for Saxony-Anhalt. The core is a «deportation offensive.» He says 100 million euros are to be provided for this in an alternative budget. He adds that the former reception center in Stendal is to become a deportation detention facility. He wants to solve the shortage of skilled workers with a «birth rate turnaround,» not through immigration.

In education, he says permanent «special classes for refugee children» are to be set up separately from regular classes to signal that their stay is «only temporary» and to protect «our children» from the «burdens» of foreign cultures.

When Siegmund is accused of the program having a two-billion-euro gap, he remains calm. «The money is there,» he tells the circle of reporters who listen intently. He says it only needs to be invested for «our own country and for our own people.» Saxony-Anhalt is the first step. But the big wheel must be turned in Berlin.

As his first official act, Siegmund wants to terminate the State Media Treaty and abolish the «mandatory GEZ fees.» The relationship with the media plays a major role in the AfD. «From day one, we had different rules of the game than other parties,» Siegmund says.

Social media became his way out. He has over 700,000 subscribers on Tiktok. He used to be mocked for it, but today it is a campaign factor. Sven Schulze reacted with a poster: «Tiktok doesn’t elect a state premier.»

Tillschneider tells the NZZ that Siegmund is «a beacon of hope for the people.» They project their hopes onto him and celebrate him like a savior, he adds. With his charisma, charm, and communication talent, he is «a great stroke of luck» for the AfD, Tillschneider concludes.

## Why he could shake the republic

Saxony-Anhalt is to become a blueprint for all of Germany. An AfD government in the smallest eastern German federal state could become a domino that topples other states and breaks down the Christian Democratic Union’s firewall. In autumn 2026, Saxony-Anhalt would, in rotation, hold the chairmanship of the Conference of Minister-Presidents. That could elevate Siegmund to the negotiating stage alongside Federal Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

Alice Weidel stated it bluntly at the campaign launch in Magdeburg: «Thanks to you, thanks to Saxony-Anhalt, Ulrich Siegmund will be the first state premier of the Alternative for Germany!»

If Siegmund were to win customers instead of voters, his turnover on a campaign day like in Zahna-Elster would be enormous. The salesman has the goods. And people are queuing up. Whether it will be enough for a single-party government will be decided on Sept. 6.
