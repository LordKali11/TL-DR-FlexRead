# First FMH data on tax point cap: only 7% of doctors affected

*There is widespread discontent among doctors. From 2027, they will only be allowed to charge a maximum number of tax points for certain treatments. An analysis by the FMH (Swiss Medical Association) shows that a large proportion of doctors are already operating below the proposed limit.*

The tax point cap for medical services caused a stir among doctors this summer. Some doctors worry they will have to close their practices earlier because they would already reach the planned maximum tax point limit by midday. This, they fear, endangers supply security.

According to initial analyses by the Swiss Medical Association (FMH), the regulation showed that it would have no impact on the majority of doctors. Only about 7% of doctors are affected by the tax point cap, which is set to be introduced on Jan. 1, 2027, the FMH said.

## Individual specializations and practice constellations affected

The planned tax point cap dates back to cost-containment package 2 from 2025. At that time, Parliament decided to introduce it along with other measures. Parliamentarians had then presented invoices on which doctors listed unrealistically high working hours. They suspected abuse behind this, which they wanted to combat with the new law.

At the beginning of July, the FMH, the health insurer association Prioswiss, and the hospital association H+ published a concept for the maximum tax point limit, which they had submitted to the Federal Council for review. It provides that doctors may charge a daily average of a maximum of 1577 tax points for medical services within one month, according to the FMH, Prioswiss, and H+. This corresponds to an average of 12 hours per day that doctors spend on patient consultations and examinations. Services billed through supplementary insurance, staff and infrastructure services, emergencies, outpatient flat rates, and report writing are not included.

The 7% of affected doctors are specialists from individual fields and specific practice constellations. There are treatments, such as ultrasound examinations, that are frequently performed in certain specializations, for which the recorded times have not been updated for years. This is because the revision of outpatient tariffs was blocked for years as the associations of health insurers, doctors, and hospitals could not agree.

Due to new technical possibilities, these treatments today, for example, take only 25 minutes, although they are still recorded as 50 minutes in the tariff system. In addition, some doctors are faster because they have more experience and routine than others. The recorded times – referred to in jargon as minute values – are average values and do not correspond to the actual time spent on every treatment. In such cases, it is possible that a doctor reaches the tax point limit of 1577 points even before the practice closes.

Furthermore, practice constellations could be affected where, for example, 10 doctors are employed but all bill through one doctor, the FMH said. Here, overruns of the maximum limit are sometimes counted where none exist. The FMH has not yet been able to filter out all affected practices.

## Council of States commission to revisit limit

The FMH said it had always fought such a limit. However, since Parliament had decided on it, the association had been forced to submit a concept together with the health insurers and hospitals. If this had not happened, the Federal Council would have had to set the limit itself in an ordinance, it added. Because the new Tardoc tariff had only been introduced on Jan. 1, 2026, no data had been available for the development of the concept, the FMH stated. These had only been available for evaluation since mid-July, it noted.

Doctors now have the opportunity to check the effects of the tax point cap on their practice in their software. The FMH has also sent questionnaires to the professional associations to determine the impact on individual specializations. They can submit these anonymously to the FMH by the end of September.

Due to protests from the medical community, the Council of States commission is discussing the maximum limit again. «We have heard the cry for help from the medical community; this absolutely must be looked at again,» said Swiss People's Party (SVP) Council of States member Hannes Germann to SRF in mid-August. Social Democrats (SP) Council of States member Baptiste Hurni sees the problem as being with the excessively high tariffs, not with the cap, he told SRF. He would therefore advocate in the commission for the cap to be introduced as planned in January, Hurni added.
