# Entry only for Black people: Theater director Matthias Lilienthal is banking on a shitstorm. Not for the first time

*Even before presenting a theater production, the designated artistic director of Berlin's Volksbühne has caused a scandal with his «Volksbad» (People's Bath). He is thus seeking attention.*

Anyone who brings theater maker Matthias Lilienthal to their city must sooner or later expect inconveniences. More often sooner than later. This happened ten years ago in Munich. And just recently in Berlin. Even before the 66-year-old took office as artistic director of the Volksbühne, he is already stirring up trouble here with his «Volksbad» project.

Lilienthal always begins new positions with a bang, one that initially resonates but yields no lasting benefit. He called an action in 2015 at the Münchner Kammerspiele «Shabbyshabby Apartments.» It was conceived as a protest against exorbitant rents and a lack of housing. Simple huts and shelters were made from scrap materials and then placed in prominent locations throughout the city, mostly where they caused disruption. One could rent these shabby accommodations for 35 euros a night.

Criticism poured in. This pompously staged debut was nothing more than an adventure playground for pseudo-intellectuals. They moved into the huts built by poorly paid artists and did not realize that they were behaving like precarious tourists: to feel like a homeless person in rough Munich for once! This action was sustainable in only one respect: it brought Matthias Lilienthal significant prominence.

## Expected shitstorm

Now, in front of the Berlin Volksbühne, where he will take over as director in the fall, Lilienthal has had a large swimming pool installed. Again, it was not intended for pure pleasure, but as an indictment of existing grievances: «Half of the indoor and outdoor pools are closed, schools are contaminated with asbestos, and trains and buses run irregularly,» Lilienthal said. «And so we thought we would help Governing Mayor Kai Wegner and supplement the offerings of the Berliner Bäderbetriebe with an additional ‹Volksbad.›»

But the wannabe lifeguard with the luxury salary did not stop there. When it became known that the «Volksbad» was to be reserved exclusively for Black people for a few hours on one day, the deliberate provocation promptly caused the next shitstorm. Imagine the absurdity: dark-skinned bathers eyed by white gawkers! However, the artistic director was not only accused of discrimination and the instrumentalization of minorities, but also of wasting subsidies. The bath costs 300,000 euros. Sources in the theater scene noted that a theater from the independent scene could manage an entire season with that much money.

Lilienthal's initial move backfired; he is being criticized everywhere in the German media. On the news channel Welt TV, for example, journalist Hans-Ulrich Jörges vehemently advocated for Lilienthal's dismissal even before a first theater production – because he had «exceeded the limits of what is acceptable in publicly funded cultural institutions.» The criticized director countered in his usual blunt manner: «The Volksbühne also understands theatrical events to include two people splashing each other.» Really? The statement diminishes anticipation for the upcoming season's schedule, as it sounds more like a children's birthday party than genuine stage art.

So what does this still have to do with theater? It should be Matthias Lilienthal's core task to revive the Volksbühne with theater starting in the fall, a venue where Erwin Piscator once caused a stir and Frank Castorf later irritated with controversies for years. But the Berlin native does not want to be confined to such mundane tasks. He sees his sphere of influence extending far beyond the stage. Cozy entertainment is as much a thorn in his side as bourgeois high culture. In the past, he often referred to Regietheater, which classically focuses on conveying text and literature, as «artistic crap.»

## The unfinished one

Matthias Lilienthal once began his theater career solidly as an assistant director at the Vienna Burgtheater. He was a dramaturg in Basel under Frank Baumbauer and was brought to the Berlin Volksbühne by Castorf. Until 1998, he already worked there as chief dramaturg and deputy artistic director. He later also organized the Theater der Welt festival in Mannheim, traveling to Beirut for months to work with young artists. Under his leadership, the Hebbel am Ufer (HAU) theater was named «Theater of the Year» in 2012.

Then came his artistic directorship in Munich in 2015, where he promoted experimental theater groups like She She Pop or Rimini Protokoll and young, innovative directors like Christopher Rüping at the Kammerspiele. After initial reservations, critics praised Lilienthal's commitment and daring. In Munich, however, the rebellious nature of the artistic director, who «crossed city theater with an international production house» («Theater heute»), was already viewed skeptically.

He left Munich in 2020 as the «Unfinished One,» as the «Süddeutsche Zeitung» wrote, when the artistic director no longer wanted to extend his contract. He was someone, the paper said, «who prefers to leave before arriving.» His critics welcomed the decision. Indeed, the CSU parliamentary group in the city council itself had voted against a contract extension. However, the artistic director had preempted them.

Matthias Lilienthal usually presents himself as a chummy nonconformist who wears his outfit like a uniform: wild hair, a somewhat bulky figure, jeans hanging at his knees. He is always quick to use the informal «Du» (German for 'you' in its familiar form) – especially when interacting with his critics, whom he quickly accuses of not understanding his concepts. In conversation, one usually feels little of the small bombs ticking in his head.

Lilienthal, however, is an old-school leftist, delighted by radical political goals – one who sees the country threatened by right-wing extremism and wants to counteract this development with art. «Theater,» he says, «is one of the social forms of expression in which politics is negotiated and, to a certain extent, also made. How we get along with each other must not only be negotiated but also tried out – in the reality that theater creates.»

## A kind of personality cult

Lilienthal had not expected to return to his old workplace, the Volksbühne – supported by event specialist Florentina Holzinger in the leadership team. «Not in my wildest dreams,» he once said in an interview. But the Volksbühne seems to fit his concept, he believed, because one cannot «muddle through with a few classics» here. In his first season, Rimini Protokoll will present a project about the turning point year of 1989, and Christopher Rüping will also direct here for the first time.

But the man who often moves quietly and charmingly in the background must constantly stage himself. Perhaps he will occasionally create a ski jump, a golf course, or a bouncy castle in front of the Volksbühne, because he needs the hype surrounding him. And he loves the role of the avenger who ostensibly champions the disenfranchised, but always co-opts them for his own glory. Thomas Bernhard always saw a theater maker in the role of a «trapper» – but the possibility that Matthias Lilienthal falls into his own trap is always present. And the joy over the greatest possible attention he has achieved with his paddling pool in Berlin might be short-lived.
