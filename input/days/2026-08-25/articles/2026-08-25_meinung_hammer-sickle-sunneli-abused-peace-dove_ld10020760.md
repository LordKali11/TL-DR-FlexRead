# Hammer, Sickle, Sünneli – The Abused Peace Dove

*Christoph Blocher is promoting his neutrality initiative with a snow-white dove of peace. But Russia has been using the dove for its own purposes for half a century – and this is evident in Blocher's following.*

The dove motif was a brilliant move. For once, the SVP is not campaigning with dark, grasping hands, boots, or black sheep, but with a snow-white peace dove inspired by Picasso. The message: Only perpetual, armed, and strict neutrality guarantees peace for Switzerland.

This, in turn, forces the opponents of Christoph Blocher’s neutrality initiative into imagery reminiscent of the SVP before it became a peace movement: soldier’s helmet and Swiss cross with a bullet hole. The SVP’s advertising is visually associated with peace, while the opponents’ advertising is associated with war and violence.

The iconography is entirely in line with Russia, which is not only following the campaign for a vote with great interest but also commenting on it. Maria Zakharova, the spokesperson for the Russian Foreign Ministry, recently informed Switzerland that it had abandoned its historic mediating role after adopting EU sanctions against Russia and organizing the Bürgenstock summit. The electorate now had the chance to correct this error, she added.

Christoph Blocher’s strongest argument is the promise of peace. It also convinces people who have little sympathy for Russia and its war policy. However, Blocher’s supporters also include circles that the old Soviet Union had already set its sights on: anti-American peace activists.

The connections to Moscow became visible as early as the early 1980s: The NATO Double-Track Decision of 1979 countered Soviet intermediate-range missiles with a buildup of American missiles. The peace movement feared a nuclear war on German soil and protested. Many demonstrated against both Russian and Western rearmament. But for some, American nuclear missiles were even more monstrous than Soviet ones – and for this group, Russian influencers had set their sights. The infiltration attempts are historically documented.

The World Peace Council was founded in 1949 with the explicit blessing of the Soviet leadership. Among its initiators was the Information Bureau of the Communist Workers' Parties, Kominform.

The founding also had an impact on Switzerland. Because the term «Friedensrat» (Peace Council) was already taken in Switzerland, the Swiss offshoot had to choose a new name and became the Swiss Peace Movement (SFB). And this very SFB is now found among the supporters of the neutrality initiative. It is part of a very broad and rather loose grouping that – driven by an aversion to the U.S. and NATO, as well as a certain admiration for Putin – stands behind the neutrality initiative.

This illustrious company includes, for example, Massimiliano Ay, the secretary of the Communist Party in Ticino. In April 2026, he participated in the founding of the socialist network Sowintern in Moscow. The event was initiated by the pro-Kremlin party Just Russia, and Vladimir Putin delivered the greeting message.

However, supporters include not only left-wingers. A closer look reveals former opponents of pandemic measures, former ambassadors, and radical Christians. But left-wingers serve as perfect argument amplifiers for the initiators: If even peace activists and left-wingers are in favor of rigid neutrality, then it can only be about peace.

This blurs the fact that the Greens and the SP are decidedly against the neutrality initiative and that the Swiss peace movement is divided. The Group for a Switzerland without an Army (GSoA), for example, rejects Blocher’s popular initiative without any ifs or buts.

Blocher is convinced that his dove secures peace. But he ignores that it increasingly stands for a peace to Putin’s liking. Since 2022, the German AfD has also positioned itself as a peace party. It created the slogan «Frieden schützen» (Protect Peace) and used the white peace dove as a motif for the European elections in 2024. The dove cannot defend itself.
