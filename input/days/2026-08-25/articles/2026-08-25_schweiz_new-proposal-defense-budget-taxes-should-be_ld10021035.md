# New proposal for the defense budget: Taxes should be increased less sharply, but for a longer period

*On Tuesday, Council of States finance politicians presented a new proposal for the army budget. If a tax increase fails, the problem would shift from the army to the federal budget.*

With little time remaining, Council of States security and finance politicians must prepare a politically and technically challenging proposal for the upcoming autumn session. At its core is the question of how the federal government should raise 24 billion Swiss francs for additional armaments spending in the coming years.

The Federal Council wants to temporarily increase the value-added tax by 0.5 percentage points for 12 years. This would cost consumers around 2 billion francs per year. The proposal is supported only by the Centre Party; the Swiss People's Party (SVP), The Liberals (FDP), and the Left oppose it.

Given this starting point, parliament's initial preliminary decision was surprising: The Council of States Finance Committee also backed a value-added tax increase on Tuesday. The committee said the increase should be smaller than proposed, at 0.2 instead of 0.5 percentage points. It noted that this would result in an additional burden of 0.8 billion francs. In return, the committee proposed extending the term to 17 years. Because value-added tax rates are enshrined in the constitution, a popular vote would necessarily take place, the committee added.

## Risk shifting from army to federal government

This is precisely where the Finance Committee's most important planned correction lies. Under the Federal Council's proposal, the army would receive a multi-billion-franc armaments fund to facilitate faster procurements. However, this special fund would be legally linked to the tax increase: If the popular vote rejects the higher taxes, the fund also collapses.

Bourgeois parties want to prevent this outcome, especially since many of their members doubt that higher levies for national defense can win majority popular support. The committee now presents a way to prevent the tax increase vote from becoming a fateful decision for the army.

For the fund to temporarily incur debt, it needs watertight financing. For the Federal Council, this financing consists of the tax increase. However, the Finance Committee now proposes instead to create a statutory earmarking at the expense of the federal treasury. The committee said the federal government would be obliged to transfer up to 1.3 billion francs annually to the fund. Ultimately, a similar amount of money would be raised with the longer term as with the Federal Council's proposal, it noted.

This fundamentally shifts the risks: If voters reject the tax increase, this is no longer primarily a problem for the new armaments fund and the army, but for the general federal budget. The federal budget, however, would still have to deposit the additional funds for armaments into the fund but would not receive higher revenues from the value-added tax. Pressure to save would likely increase again.

## Later vote

The committee also reached a second decision: It wants to use a portion of the budget underspendings that regularly occur in the administration for the fund as well. Currently, these funds flow into reducing the debt the federal government incurred during the Covid-19 pandemic. Repaying these legacy burdens would thus take longer. According to the committee, half of these unused appropriations should flow into armaments, which should amount to about 0.5 billion francs per year.

If the committee had its way, the popular vote on the tax increase would not take place until 2028. The Federal Council had aimed for a decision in 2027. The new proposal could combine the ballot with another issue: Currently, another fund — for the railway — receives one per mille from the value-added tax. However, this is also temporary, set to expire in 2030.

The Federal Council wants to extend that time limit. This decision could now be linked to the new — also temporary — increase of 0.2 points. This comes even at the risk that the numerous earmarking and time limits will make it difficult for anyone to keep track.

The Finance Committee also discussed proposals for extraordinary financing of armaments spending. Proponents argued that this would allow the 24 billion francs to be channeled past the debt brake entirely or in part. However, these proposals clearly failed.

Next, the Council of States Security Committee will take up the issue.
