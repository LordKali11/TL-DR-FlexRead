# Washington's increased pressure – Tehran unimpressed

*Iran's economic pressure and Tehran's calm reaction to new sanctions against the Islamic Republic and its trading partners*

Just minutes after U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced new sanctions against Iran and its economic partners in Washington on Monday, Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammed Bagher Ghalibaf spoke out. «The Americans know that no one takes their grandiloquent threats seriously,» Ghalibaf wrote on the social media platform X. «The United States is not economically capable of further restricting Iran's relations with other countries.»

With this, Ghalibaf indirectly pointed to the greatest challenge for the American government: to cut Iran off from its main foreign sources of income without simultaneously provoking a new economic confrontation with China. The People's Republic is by far the most important buyer of Iranian oil and does not want to be deterred from this in the future. «China's cooperation with Iran is taking place within the framework of international law and should not be hampered or disrupted,» said Lin Jian, a spokesperson for the Foreign Ministry, on Tuesday.

## U.S. efforts for Iran's economic isolation

If it were up to the American government, officials said, all countries should cut their economic ties with Iran. Financial institutions that supported Iran in processing or concealing its financial flows faced exclusion from the American financial system, Bessent announced. Last week, U.S. President Donald Trump had spoken of «measures of historic proportions.» However, the steps presented on Monday fell short of this announcement.

Countries and companies that continued to do business with Iran would have to expect American sanctions, Bessent said, without specifying them further. In recent times, in addition to China, the United Arab Emirates, Iraq, and Turkey were among Iran's most important economic partners. However, the Emirates suspended all trade with Iran a week ago.

It remains unclear when Washington will actually apply the threatened secondary sanctions against Iran's foreign trading partners. However, it is clear that Chinese President Xi Jinping will visit the U.S. at the end of September. This is likely to limit Washington's willingness to impose sanctions on major Chinese banks or important companies precisely now.

## Oil exports to China as Tehran's foreign currency source

Due to its oil imports, China is the lifeline for the Iranian regime. The revenues from exports provide Tehran with important foreign currency, with which the state can continue to finance its institutions and extensive security apparatus – even if revenues have fallen sharply as a result of the war and the blockade. Even before the start of the war against Iran, the Islamic Republic's economy was severely weakened. Since then, the situation has worsened further.

For example, the Iranian currency is in free fall. The entire population felt the consequences of inflation, conceded Mohammed Jafar Ghaempanah, Iran's Vice President for Executive Affairs, according to the semi-official news agency Isna on Monday. Previously, the Iranian rial had fallen to a record low of 2.02 million rials per dollar. Already on Friday, President Masud Pezeshkian had stated, with regard to the economy, that the naval blockade made trade difficult and that the government no longer had sufficient funds to meet all its financial obligations.

Nevertheless, there are currently few signs that Tehran wants to give in to American pressure. Even before the announcement of the new sanctions, Tehran had threatened a possible military response. It also announced that it would further restrict oil exports from the Persian Gulf if the U.S. imposed further economic measures against Iran. Mohsen Rezai, head of the Supreme National Security Council, stated over the weekend that any country participating in the U.S.-led economic blockade must expect an Iranian reaction.

## Tehran's steadfast course

«Even if the sanctions pressure is further increased, a short-term change in Iran's strategy in the war is unlikely,» says Iran expert Ali Fathollah-Nejad in response to an NZZ inquiry. However, in the medium or long term, targeted sanctions against the regime could pave the way for Iran to re-evaluate its strategic calculus and develop a greater willingness to reach understanding and concessions, he adds. «Iran's severe economic crisis will play an important role in this, as the regime fears a renewed flare-up of street protests by autumn,» says Fathollah-Nejad.

The U.S.'s idea that economic rather than military warfare will move Iran to concessions in the short term is assessed by the Iran expert as grandiloquent rhetoric. Danny Citrinowicz assessed the announcement of the new sanctions similarly. «If Washington continues to believe that sufficient economic pressure will eventually force Iran to capitulate, then it is no longer just a misjudgment,» the former head of the Iran department of Israeli military intelligence wrote on X on Monday. «It demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of the adversary.» The «economic D-Day» that Bessent announced for Iran on Monday is likely to be some time coming.
