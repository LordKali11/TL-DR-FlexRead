# German government cabinet retreat – economy demands «Schröderize» courage

*The political summer break is over, and now the «autumn of reforms» is supposed to begin. But the problems are already so great that these ambitions could fail.*

Probably the best news reached Friedrich Merz even before the start of the two-day cabinet meeting. On Tuesday morning, the Federal Statistical Office reported that the German economy had grown more strongly in the second quarter than expected. That was exactly the signal the Federal Chancellor also wanted to send with the meeting at Neuhardenberg Castle in Brandenburg: Germany’s economy was recovering, and the federal government was strengthening competitiveness and innovation, and securing prosperity and jobs.

These topics will also be the focus of the meeting of the coalition partners from the CDU, CSU, and SPD. To address the urgency of the situation – Germany has seen no significant economic growth for seven years – high-ranking business representatives will also participate in the cabinet meeting alongside the ministers, and they are expected to provide their own insights with guest lectures and discussions.

## Business representatives' call for «autumn of action»

Merz set the direction in a short speech before the cabinet meeting. He wanted to «hear from practitioners how they view the current situation,» because the population rightly expected «the federal government to act,» the Federal Chancellor said, adding: «We are acting.» Everything the coalition still planned to do over the course of the year would be prepared at the cabinet meeting, Merz said.

With the end of the parliamentary summer break, the much-invoked «autumn of reforms» is now set to begin. Although Federal Chancellor Merz announced this a year ago, what followed were merely minor adjustments. To ensure it works this time – for example, with a reform of pensions, the healthcare system, or taxes – various economic actors had previously exerted pressure. These actors agreed that things could not continue as before.

For example, Marie-Christine Ostermann, President of Family Businesses, said that the federal government had to present a «coherent growth strategy so that companies would believe in the future of Germany as a business location and invest again.» She believed Merz had to transform from a foreign policy chancellor to an economic chancellor. Tanja Gönner of the Federation of German Industries also said that the cabinet meeting had to be the starting point for an «autumn of action.»

And Wolfgang Grosse Entrup, CEO of the German Chemical Industry Association, said that Germany needed «the courage to Schröderize.» He added that the federal government had to show that it had understood the seriousness of the situation. Grosse Entrup therefore called for an «Agenda 2030,» echoing Agenda 2010 under the then-Social Democratic Federal Chancellor Gerhard Schröder. Neuhardenberg Castle provides at least symbolic support for this: in 2003, the Schröder government also met here.

## Summer break: Rarely a break

However, it will not only be about economic issues. According to government circles, the meeting was also intended to provide an opportunity to work together intensively in a new government constellation and in one place, thereby building new trust. This is because the political summer break hardly lived up to its name. After the then-parliamentary group leader of the CDU/CSU, Jens Spahn, resigned from his post due to his decision on surrogacy, Federal Chancellor Merz rigorously reshuffled his cabinet. This led to great criticism and internal party disputes, such as the CDU/CSU had rarely seen. The already tarnished authority of the Federal Chancellor eroded further.

A few days later, there was another stress test: The CDU state premiers Michael Kretschmer from Saxony, Sven Schulze from Saxony-Anhalt, and Mario Voigt from Thuringia opposed their own party and spoke out in a joint paper against abolishing the pension at 63. However, Merz had precisely decided this with Social Democratic Labor Minister Bärbel Bas a few weeks earlier and announced that the federal government intended to implement all proposals developed by a pension commission. The debate about the pension at 63, which is now primarily driven by Social Democrats, continues to simmer and is likely to be discussed at Neuhardenberg Castle.

Further issues that required urgent action during the political summer break included security and climate protection. This is because a drone loaded with military explosives was found at Leipzig Airport, which prompted discussions about Germany’s defensibility and the protection of critical infrastructure. In addition, the drought period led to historically low water levels in important rivers, posing high risks for the entire national economy. The federal government also wants to address these problems at the cabinet meeting. Merz said before the meeting that he wanted to derive «conclusions with concrete decisions» from the heatwave.

A topic that is not on the official agenda, but which the ministers are likely to discuss intensively, are the elections in Saxony-Anhalt, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, and Berlin, which are due in a few weeks. The election of the state parliament of Saxony-Anhalt on Sept. 6, in particular, has preoccupied Germany for months. This is because there, the AfD could win an absolute majority and for the first time provide the state premier.

At the concluding press conference on Wednesday afternoon, Federal Chancellor Merz is therefore likely to do everything he can to signal political determination.
