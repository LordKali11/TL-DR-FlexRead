## Numerous deaths in Nepal flash flood; further floods feared

![](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2026/08/27/52fd1516-f1ea-4f97-835f-f83ee16a7a9d.webp?width=1024)
** (Niranjan Shrestha / AP)

**What happened:** At least 359 people died in the devastating flash flood in the border region between Nepal and China. More than 1,300 remain missing. One Swiss national was rescued. Chinese authorities fear further floods. Chinese state television, citing the Ministry of Water Resources, reported that a dammed lake formed at a river mouth on the Nepalese side. Sources said there was a risk of a dam breach. For the latest developments

**The background:** The flash flood was possibly triggered by a glacier collapse. The fall of ice masses from a great height could have transformed them into water. However, a glacial lake outburst is also possible. In the Himalayas, there are several glacial lakes that must be closely monitored because they can easily breach. The flash flood buried entire settlements over a distance of more than 60 kilometers. For many residents, there was no escape. For background

**The Gyirong border crossing:** The flash flood also destroyed an important border crossing between China and Nepal. It is Beijing's strategic corridor through the Himalayas. A flash flood destroyed the bridge in 2025. Why do Nepal and China nevertheless continue to rely on this dangerous trade route? To the background

## Srebrenica massacre: Death of war criminal Ratko Mladic

**What happened:** Former Bosnian Serb military leader Ratko Mladic died at age 84 in The Hague. Mladic was sentenced to life imprisonment for war crimes and served his sentence in the international prison in The Hague. For the report

**A look back:** Mladic had, among other things, ordered the massacre of more than 8,000 people – mainly men and young men – in the eastern Bosnian enclave of Srebrenica in July 1995. In 2017, he was convicted by the Hague War Crimes Tribunal.
