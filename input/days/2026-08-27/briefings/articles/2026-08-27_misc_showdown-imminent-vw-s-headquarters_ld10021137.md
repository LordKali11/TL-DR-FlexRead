**What happened:** In the dispute over new savings measures at VW, the works council invited employees to extraordinary company meetings at all major locations. Representatives from the management board are expected today in Chemnitz and Dresden. Next week, CEO Oliver Blume will once again ask the supervisory board for approval of his savings plans. However, the chances of success appear slim. To the report

**Why it matters:** Blume already promised investors a radical restructuring of his company at the beginning of the year, which is intended to make the company competitive again. However, the concrete implementation remains open. Measures such as the reduction of 50,000 additional jobs or the closure of four plants in Germany, which according to media reports, the company's management is examining, are controversial.

**The background:** Legally, there are scenarios in which the management board could circumvent the supervisory board's decision-making process. However, these would probably fail due to resistance from Lower Saxony. At VW, there is only one way to implement the savings plan: the management board must seek compromises within the existing power structure.

**What happened:** Meta reached an out-of-court settlement with a group of plaintiffs to avoid paying a fine of around 18 billion dollars. The plaintiffs had accused the social media company in federal court of making children and adolescents addicted to its products, Instagram and Facebook, and psychologically harming them. For the first time, Meta committed to a whole series of child protection measures. To the report

**Why it matters:** Meta is introducing a daily time limit of two hours for children and adolescents under 18. Every 30 minutes, minors also receive a «prompt» designed to encourage more conscious use of social media. Access to Instagram and Facebook is also to be blocked overnight. Automatic video playback will be able to be switched off. And Meta said certain filters that allow children to «extremely» alter their appearance will no longer be available in the future.

**The background:** According to Meta, the new regulations will only come into force in those states that have signed the settlement. For children and adolescents living outside the U.S., nothing will change for now — although the solution found could also serve as a template in Europe. In April, the European Commission criticized Meta for neglecting child protection and threatened the U.S. company with a fine.
