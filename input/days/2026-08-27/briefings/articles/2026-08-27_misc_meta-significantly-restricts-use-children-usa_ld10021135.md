# Meta significantly restricts its use for children in the USA

**What happened:** Meta reached an out-of-court settlement with a group of plaintiffs to avoid paying a fine of around 18 billion dollars. These plaintiffs had accused the social media company of addicting children and adolescents with its Instagram and Facebook products and psychologically harming them before the Federal Supreme Court. For the first time, Meta committed to a whole series of child protection measures. To the report

**Why it matters: **Meta introduced a two-hour daily time limit for children and adolescents under 18 years of age. Every 30 minutes, minors also received a «prompt» designed to encourage more conscious use of social media. Access to Instagram and Facebook was also blocked overnight. Automatic video playback could be switched off. And certain filters that allowed children to «extremely» change their appearance would no longer be available in the future, Meta said.

**The background:** According to Meta, the new regulations would only come into force in those states that had signed the settlement. For children and adolescents who lived outside the U.S., nothing would change for now — although the solution found could also serve as a model in Europe. In April, the European Commission criticized that Meta had been neglecting child protection and threatened the U.S. company with a fine.

**What happened:** E-scooters had shaped Zurich's cityscape for almost ten years. But they were to be reined in. On Wednesday evening, the City Parliament discussed and approved a package of measures from Green Security Director Karin Rykart. What influence the newly decided regulations would have remained to be seen. Rykart, in any case, showed herself firmly determined to curb the «wild growth» of e-scooters. To the report

**Why it matters: **The city had over 7,000 rental vehicles at the time. Two-thirds of them were e-scooters, and the rest were e-bikes and e-mopeds. The supply was to be drastically reduced to 3,000 vehicles. In addition, rental vehicles would no longer be allowed to be parked just anywhere in the city center; they had to be deposited at permanently installed stations. The same applied in other «heavily used areas of the city,» for example at train stations.
