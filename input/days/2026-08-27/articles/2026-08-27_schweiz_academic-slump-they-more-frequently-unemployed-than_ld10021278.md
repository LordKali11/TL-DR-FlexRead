# The academic slump: They are more frequently unemployed than the average Swiss, often only temporarily employed – and in part-time roles at that

*New figures from the Federal Statistical Office paint a bleak picture, especially for university graduates. Is a degree still worth it?*

Is a university degree in Switzerland still the royal road, as is always touted in compulsory schooling?

Between 2000 and 2020, the number of students doubled. And baccalaureate rates remained high thereafter – especially in urban cantons. In Geneva, for example, only 15% of young people began a vocational apprenticeship this year. Yet the figures have long shown: Those who study no longer receive – as was undoubtedly the case in the past – a life of luxury. Job security, good pay, high prestige.

Much is changing in the labor market. The unemployment rate for academics has risen to over 2% – and has already aligned with that of non-academics. And for those who have only recently completed their studies, the outlook is even less rosy. New figures from the Federal Statistical Office show this.

Between 2023 and 2025, the unemployment rate for university graduates one year after graduation rose significantly. For master's graduates at universities, it rose from 3.9% to 6.4% – and for bachelor's graduates, from 3.4% to 4.9%. These are higher values than for the general population, whose unemployment rate stands at 4.8%.

## Not just niche subjects affected

There are significant differences between fields of study. Those who study music, theater, and other arts at a university of applied sciences – or have specialized in design or applied linguistics – face difficulties. One in ten does not find a job afterward. At universities, it is primarily those from the subject group «interdisciplinary and other» who do not find a job (8.6%). This includes niche subjects such as Urban Studies. In the humanities and social sciences, the unemployment rate has generally risen from 5.1 to 8.5%.

In comparison, classic subjects continue to show the lowest unemployment rates: Those who pursue a master's in medicine or pharmacy at a university are almost guaranteed a job (1.6%). Bachelor's graduates from universities of applied sciences in the fields of health and social work are also in high demand. Only 1.7 and 1.5%, respectively, are without a job one year after graduation.

However, the downturn in the labor market does not only affect niche subjects. The unemployment rate in economics at universities has more than doubled in just two years – from 3.5 to 8.5%. Even a bachelor's degree in IT from a university of applied sciences no longer guarantees a job: The unemployment rate rose from 3% to 6.4%.

In addition, almost half of university graduates are only temporarily employed one year after graduation. Naturally, medical professionals, who work in various hospitals for their specialist titles, contribute to this. However, this alone does not explain the high number. Almost half of humanities and social science graduates also have temporary contracts. One-third of university graduates even hold intern status. In comparison: For graduates of universities of applied sciences, practically none hold intern status. On the other hand, they have – compared to university graduates – enabled more people to attain leadership positions.

However, unemployment – or temporary jobs – is not the only reason that university academics are no longer «masters of the universe» and their living conditions are becoming increasingly precarious. Part-time work also plays a significant role: In the humanities and social sciences, it accounts for almost 60%, and in interdisciplinary fields of study, it is over half. These two groups also most frequently report being «underemployed» one year after graduation. In comparison: For graduates from other faculties, less than a quarter work part-time.

## Graduates of universities of applied sciences earn more than university graduates

Overall, almost half of university graduates state that they have no opportunity or offer for full-time employment. At universities of applied sciences, it is about one-third. However, in those cases, it is a personal decision. And only in certain areas. More than 70% of those who have studied music, theater, or social work work less than full-time. In construction and planning, IT, or economics, however, significantly fewer than 20% are not employed full-time. Across all higher education institutions, women are significantly overrepresented in part-time work.

This also affects salaries: The median salary of bachelor's graduates from universities of applied sciences is higher than that of master's graduates from universities. And only at universities are there graduates who earn less than 50,000 francs. Besides lawyers in legal traineeships, this again affects humanities and social science graduates.

The cause of the academic misery is clear. Companies are currently hiring fewer entry-level employees. On the one hand, because they are reacting to economic and geopolitical crises. On the other hand, because they want to wait and see how AI will affect the labor market. In short: which jobs will even be needed in the near future. The economist Mathias Binswanger has long said: It will primarily affect academics who hold so-called bullshit jobs that constitute pure administrative self-employment.

In addition, the federal government and cantons must save money. The public sector, a paradise for many university graduates, offers fewer entry-level jobs than it used to. This is now becoming noticeable.

The land of milk and honey has also closed its doors in Switzerland.
