> On Wednesday, a massive flood wave devastates the border region between Nepal and China. Water, mud, debris and ice sweep away houses, roads and people.
>
> The disaster originates here: on the more than 7,200-meter-high Langtang Lirung in the Himalayas. In the morning, a large part of a glacier breaks off there. Around 8:37 a.m., monitoring stations register the seismic signal of the collapse.
>
> These satellite images show the break-off point at an altitude of approximately 5,200 meters. The ice and rock masses plunge about 1,200 vertical meters into the valley. In doing so, they carry further rock and sediment with them. The avalanche hits the Lhende Khola, a tributary of the Bhote Koshi.
>
> Nepalese hydrologists suspect, based on satellite images, that the material temporarily blocked the Lhende Khola. Behind this natural barrier, water accumulated. When it gave way, a debris-laden flood wave shot downstream. Scientists are currently investigating whether the disaster actually unfolded this way.
>
> Around 8:45 a.m., the flood reaches the Gyirong/Rasuwagadhi border crossing between China and Nepal. This surveillance camera footage shows the moment the flood hits the border complex.
>
> From here, the flood rushes further down the Bhote Koshi. Syabrubesi lies about 14 kilometers further south. This video shows the destruction of the village during a flyover on Wednesday.
>
> The Bhote Koshi continues downstream into the Trishuli. Trishuli Bazar lies more than 35 kilometers further south. Here too, the masses of water still possess enormous power. These images from the city show this. Drone footage along the river reveals the extent of the devastation.
>
> The flood continues to move south via the Trishuli. Around 1 p.m., it already passes Mugling. About two hours later, the flood reaches the Narayani near Devghat. There, the water level reaches its peak around 4 p.m. Afterwards, the extraordinary flood wave gradually weakens.
>
> The water flows further in the Narayani towards India. There, one day after the flood, three bodies are recovered from the water in the Maharajganj district. Barely a day and a half after the disaster, more than 350 people are already dead. In Nepal and Tibet, more than 1,300 people are still considered missing.
