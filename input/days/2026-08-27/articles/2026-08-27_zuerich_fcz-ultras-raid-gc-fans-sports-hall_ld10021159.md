# FCZ ultras raid GC fans in a sports hall. They feel safe in the anonymous crowd — but then a candy becomes the undoing of a ringleader

*Two men have been convicted by the court. They are paying a heavy price for attempting to steal a banner from the supporters of their rival city team.*

On a Wednesday evening in autumn 2024, shortly before 10 p.m., more than two dozen cars pulled up simultaneously in front of the sports hall of Rickenbach secondary school, a small village outside Winterthur with fewer than 3,000 inhabitants. About 60 men sat in the vehicles. They wore sneakers and black clothing, and masked themselves as they got out. They carried batons, knives, and pepper spray.

Inside the sports hall were nine GC ultras. They were innocently working on a banner they intended to display at the derby against rival FCZ in three days. Outside the hall, the attackers left the parking lot. One of them carried a hammer. He walked ahead and smashed the glass pane of the entrance door. Then the men stormed the building. «They were like predators that pounced on their prey out of nowhere and with all their might,» the prosecutor later said.

## A long-planned attack

The outcome of the attack: a broken glass door, several minor injuries, FCZ graffiti on the school grounds and at the train station. And a stolen GC banner, which the FCZ ultras burned a few days later during the derby. It was an action intended to humiliate the GC fans.

The surprise attack was probably planned long in advance. It was a reckoning in the football fan scene, rarely seen before in Switzerland.

Now, about two years later, the case was heard before the Winterthur District Court. It involved serious criminal offenses such as robbery, and the accused faced harsh penalties — for the attack and for a stolen piece of fabric that objectively had no value whatsoever.

The attack in the Rickenbach sports hall was documented by recordings from permanently installed video cameras there. But law enforcement authorities faced the familiar problem: how to prove individual guilt when a group appears masked? In this case, they had a lead. Because one person from the black-clad crowd dropped a small yellow object: a pack of Ricola cough drops.

The police found DNA traces on the cough drop package. They led to a man well-known in the fan scene. Video footage showed that he was presumably the ultra who smashed the entrance door. Authorities were able to identify a second accused based on his clothing. According to the prosecutor, he stood guard and monitored the operation as a «person of respect» among the fans.

One piece of evidence that incriminated both accused: they switched off their cell phones almost simultaneously and at the same location in the city of Zurich on the night of the crime — and reactivated them about an hour and a half later, as they approached the city from the north. From the prosecutor's perspective, this gap in cell phone reception perfectly matched the crime.

## Accused's claims of politically motivated proceedings

At the trial, the prosecutor accused the two men of massive criminal energy. The mob had been driven by a «purely anti-GC-oriented emotional impulse» and a «desire for violence,» he said. Only by luck had no serious injuries occurred, he added, and thanks to the intervention of a resident. This resident had then also been attacked, he said: he had defended himself with a punch against an attacker, whereupon another had attacked him with pepper spray.

It was striking that the two accused were by no means adolescents acting recklessly. The man whose DNA trace was discovered on the Ricola package is 28 years old, a trained gardener, and now works as a road builder. The authorities probably identified him based on his previous convictions: in 2020, he was sentenced to 24 months in prison for attempted grievous bodily harm in connection with a fan altercation.

The second accused is a university employee and eight years older. He also has previous convictions for breach of the peace and criminal trespass. The professional background made the defendants' actions even more incomprehensible, the prosecutor said at the trial: they were «educated Swiss citizens.»

The two men remained silent on the accusations during the trial before the District Court. The university employee even answered the question about siblings or his wife with the same sentence: «I refuse to testify.» The road builder said nothing about the matter but readily spoke of his career plans and his recent marriage proposal to his fiancée — obviously with the motive of averting a looming prison sentence.

The road builder's defense counsel attempted to invalidate the DNA evidence. Her client had moved in fan circles at the time, she argued, and the DNA could also have gotten onto the Ricola package in other ways and been lost at the crime scene by a third party. And there were many reasons for turning off a cell phone, she maintained, for example, that one wanted peace and quiet.

One had to «keep things in perspective,» the lawyer found: the material damage from the attack was limited, and there had been no injuries — contrary to what the prosecutor's office stated in its indictment. It was not even certain that it was a robbery, she added: it was not visible in the video footage whether a GC banner was actually being carried away.

The university employee's defense counsel said the accusations were fabricated. The authorities relied on «alleged scene experts» who had «allegedly identified» his client, he contended. And the clothes found by the police during a house search half a year after the crime could also not be clearly assigned to the video footage, he added. The shoes — black Reebok Classic Leather — were moreover a mass-produced item popular in football circles, he argued.

At the end of the hearing, the university employee finally spoke. He presented his own interpretation of events. The trial was politically motivated by the Security Directorate of cantonal government member Mario Fehr (independent), he claimed. Fehr had announced a tough crackdown after the sports hall raid, he said, so actions now obviously had to follow. And the fan sections were a thorn in the side of «bourgeois society,» he contended, because «solidarity instead of competition» was lived there. Fehr therefore wanted to set a «repressive example,» he concluded.

## Court without doubts

The District Court in Winterthur, however, paid little heed to this. It convicted the two men of robbery, breach of the peace, criminal trespass, and property damage, sentencing them to conditional prison sentences of 22 and 20 months, respectively. In addition, there were fines amounting to 180 daily rates of 50 Swiss francs. In the case of the road builder, the sentence was unconditional; in the case of the university employee, it was conditional.

At the sentencing, the judge said: «There were not the slightest doubts that FCZ fans had been at work to steal the banner from the sports hall.» The perpetrators had acted with quite high criminal energy, he added.

The judge said they had also had no doubts that the two men had been present during the storming of the sports hall. The prosecutor's office had conclusively presented the chain of circumstantial evidence, he noted. There had been the Ricola package, he explained. Only the DNA of the road builder had been found on it, he stated. «You had not provided an alternative explanation,» he told them, «why only your trace had been found on the package.» The same applied to the switched-off cell phones, clothes, and shoes, he added. The court credited the road builder for making an effort to lead a life that was going in a different direction.

The men must also pay for the damage caused at the school in Rickenbach and for the costs of the proceedings. The verdict is not yet final. The two FCZ fans can still appeal against it.

_Judgments DG 250 045 and DG 250 046 of Aug. 27, 2026, not yet final._
