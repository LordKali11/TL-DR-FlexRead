# Flash flood hits Beijing's strategic corridor through the Himalayas

*A natural disaster has destroyed a key border crossing between China and Nepal. This happened once before, a year ago. Why do the two countries cling to such a dangerous trade route?*

The Gyirong border crossing is the most important trade route between China and Nepal. In 2024, almost 30% of bilateral trade flowed through that Himalayan valley, according to the Tibetan regional government. Now everything lies buried under mud and rubble. On Wednesday, surveillance camera footage showed how the gray border building, trucks and people in the forecourt were swallowed by a flash flood within seconds. Later, social media channels showed the flood continuing its path through the valley. Hundreds of people have died, and more than a thousand are considered missing.

It is not the first time such a natural disaster has occurred at the Gyirong border crossing. In July 2025, a flash flood destroyed the border bridge. The crossing remained closed until January 2026. Why do China and Nepal nevertheless cling to such a dangerous trade route?

## Beijing's goal: Tibet as South Asia gateway

China reacted quickly and at the highest level to the disaster. Party and state leader Xi Jinping issued «important instructions,» Premier Li Qiang also commented, a national emergency response of the second-highest level was triggered, and a Politburo member traveled to the disaster area.

The Gyirong border crossing has been of strategic importance to Beijing for years. The route is one of the few possible north-south connections through the Himalayas, a centuries-old trade route. Now it is part of Xi Jinping's «New Silk Road» and serves the goal of making Tibet a hub for trade with South Asia. China's central government is building roads, customs and logistics buildings, and is even examining a train connection between the Tibetan region of Shigatse via Gyirong to Kathmandu.

But not only China has an interest in developing this trade route as effectively as possible; Nepal does too. The country wants to reduce its dependence on India through more trade with China. At the same time, Nepal urgently needs modern infrastructure such as tunnels and railways to boost its economy. Kathmandu hopes for Chinese investments as part of the «New Silk Road.»

Trade via Gyirong primarily consists of Chinese exports; Nepal sells relatively little to China. The customs data for the delivery of Chinese electric and hybrid vehicles, for example, is impressive. In 2020, only 39 were exported via the border crossing; in 2025, according to local government figures, there were already 4,126 in the first half of the year before the flood destroyed the bridge.

## China's dominance in the trade relationship

Yet, while trade relations are becoming increasingly close, there are also tensions in the border region. Last year's flash flood was triggered by the emptying of a glacial lake in Tibet. The Nepalese disaster management agency discovered this using satellite data. The Nepalese side told the «Kathmandu Post» at the time that China had not warned them. There was no such mechanism, they said.

Although there has been an agreement since 2019 to reduce the risk of natural disasters and to cooperate better in emergencies, there appears to be a lack of implementation. Experts have therefore been calling for real-time data exchange and joint early warning systems for years. China already systematically exchanges hydrological data with the Mekong states. Following the current disaster, Chinese and Nepalese authorities spoke via video conference Wednesday. China subsequently promised to inform Nepal in the future as soon as the water level on the Chinese side rises.

China dominates its small southern neighbor. The Chinese side is teeming with surveillance cameras and border officials – the Nepalese side is less well-equipped, which gives China the power to unilaterally enforce border closures. In addition, residents of the border region used to often communicate in Tibetan. However, residents interviewed for a 2023 Nepalese study said that Chinese border officials are now predominantly Han Chinese who do not speak Tibetan. This leads to communication problems, they added.

## China's paramount interest: Preventing separatism

China's rigid controls also cause friction. This has historical reasons. China's People's Liberation Army marched into Tibet in 1950; the following year, Tibet came under the control of the People's Republic. From the early 1960s, some Tibetan resistance fighters maintained a guerrilla base in Nepalese Mustang. The CIA supported them with weapons, money and training.

Beijing fears to this day that Tibetan independence aspirations could seep into the country from Nepal. The veneration of the Dalai Lama, who has lived in Indian exile since 1959, appears particularly dangerous to the Chinese authorities. Border controls to Tibet are therefore rigorous – foreigners require special permission to enter. Nepalese border residents report strict controls at the border with China. Their phones are searched, and if an image of the Dalai Lama is found, entry is denied, they say.
