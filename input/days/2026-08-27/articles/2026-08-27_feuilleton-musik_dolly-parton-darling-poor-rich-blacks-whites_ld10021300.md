The corresponding bill had been introduced by representatives from both parties. However, it failed due to the artist’s own objection. The episode, however, is symptomatic of the phenomenal country singer’s popularity; she died on Tuesday at the age of eighty. «Dolly» had long been the nation’s «darling» – from rural Tennessee, where the singer was born in 1946, to the depths of New York’s high society. «Is there anything we can all agree on?» asked the «New York Times» in a 2021 headline, and answered itself: «Yes: Dolly Parton.»

## No Politics

In light of her death, all Americans profess to be Dolly Parton fans. Even President Donald Trump, who had the American flag lowered to half-mast in mourning for the country singer. One might wonder whether Dolly Parton herself, given her incomparable popularity, might not have been a viable candidate for the presidency.

What a wonderful image, the diva with the touching girlish voice, the blond hairstyle, the large bust, and the even larger soul as a representative of her country! Given the intelligence she displayed not only as an artist but also as a producer, entrepreneur, and quick-witted media star, she could have been entrusted with the task. However, she deliberately stayed out of party politics. She liked to express herself on political issues in her songs or in the media, but not on politicians.

## Out of Poverty

The prerequisite for Dolly Parton’s broad appeal was initially her talent. Without her warbling soprano and bubbling creativity, the country singer would not have succeeded in Nashville’s competitive music scene. Her talents also fostered the feminist self-confidence with which she emancipated herself from her patron, Porter Wagoner, at the right time. In 1973, she bid farewell to the mentor whose TV show she had joined in 1967 by writing him a friendly farewell song: «I Will Always Love You.»

Afterwards, the singer landed hit after hit. Success slowly carried Dolly Parton, who had grown up in extreme poverty, up the ladder into American high society. Her home remained the rural society of Tennessee, but thanks to 100 million albums sold, she soon counted among the super-rich. «To look as cheap as she did, it would take a lot of money,» the artist quipped, who never made a secret of her cosmetic surgeries. In fact, however, she gained sympathy everywhere because she philanthropically invested her capital – for example, in the Imagination Library, a project that provided children with free books, or in the development of a COVID vaccine.

Born in the Appalachian Mountains, deep in the rural music scene’s hinterland, the singer grew up, in a sense, into the pure culture of country music. But her expressiveness was already nourished by contrasting sources. She learned to sing through the hymns sung in her grandfather’s church services. Her uncle, however, taught her to play the guitar. Thus, profane and religious motifs blended in her music even before she moved to Nashville at the age of seventeen.

Anyone as deeply rooted in a tradition as Dolly Parton can afford stylistic excursions. In Nashville, she became acquainted with a more modern, opulent country style. Later, with songs like «Here You Come Again» (1977), she went straight into pop. This flexibility, however, not only brought her additional income but also enabled her to connect with different milieus and generations. Her country repertoire continued to prove itself a musical home for an audience for whom pop was too fleeting and superficial. Her forays into contemporary song productions, however, kept her relevant.

## The Pillar Saint

In her lyrics, Dolly Parton championed women and showed solidarity with minorities: country music is considered the music of white people in the USA. Dolly Parton, however, welcomed it when her songs were covered by Black female artists. Whitney Houston’s version of «I Will Always Love You» became a global hit. In 2024, Parton herself re-recorded «Jolene» with Beyoncé. She also became an idol for the African American population by supporting the «Black Lives Matter» movement: «Of course Black lives matter; do we think our little white asses are the only ones that count?» she said in a 2020 interview.

Dolly Parton was thus an idol of country music; nevertheless, she led an artistic life beyond stylistic, social, or political constraints. Familiar with the poor and the rich, with whites and Blacks, with rural farmers and city dwellers, she lived out her Christian faith in worldly reality. She embodied, as it were, old Christian values – and represented democratic ideas regarding homosexuality and queerness.

Perhaps the shared grief for Dolly Parton will once again provide unity between Democrats and Republicans. Perhaps they can now finally erect a monument to the pillar saint of American patriotism. Preferably right in front of the White House.
