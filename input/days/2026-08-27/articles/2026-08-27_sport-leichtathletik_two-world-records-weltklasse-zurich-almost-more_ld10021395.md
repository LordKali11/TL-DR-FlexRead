Track and field witnessed a golden hour on an August evening in Zurich: two world records in one evening, which is an absolute exception in this sport, where all performances have long since been pushed to their limits. Zurich, however, once before experienced three world records, but that was a long time ago.

In 1997, three exceptional runners made the stadium tremble: Kenyan Wilson Boit Kipketer set a new best mark in the 3000-meter steeplechase, Ethiopian Haile Gebrselassie did the same in the 5000-meter, and Kenyan Wilson Kipketer, competing for Denmark, broke the then 16-year-old 800-meter record of Briton Sebastian Coe.

Zurich was long considered the true world record meeting, but then the momentum broke: Yelena Isinbayeva's pole vault of 5.06 meters was the 25th world record at the event, set in 2009. After that, for more than 17 years, there were always great performances, but nothing that could be entered into the record books.

This had several causes, but was often simply due to the weather. Zurich in late August is often a cool, rainy place. In addition, Weltklasse is regularly the final meeting of the Diamond League. It is primarily about victory and money, not about times, distances, or heights.

In 2026, everything aligned for once. Conditions were ideal – it was warm, dry, and windless. Furthermore, the overseas athletes had a competition break during the European Championships, which they used to push their form to a peak once again. They rewarded the fans with world records number 26 and 27 at Letzigrund Stadium.

Just a little more was needed, and there would have been a third world record. In the men's pole vault, the audience saw a competition like never before. Greek Emmanouil Karalis's 6.20 meters was only enough for second place, Armand Duplantis won with 6.25 meters – and he narrowly failed three times to break the world record of 6.32 meters.

## Long Hurdles: Exceptional Discipline

The first record of the evening came almost out of nowhere. Brazilian Alison dos Santos ran the 400-meter hurdles as if possessed. And when he crossed the finish line, the clock read 45.80 seconds. The athlete had improved his personal best by almost half a second, storming into a new dimension.

In recent years, the long hurdles had been a discipline in which several exceptional talents pushed each other. Norwegian Karsten Warholm always started like a wild bull and showed no weakness through to the finish line. In 2021, at the Tokyo Olympics, he not only won gold but also became the first person to run the 400-meter hurdles in under 46 seconds, clocking 45.94.

He never quite replicated these performances, which was partly due to injuries. Dos Santos used this opportunity to climb the hierarchy. This year, he was the dominant athlete in the long hurdles.

In Zurich, the 26-year-old beat Warholm using the Norwegian's own tactics. He started incredibly fast and overtook Warholm on the back straight. When he burst out of the final curve, he already had the victory in his pocket. But he wanted more: Dos Santos pushed through, sprinting to the world record.

## Russell's Stronger Comeback After Defeat

The second world record fell in the women's 100-meter hurdles, one of the disciplines with the highest concentration of talent worldwide. The previous world record had stood for only four years – American Masai Russell snatched it in Zurich in 12.09 seconds. Before that, Nigerian Tobi Amusan had held it with a time of 12.12.

Russell's strength this year is also due to a setback. As the 2024 Olympic champion, she was considered a favorite for the World Championships held in Tokyo last year. In the final, however, she finished fourth. It was the race in which Swiss Ditaji Kambundji delivered her sensational masterpiece and was crowned world champion. For Russell, missing the medals was «very tough and embarrassing,» as she recalled in Zurich.

After the defeat, she would have liked to turn everything upside down. Her agent, however, had talked sense into her and told her to continue on her path. «I looked in the mirror and asked myself: What am I capable of? What else can I do? I don't want to regret anything.» She learned to detach herself from expectations, from specific times and placings. And this year she ran six races in times between 12.14 – just two hundredths of a second over the previous world record – and 12.27 seconds.

In athletics, the rule is: anyone who achieves such a series of consistent times is ready for an exceptional performance. Ditaji Kambundji achieved this at the 2025 World Championships; for Russell, that moment arrived on this magical evening at Weltklasse Zürich. «I have proven this year that I can run strongly in any weather, any condition, and any location,» she said.

She had worked hard on this mindset, and also changed her preparation after the World Championship defeat. After the 2024 Olympic victory, she had experienced what happens to many athletes after great success: she had not fully recovered until the following season, experiencing mental fatigue during the summer.
