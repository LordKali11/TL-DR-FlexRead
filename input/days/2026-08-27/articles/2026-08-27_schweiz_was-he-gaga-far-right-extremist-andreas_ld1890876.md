# Was he a «gaga far-right extremist»? Andreas Glarner resigns

*The Aargau Swiss People's Party (SVP) National Council member and cantonal president is one of Switzerland's most controversial politicians. Now he is stepping down from his offices. A look back at a turbulent career.*

On Feb. 7, 2024, Andreas Glarner told private broadcaster Tele M1 that one was «politically and socially dead» if one was labeled a far-right extremist outside of one’s own spectrum. «Someone considered a far-right extremist loses their job and reputation.»

Now, two years later, on Aug. 26, he announced his resignation. He would step down from his positions as National Council member and president of the Aargau SVP in December, Glarner announced at the cantonal party conference on Wednesday. He assured the NZZ that this had nothing to do with the judicially approved epithet «gaga far-right extremist.» Nor did it have anything to do with the criminal proceedings currently ongoing against him. There had been no internal party pressure. Rather, he was resigning voluntarily to make way for younger forces, he added.

Glarner perhaps still feels a bit of pressure. Aargau SVP politicians who are 63 years old on election day or have served 16 years in office by the end of the legislative period must secure two-thirds of the votes in the cantonal executive board and at the party conference to be nominated. Andreas Glarner will be 64 years old in October.

He has served in the National Council since 2015 and has been president of the Aargau SVP since 2020. The party grew under his leadership and won a seventh seat in the National Council in the 2023 national elections. Although there was repeated loud criticism and distancing from individual figures within the party, official majority decisions or concerted demands for his resignation never materialized.

The cantonal party wrote about his resignation: «The Aargau SVP thanks Andreas Glarner for his many years of influential work for the benefit of the Canton of Aargau and the Swiss Confederation. He has sustainably shaped the political landscape and always maintained his clear compass.»

## All just «fun»

Andreas Glarner is asylum policy, provocation, and constant media presence. Already as municipal president of Oberwil-Lieli in Aargau about ten years ago, he was responsible for posters like «Maria instead of Sharia» and exempted the municipality from the obligation to take in refugees. SRF called him the «refugee bogeyman» in 2015, and «Blick» wrote of «asylum rampage.» He also repeatedly sought to cross boundaries on the national stage. A selection:

In 2018, Glarner exposed Dübendorf schoolchildren on Facebook because of their partly foreign-sounding names.

In 2019, he published a teacher’s phone number because she had pointed out in a parent letter that Muslim children were allowed to stay home during the fast-breaking meal. Afterwards, her phone rang off the hook. Glarner apologized and pointed out that the number had been declared a «school phone.» Otherwise, he would not have published the number, he said.

In 2024, Glarner published a picture of a Center Grand Council member on social media. «Ms. Rita Brem-Ingold wants criminal foreigners to be naturalized too!» The Grand Council member later reported that she had been threatened online and during walks.

Criminal proceedings are currently ongoing against Glarner for identity misuse. He had fabricated a deepfake video of Green National Council member Sibel Arslan. The responsible parliamentary commissions therefore lifted his immunity – which rarely occurs.

Glarner himself speaks of «fun.» He simply likes to provoke; it just comes out of him, he says.

## Girlfriend, sex, wine cellar

In 2022, the fun even ended for him. The «Aargauer Zeitung» reported on Dec. 17 that Glarner was demanding transit zones for asylum procedures and border controls. This prompted media entrepreneur Hansi Voigt to criticize the media on Twitter (now X):

«We should stop being outraged by what a gaga far-right extremist like Glarner says, who is completely ineffective in parliament. We should be outraged that @chmediaag constantly gives an ineffective parliamentarian a platform and thus ‹normalizes› extreme positions.»

Glarner associated the word far-right extremism with violence and Nazi ideology and filed a criminal complaint. On June 29, 2023, Voigt was convicted by a penal order for «defamation» and «insult.»

He appealed the case, and the Bremgarten District Court acquitted him. The court stated in its written judgment that media professionals, in particular, must be allowed to attribute a political conviction to someone that fits the public perception of their person in the political environment.

The Aargau High Court reached a similar conclusion in March 2025. It stated that the tweet did not give the impression that Glarner was anti-democratic, violent, or harbored sympathies for the Nazi regime, but rather was to be understood as a classification within the political spectrum as extremely right-wing.

Glarner insists that as a «democratically elected representative of the largest party,» he had never done anything far-right. In court, he argued that he appeared so right-wing partly because parliament had shifted to the left. In the end, he could not appeal the case further due to self-inflicted formal reasons.

His party has repeatedly distanced itself from far-right extremism in the past. Christoph Blocher, for example, said in the NZZ in 2024: «Everyone knows: We are against extremist ideologies, be it left or right.» The party leadership always remained silent about Glarner himself.

## The alcoholic mother

It is easy to get Andreas Glarner talking. He talks about his children and his partner («Did you know that she is the sister of SVP National Council member Christian Imark?»).

In the Swiss podcast «Thronfolge,» he let the audience know that sex with her was good and that he had also cheated in the past. «If a woman starts to deny a man, then he cheats or goes to a bordello.»

It is a bit as if Glarner wants to show off everything he has and what is important to him, he says. This includes his girlfriend and sex, as well as the outdoor kitchen, the wine cellar, or the library, where Uwe Diller («Corona was a crime of the century») lies next to Martin Suter. «I don’t want to boast; I am simply happy with my things,» he adds.

Andreas Glarner was born in 1962 in Glarnerland as the third of four children to a carpenter and advertising specialist and a textile laboratory technician.

When he was about thirteen years old, his parents divorced. His mother moved with Andreas to Dietikon in Zurich’s Limmattal. From then on, mother and son were alone; the younger brother was in a home, the two older sisters had already moved out.

After a botched hip operation, his mother succumbed to alcohol. When Andreas came home from school, she sat there in a delirium. The son emptied the bottles, went to the pubs and asked the landlords not to give his mother any more to drink. «That made me strong,» he says today.

Every two weeks, his father, a Social Democrat, visited his son. He wanted to take him in. But his new wife objected. Glarner doesn’t blame his father: «He simply fled from my mother.» Until his death, Glarner and his father took a boat trip together twice a year.

His childhood taught him to become independent, Glarner says. He worked as a newspaper delivery boy early on and resolved to become successful. He succeeded.

## Millionaire at 45

Glarner completed an apprenticeship as a ventilation plumber. But he has the nature of a salesman. After further training in industrial engineering and management, he founded his first company, Airproduct AG, in mid-1991, which he sold in 2006, becoming a millionaire at 45.

Later he built up other companies and entered cantonal politics. Today he sells this and that: wine, olive oil, the Thermomix kitchen machine. When he recently accidentally ran over one of the devices with his car, several media outlets reported the incident.

Glarner’s media presence is disproportionate to his political influence. In legislating, where precise detailed work is required, he has a reputation for making biting comments but rarely introducing substantive proposals.

He himself locates his greatest political success in the time of local politics, where as municipal president he had made Oberwil-Lieli «the jewel of Mutschellen,» with the lowest tax rate and social costs, the highest per capita income, and the highest assets in Aargau, Glarner says.

After his resignation, he wants to achieve something entrepreneurially and devote himself to the Mutschellen district school, where he holds the presidency, he says. He also wants to spend more leisure time with his partner and her children. Hardly anyone today believes that nothing more will be heard from him outside of Aargau.
