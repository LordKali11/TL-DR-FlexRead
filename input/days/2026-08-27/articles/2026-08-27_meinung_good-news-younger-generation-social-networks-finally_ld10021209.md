And suddenly it works. No more endless scrolling at night, no more comparing likes: How children and young people in the US use Instagram and Facebook is expected to fundamentally change in the coming year.

Meta, the owner of Facebook and Instagram, has agreed to an out-of-court settlement. In it, the company commits not only to payments of up to 18 billion dollars to US states but also to serious youth protection measures. The signal could hardly be clearer: Social networks are finally being held accountable. Other platforms – and countries – should follow suit.

## Lost social media generation's lawsuits

It has been more than twenty years since Facebook was launched. What began as a platform for a few personal thoughts and pictures has evolved into an app where people mindlessly scroll through distracting short videos for hours.

Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat – they have become quite similar. They have all learned what keeps users glued to their screens for as long as possible: social recognition on the one hand, and fast-paced videos on the other, highly individually adapted to what interests each person.

An entire generation was defenseless against this rush at a vulnerable age. The wave of lawsuits against social networks in the US, to which this latest settlement also belongs, began with the case of a 20-year-old who acted as a spokesperson for this «lost generation.» Eating disorders, suicidality, sexualized messages from strangers: Her fate impressively demonstrated the consequences of social platforms at their worst.

But it doesn't even have to come to that. There is no need to argue with seriously ill young people to restrict social networks. The constant distraction in school, the bullying, the endless circling around filtered selfies and scrolling all night long: It is obvious that all of this in such quantities is unhealthy.

In the wake of the wave of lawsuits, a flood of internal documents and studies became public, supporting this intuition. They show how employees of the platforms discussed the problems and devised solutions, but were repeatedly held back for fear of economic losses.

## Scrolling restrictions at night and during school hours

Now, finally, the pressure has become so great that the first platforms are implementing the solutions they have long known.

These include time limits: All users under 17 on Instagram and Facebook will be put into a mandatory night mode from midnight to six in the morning. They can still read messages but cannot scroll normally on the apps.

Strict limits also apply during the day: In total, children should not spend more than two hours on Meta apps. And during school hours, push notifications are deactivated – parents can also set the «night mode» for school hours.

Beauty filters that simulate surgical procedures will be banned, likes will be hidden, and if users report content due to bullying, nudity, or self-harm, Meta commits to responding to at least 90% of reports within six hours. And the list goes on.

Of course, there will be hurdles in implementation. Young people are resourceful at circumventing rules. An external body will therefore continuously monitor the status of the measures. Of course, such rules only make sense if they encompass all networks. The fact that Meta has given in means that it expects a turnaround in the entire industry.

This settlement is a strong signal. Suddenly, the US is leading the way in child protection on social platforms. With measures that are more elegant and well-conceived than the ban for under-16s, because they preserve the positive aspects of networks while mitigating the harms. This is good news, especially for those growing up now.
