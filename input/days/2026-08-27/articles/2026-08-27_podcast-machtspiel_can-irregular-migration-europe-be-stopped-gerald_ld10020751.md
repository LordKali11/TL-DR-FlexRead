## In this podcast episode

His calendar seems to be overflowing; the agreed-upon conversation has to be postponed multiple times. Even ten years after the EU-Turkey deal, Gerald Knaus remains a highly sought-after man. The Austrian migration researcher is considered the architect of the agreement, and to this day he advises numerous governments and UN organizations. After tens of thousands of migrants had arrived in the Spanish exclave of Ceuta in the summer of 2026, interest in Knaus’s expertise is particularly high again. His proposal then, as now, remains the same: the EU should conclude agreements with other states to end irregular migration and deaths in the Mediterranean, he argues.

For Knaus, control over who comes is the key question par excellence. However, corresponding measures such as border controls at Germany's external borders have so far had no significant effect, Knaus says; he calls them «theater politics.» His proposal therefore: Outsource asylum procedures to so-called safe third countries. «If we had agreements with three or four safe third countries, then a goal of virtually zero irregular migration next year is legally possible today,» he says. Safe third countries are by far the best way, politically, morally, and practically, to curb migration without abandoning human rights standards, he argues.

In the new episode of «NZZ Machtspiel,» Gerald Knaus, chairman of the European Stability Initiative (ESI) think tank, discusses with Beatrice Achterberg, NZZ Germany editor, why he considers the term «remigration» to be «poisoned» despite its scientific roots and why he views the right to asylum as part of Europe's identity.
