# Marc Lüthi is the new President of Swiss Ice Hockey – can the dynamic leader bring peace to Swiss ice hockey?

*The election of the long-time CEO and co-owner of SC Bern will have personnel consequences. For now, however, a ceasefire is likely to prevail for a month, with the next international election approaching.*

On Thursday afternoon, Marc Lüthi was elected Chairman of the Board of Directors of Swiss Ice Hockey by an online vote. His appointment was largely a formality; no opposing candidates emerged. The Lüthi camp had anticipated nearly 60% of the votes in advance, despite opposition from amateur sports. He received significantly more.

Lüthi, 65, was CEO of SC Bern until spring; SCB, as it is known today, is practically his life's work. There are differing accounts of how voluntary his departure from Bern was, but that is a topic for another day. He assumes his role as helmsman of Swiss Ice Hockey as a figure of hope – he is expected to modernize, invigorate, and presumably streamline the association.

Stefan Schärer and Urs Kessler, Lüthi's predecessors in this position, started with similar optimism but were soon so worn down that they resigned after a few months. For years, all presidents have said the same thing: they should finally manage to put club and particular interests aside for the good of Swiss ice hockey. But as soon as a contentious issue, such as a reduction of the National League (NL) from 14 to 12 teams, is discussed, harmony ends.

There is an almost 100% consensus in the industry that 14 teams are two too many. But it is impossible to find the necessary majority for this in a vote – too many club representatives fear relegation and, above all, job losses. Lüthi will not be able to change that either. He informed the media about his plans for the first time at a press conference in Fribourg on Friday morning but remained vague. «My vision for Swiss Ice Hockey is lean and efficient. But I won't start with dismissals immediately. First, I will gain an overview. The first 100 days belong to me,» he said.

## How long can CEO Martin Baumann hold on?

The dynamic leader often saw SCB as a kind of one-man show. It remains to be seen whether he has the patience and tact to succeed in association politics. Compromises have rarely been his forte in the past. Lüthi's first important task is the new cooperation agreement with the National League. The existing one ends after the 2026/27 season.

This agreement regulates which services (such as refereeing) the National League obtains from Swiss Ice Hockey and at what price. NL representatives have been accusing Swiss Ice Hockey for years of spending money too carelessly and want to drastically cut funds. The negotiations could become unpleasant.

It can also be assumed that personnel changes will occur within Swiss Ice Hockey under Lüthi. Regarding the replacement of CEO Martin Baumann, who fell out of favor with National League representatives some time ago, a change is virtually guaranteed. Before the press conference on Friday, he summoned Baumann to a meeting to discuss «immediate measures.» However, a majority on the Board of Directors is needed for a separation from the CEO.

Kessler had already tried to push for Baumann's dismissal in the spring. And despite assurances to the contrary prior, he received not a single vote for it. The intrigues among the officials sometimes seem as if they sprang from «Gute Zeiten, schlechte Zeiten» or «Lüthi und Blanc.»

For now, however, a truce is likely to emerge. In one month, comprehensive elections will take place at the mid-year congress of the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) in Tenerife. Peter Zahner, CEO of the ZSC Lions and a National League representative on the Board of Directors of Swiss Ice Hockey, has had himself nominated by the association as a candidate for the IIHF Council using very tough tactics.

This is despite the fact that there was no reason to replace the well-connected (and 21 years younger) incumbent Swiss Council member Raeto Raffainer. A failure to consider Zahner would be a major defeat, primarily for Zahner himself, but also for Swiss hockey politics. It can be assumed that Lüthi will not torpedo the candidacy of his long-time companion with an upheaval before the mid-year congress.

## René Fasel's power play for Russia and Peter Zahner

It is heard that René Fasel, among others, is currently lobbying for Zahner. Fasel, 76, the former IIHF President, is working to elevate candidates into key positions who support a swift return of Russia. The 27-time world champion has been excluded from all IIHF competitions since the start of the war against Ukraine.

The political power play for a comeback has been in full swing for some time; observers assume that Russia will participate in the 2028 World Cup of Hockey. And specifically, as a group opponent of Switzerland in Calgary. The World Cup is hosted not by the IIHF but by the NHL. But it would be a door opener for Russia's reintegration starting with the 2028 World Championship in Lyon and Paris.

It remains to be seen how successful the lobbying efforts of Fasel, who has held Russian citizenship for some time, actually are. His group is trying to nominate two candidates as replacements for the outgoing French IIHF President Luc Tardif: the German Franz Reindl and the current regional Vice President for Asia and Oceania, Aiwaz Omorkanow from Kyrgyzstan.

The election campaign there is also being conducted with little restraint. The Czech Petr Briza is considered the favorite to succeed Tardif, having secured the support of the most important Western European nations. However, it is not clear what Switzerland's position on the Russia question will be in the future, especially given this election campaign.

International politics is likely to be relatively low on Lüthi's agenda. There is more than enough to do in his own country.
