# Mail-in voting chaos threatens US midterm elections

*A bitter legal battle is raging in the US over mail-in voting. Donald Trump recently won a partial victory, but time is running out to implement new rules before the midterm elections.*

There are still a good two months until the American midterm elections on Nov. 3. The final state primaries will take place by early September, after which the hot phase of the election campaign begins. It is all the more astonishing that it is not yet conclusively clear whether the midterm elections will take place under the familiar rules or whether stricter national restrictions will ultimately apply to mail-in voting. Nationally, almost a third of all voters cast their ballots by mail in the last presidential election in 2024, with rules varying by state.

## Partial victory for Trump

The reason for the uncertainty is a bitter legal dispute surrounding Trump's March attempt to restrict mail-in voting with a new regulation. A few days ago, the Supreme Court granted the president a partial victory: In a narrow decision, the court's conservative majority dismissed a lawsuit from various states against the regulation, arguing that it had been filed too early.

Although the highest judges did not yet address the merits of the lawsuit, the plaintiffs contend that the federal government is interfering with states' authority to conduct elections, the Supreme Court is provisionally allowing the Trump administration to advance its plans to restrict mail-in voting in the midterm elections.

The regulation instructs the Department of the Interior to create a list of all eligible voters from each state. In addition, the United States Postal Service (USPS) is instructed to deliver mail-in ballot documents only to those voters who are pre-registered on a special list.

## Postal Service develops plans

Despite the legal uncertainty, the USPS is now also moving forward with its plans to implement Trump's regulation. A few days ago, the Postal Service presented a 95-page plan for this. According to the plan, the USPS would check every ballot envelope before it can be sent. Specifically, the Postal Service would have to ensure that the addressee of the envelope is registered on a special voter list that entitles them to vote by mail. In addition, the envelope would also have to meet certain requirements and contain a special barcode that is intended to prevent fraud and counterfeiting.

Should the new rules be introduced for the upcoming midterm elections, the states would hardly have enough time for careful implementation. Moreover, there would be a risk that voters who had previously always voted by mail could miss the election.

Further lawsuits are pending against Trump's regulation, and the Postal Service's plan could now also be challenged. Professor of constitutional law Steve Vladeck from Georgetown University told the TV channel PBS that the Supreme Court would very soon have to clarify conclusively whether the federal government is allowed to amend the rules by regulation or not. Otherwise, chaotic conditions would threaten the midterm elections, he said.

## Politically charged

The dispute over mail-in voting is politically charged in the U.S. According to a survey, 37% of Democratic voters nationwide voted by mail in 2024, compared to only 24% of Republican voters. Trump's regulation is therefore also seen as an attempt to give Republicans an advantage in the midterm elections. Since he lost the presidential election to Joe Biden in 2020, Trump has also viewed mail-in ballots as a source of manipulation and fraud.

However, states enjoy relatively broad constitutional freedoms in conducting elections. In June, the Supreme Court therefore concluded in a ruling, to Trump's annoyance, that states are allowed to count late-arriving mail-in ballots in the November midterm elections.
