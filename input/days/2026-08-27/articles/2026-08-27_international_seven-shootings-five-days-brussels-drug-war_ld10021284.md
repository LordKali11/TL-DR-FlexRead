# Seven shootings in five days: Brussels drug war escalates

*The Belgian Interior Minister spoke of a «national emergency.» Authorities announced new measures in the fight against drug trafficking. But the problem has long since spiraled out of control.*

It began on a Friday evening: Shortly before midnight, two men appeared in front of a police station in the Brussels district of Anderlecht. One filmed with his mobile phone while the other fired twelve shots at the entrance door with a Kalashnikov assault rifle. The two then fled on an electric scooter. Because the station was closed at the time of the attack, no one was injured. Authorities suspected a retaliatory act by a criminal gang – the police had carried out several raids in the drug scene during the summer.

The violence did not abate in the following days. On Saturday evening, a man was injured in the leg by a gunshot in the Saint-Gilles district. Early on Sunday morning, a bullet pierced the window of a bedroom where the resident was still in bed. On Monday, a man opened fire near the Bruxelles-Midi train station with an automatic weapon, forcing the guests of a pizzeria to take cover.

By Tuesday, Brussels authorities had recorded a total of seven shootings within five days. Authorities linked all incidents to drug crime. What is happening in Brussels?

## Violence at record levels

For years, the Belgian capital has been fighting criminal gangs involved in the drug trade, some of whom are rivals. The situation is particularly precarious in the structurally weak west of the city with a high proportion of migrants, for example in Anderlecht. However, in recent years, drug crime has increasingly spread to the wealthier east. As dealers get younger, cocaine gets cheaper, and violence becomes more brutal, authorities are progressively losing control.

In 2025, there was a record number of 101 shootings, in which a total of 8 people were killed and 43 injured. This year, the figures are similarly high: So far, 66 shootings with 25 injured and 2 dead have been recorded.

Drug crime in the capital has long occupied national politics. Belgian Interior Minister Bernard Quintin said on Thursday morning: «The fight against organized crime associated with drug trafficking is a national emergency.» He announced that the police presence in the affected neighborhoods would be increased, adding that the areas needed to be «flooded» with police officers.

Representatives of national and municipal authorities had already met for a crisis meeting on Wednesday evening. Among other things, authorities decided to send forty additional police officers to the affected municipalities and districts and to install twenty mobile cameras in the coming days. They also decided that a coordination office would be established to improve the information flow between the police, municipalities, and regional authorities.

## Protests Announced

Whether these measures will have the desired effect is questionable, however. Even before the latest escalation, the Interior Ministry had sent 77 additional police officers to Brussels and provided funds for surveillance cameras. In addition, police have been patrolling metro stations and train stations with soldiers since spring. However, the current legal situation does not allow military personnel to carry out arrests or searches on their own initiative. So far, none of the measures taken have led to a significant reduction in drug crime.

Therefore, authorities are now openly considering much sharper steps: Mayors of the Brussels municipalities were informed at Wednesday's crisis meeting that they could impose curfews or establish zones to which only residents have access.

Such measures, however, would restrict the population, who are already suffering the most from the situation. Many are losing patience: Several organizations have called for a demonstration on Sunday at a well-known drug dealing hotspot in Saint-Gilles. But the dealers will not be driven away by that either.
