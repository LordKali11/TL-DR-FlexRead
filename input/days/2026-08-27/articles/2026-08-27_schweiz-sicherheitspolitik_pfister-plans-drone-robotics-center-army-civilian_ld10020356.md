# Pfister Plans Drone and Robotics Center for Army and Civilian Emergency Services

*Switzerland faces a proliferation of drone defense systems. Defense Minister Martin Pfister now aims to consolidate responsibilities and expertise. The project faces various hurdles: neither the location nor the budget or legal framework have been clarified.*

«Eiger» is the project name for the Defense Department's (VBS) new competence center for unmanned systems — meaning drones and robots. The task for Defense Minister Martin Pfister is as challenging as its namesake: He explained to the media in Thun on Thursday that he wanted to consolidate civilian and military actors from the army, the armaments industry, science, and emergency services in one location and thus prepare the entire country for this threat.

The initiative is driven by Russia's covert warfare against Western countries. Another clear warning sign for Pfister was the failed attack at Leipzig/Halle Airport, where earlier this month a drone with explosives was found near a Ukrainian cargo plane. Moscow is suspected. Moreover, according to research by the British «Telegraph», Russia is building new launch sites for attack drones near Belarus and Ukraine, whose range extends to NATO's eastern flank. Pfister warned that the security situation in Europe had reached a new level of danger. Switzerland needed to know what to do about it at all levels of government.

Today, defense here lags behind. Neither police forces nor airports, infrastructure operators, or the army have comprehensive protection systems. Army Chief Benedikt Roos emphasizes that Switzerland is also at risk: «Switzerland is attacked every day.» The army located around 100 overflights per month above a single military training area. In one in five cases, the origin had remained unclear. During the G7 summit in Évian, the troops also registered around 250 drones in the area and intervened in almost half of them, for example, with jammers. In three cases, the pilots were identified and reported. For Roos, it is clear that drones are no longer a future scenario but have long been a «harsh reality.»

## **Numerous Legal Hurdles**

However, part of the Swiss reality is also that the planned center currently exists only on paper. Neither the location nor the budget framework or opening date are set. Pfister aims for its establishment in the «coming months.» Funding is to come from the current budget — also by consolidating existing structures. The VBS alone has at least three departments for drones or robotics; some cantons operate additional drone centers. Where the new center is to be built is still being examined. Legal hurdles also weigh heavily in the search for a location, as a demonstration at the Thun military training area shows.

There, trained drone soldiers waited for the media on Thursday to demonstrate their skills. They controlled reconnaissance and attack drones and combated approaching flying objects with special ammunition. When three approaches were successfully stopped in the last firing round, an abrupt end followed: the maximum shooting time before the lunch break had been reached, the exercise leader announced. In addition to strict noise regulations, further legal hurdles hinder the troops. While the army may locate foreign drones over its own area, only the cantonal police may shoot them down if necessary. The Defense Minister demanded that the legal basis needed to be regulated more clearly. For this, he said, they were in close contact with the cantons.

The new center is to be organized in both civilian and military capacities and open to other departments, cantons, and emergency services. It is to house a military command and a tech hub. The command will train soldiers and test unmanned systems under conditions as realistic as possible. In the tech hub, the Federal Office for Armaments (Armasuisse), universities, startups, and industry will combine their knowledge to rapidly develop new solutions and transfer them to the troops.

Head of Armaments Urs Loher emphasized that the goal was no longer to «preserve a technology unchanged for as long as possible.» Rather, he said, it needed to be clarified early on whether systems could be independently further developed. He added that the war in Ukraine showed this, where systems needed to be adapted every few weeks. Modern warfare necessitates a change in mentality. Loher intends to focus on modular, standardized products with open interfaces in the future, which are to be procured in small tranches.

## Pfister's campaign for VAT increase

What sounds like a revolution in the sluggish Swiss procurement system, the Head of Armaments considered feasible. Switzerland has top universities, innovative startups, and specialized companies for robotics, sensor technology, and artificial intelligence. Until now, however, this ecosystem has been lacking the structure to have an impact in the military. The new center is intended to change that.

Pfister did not only invite the media to Thun to present a center that does not yet exist. Before the autumn session, the Defense Minister used the stage to once again advocate for the army's financing package. The development of drone and robotics capabilities is central but requires more funds.

He stated that a total of 24 billion Swiss francs in armaments investments were necessary over the next twelve years for the army's new orientation towards the most probable threats — namely cyberattacks, disinformation, sabotage, and air attacks. The Federal Council proposes a temporary increase in value-added tax by 0.5 percentage points and a debt-eligible armaments fund for this purpose. However, the proposal faces difficulties in parliament — only Pfister's own party, The Centre, supports it.
