# «The market was gone in moments»: No escape from Nepal’s flood disaster

*The massive tsunami swept away entire villages in the Himalayas, and the number of missing has risen to as many as 1,400. The cause may have been a glacial collapse. The flash flood was so powerful that it triggered a 5.2 magnitude earthquake.*

Destroyed houses, buried roads, swept-away bridges: The flash flood on the border between Nepal and China left a trail of destruction along the Trishuli River. The mass of water, ice, mud and debris poured down the valley like liquid concrete. Over a stretch of more than 60 kilometers, it buried entire settlements. For residents in many places, there was no escape: The meter-high flood wave moved with such speed that fleeing was hopeless.

Rajendra Dhungana, the customs chief at the Nepalese border crossing in the village of Timure, told the «Kathmandu Post» that he had initially believed it was an earthquake. When he ran out, he said he saw a 100-meter-high wall of water that had plunged down the valley. «The market was gone in moments,» Dhungana told the newspaper. Some 300 trucks and other vehicles at the Timure border crossing had also been swept away, he added.

Even a day after the disaster, many places were unreachable. According to authorities, at least 19 bridges and 40 kilometers of road were washed away. There was no contact with the Gyirong border crossing between Nepal and China, which had been hit with full force by the flash flood, even on Thursday morning. At least a dozen villages along the Trishuli River were devastated, and thousands of houses were swept away or buried.

## Thousands of families' concern for relatives

Among the destroyed villages is the hamlet of Soley. «Our entire village was swept away by the river,» resident Bidhya Rajput told the NZZ. She said her father-in-law was on his way to the school where he taught as a teacher when the flash flood hit, and her mother-in-law was at home taking care of the household. Since then, there has been no trace of either of them, Rajput added. Three other family members may also have been swept away by the Trishuli, she said.

The Trishuli River originates in the Tibetan highlands and flows through Nepal to India into the Ganges. In its upper course in Nepal, it is also called Bhotekoshi, which means «Tibetan River.» The flash flood came from the Lhende River, which flows into the Trishuli at the border crossing. The Friendship Bridge, which connects the two countries there, had already been destroyed a year ago in a flash flood. Nepal’s most important trade route to China runs across the bridge.

## Almost 1,400 people missing in Nepal and China

Hundreds of pilgrims, mountaineers and other tourists, many of them foreigners, were in the disaster region on Wednesday. Hundreds of Hindu pilgrims from India and Nepal were on their way to the holy Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar in Tibet. Nepal announced that 823 people were missing, including 579 tourists. Among them were almost 300 Indians, as well as dozens of Americans, Australians, Canadians, Britons and Ukrainians, according to Nepalese authorities. China reported the number of missing as 558.

A 39-member Nepalese tour group from the Gorkha district was also reported missing. Shanta Adhikari, the sister of one of the missing, told the NZZ that the pilgrim group had wanted to take a holy bath in Lake Gosaikunda on Friday for the Hindu festival Janai Purnima. However, on the way to Dhunche, from where the trek to the lake at 4,300 meters altitude starts, the three vehicles of the tour group had been caught by the flood and swept into the river, she added.

«We heard that a bus attendant survived by jumping out of the bus,» Adhikari said. A few other occupants might also have been able to escape, she noted. However, there was no news from her sister and her husband. «Their six-year-old daughter and eight-year-old son are waiting at home,» Adhikari said. «We hope for a miracle. All we can do now is pray to God,» she added.

## Continuing rise in death toll

By Thursday evening, the death toll in Nepal had risen to 353. China initially confirmed three deaths. However, the actual death toll is likely to be much higher. A video from the Chinese side of the border crossing shows how the massive flood wave overtakes dozens of fleeing people and buries them. Dozens of buses, trucks and cars were also swept away. In other places, too, there are likely to be few survivors.

A video from the town of Bidur, about 55 kilometers downstream from the border, shows how the flood wave first sweeps away a steel bridge and then pulls a whole row of houses into the water. Another video shows the masses of gray water surging into the pilgrimage site of Betrawati Bazar a few kilometers farther north. Many residents of the river valley had no choice but to run up the hills to safety.

## 5.2 magnitude tremor from ice avalanche

The cause of the flash flood may have been a glacier collapse above the Lhende River. The fall of ice masses from a great height could have turned them into water. However, a glacial lake outburst is also possible. According to a study by the environmental organization Icimod, there were 3,624 glacial lakes in the Himalayan region of Nepal, Tibet and India in 2020, of which 47 need to be closely monitored because they can easily burst and thus pose a danger.

Nepal’s government’s initial assumption that an earthquake had triggered the flood proved false. The American earthquake observatory announced that a tremor of 5.2 magnitude registered in the region at 8:37 a.m. on Wednesday morning had not been an earthquake, but had been triggered by the fall of ice masses from the glacier. However, the exact cause of the disaster will probably only become clear later.

The Himalayas lie on the border of two tectonic plates. Earthquakes are therefore frequent in the mountain range, which contains some of the highest mountains in the world. In addition, there are regular avalanches, landslides, flash floods and glacial lake outbursts, especially during the monsoon in the warm summer months, when heavy thunderstorms and torrential rain often occur in the mountains. However, there were no heavy rains in the region before the current flash flood.

## First warning message from authorities at 9:15 a.m.

The Nepalese authority responsible for monitoring water levels stated that it received information about an impending flood wave from Tibet at 9:05 a.m. Previously, a water measuring station below the village of Timure had stopped sending data. As the «Kathmandu Post» writes, residents in the river valley were called upon to be vigilant via SMS at 9:15 a.m. Five minutes later, the flood wave already reached the village of Betrawati, about 45 kilometers downstream from the border.

Warning systems in Nepal have repeatedly proven inadequate in the past. After last year’s flash flood, which destroyed the Friendship Bridge on the border, Kathmandu and Beijing agreed on increased information exchange, but an operational warning system is still lacking. After the disaster, a discussion erupted in the media about how the early warning system and information exchange could be improved.

The most severely affected areas are the districts of Rasuwa and Nuwakot. According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (Ocha), 10,000 households now urgently need help. The Nepalese Red Cross mobilized 200 helpers to distribute tarpaulins, blankets, mats and first-aid kits in the affected settlements. India, China and the U.S. pledged aid. The Swiss aid organization Helvetas, which has been active in Nepal for many years, is also examining how it can help those affected.
