# Red-Green refuses to give up: Zurich City Parliament wants to fight for cash payments for undocumented migrants before the Federal Supreme Court

*Despite low chances of success, the City Council is appealing a ruling on basic economic assistance. The council president casts the deciding vote.*

Such close decisions are rare in the Zurich City Parliament. The vote on Wednesday evening in the council chamber was 60 to 60. The president of the City Council, a Social Democrat, ultimately cast the deciding vote to continue the long history of a controversial municipal social program.

The point of contention was what is known as basic economic assistance. Raphael Golta (SP), the former head of social services and current city president of Zurich, had launched this program during the coronavirus pandemic. Poor foreigners, including Sans-Papiers (undocumented migrants), were to benefit from cash payments, Golta intended.

But Golta operated without legal expertise. Several courts ruled that his approach was not compatible with higher-ranking law – most recently the Zurich Administrative Court.

The Red-Green parties do not want to accept this. On Wednesday, they decided in parliament to appeal the ruling to the country's highest court, the Federal Supreme Court. The arguments of the previous courts were not convincing, Lisa Diggelmann (SP) found.

Diggelmann argued that the Administrative Court – like the District Council and Zurich's cantonal government before it – misunderstood that municipalities have a core competence in social welfare. They had broad discretion in designing measures to combat poverty, she said. The basic economic assistance was also designed as a temporary pilot project, she maintained. «In our rich city, no one should have to live in undignified circumstances,» Diggelmann said. This also applied to undocumented migrants, she added.

The center-right unsuccessfully resisted the appeal. «No private individual would pursue such a hopeless request,» said Benedikt Gerth (Mitte). The chances of success were so small, he said, that one might as well throw the money for the legal dispute out the window. Sven Sobernheim (GLP) also spoke of a «quibble» that, he said, would probably only end before the European Court of Human Rights.

Emanuel Tschannen (FDP) declared: «Shoemaker, stick to your last.» He argued that the city was simply not responsible in this matter. Even if the City Council decided that a speed limit of 130 km/h should apply on highway sections within the city, responsibility for national roads still lay with the federal government, he said. He expected «less showmanship» and more acceptance of the legal framework and federal structures from the left.

Stephan Iten (SVP) took a fundamental stance. Undocumented migrants were in Switzerland illegally, he said. «Why should we financially support these people?» He contended that every undocumented person had the opportunity to apply for a residence permit through regular channels.
