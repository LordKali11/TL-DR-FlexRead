# Valérie Dittli founds a liberal movement – Vaud's left is likely to rub its hands together

*It seemed only a matter of time: After new allegations, the controversial State Councillor leaves her Centre Party. This has far-reaching political consequences.*

Vaud State Councillor Valérie Dittli left the Centre Party six months before the cantonal elections. «I had no other choice,» she told the newspaper Le Temps Thursday evening. «I am no longer available to the party. What we have experienced in recent weeks has become too difficult. There are no words to describe it.»

Dittli's departure comes as little surprise. For years, supporters and critics alike have been feuding within the Vaud Centre Party over the controversial State Councillor, who has been involved in several political affairs since March 2025. Next week, a delegates' assembly was supposed to decide whether the party would support Dittli's candidacy for the State Council – after the executive board and the party committee had already rejected it.

Dittli indicated in a newspaper interview mid-August that in the event of another rejection, she would have run even without the Centre Party. In that case, she would presumably have left the party anyway. In the same interview, Dittli still referred to the Centre Party as «my family.»

## Dittli accompanied by Centre executive board members

Now Dittli is launching a new group called Mouvement libéral (Liberal Movement). «The idea didn't even come from me, but from the people around me,» Valérie Dittli said when asked. She is accompanied by three of the nine executive board members of the Vaud Centre Party. Among them is the vice president of the cantonal Centre Party, Jacqueline Bottlang-Pittet, who has been a member of the party, or its predecessor the CVP, for about 50 years.

The movement, Dittli added, would be formally established in the coming days. She had already received quite a few inquiries from people interested in joining, including former members of The Liberals and the Centre Party, as well as independents. The movement primarily aimed at the middle class, at lower taxes and less bureaucracy.

How deep the split in the Centre Party goes is still difficult to say. The Vaud section was politically insignificant for a long time. Dittli is its first State Councillor. The party has only a few hundred members and few local officeholders.

However, Dittli and her allies could certainly appeal to other voters with the new movement, for example, disappointed supporters of the Vaud Liberals. First and foremost, former Liberal municipal president and winemaker Philippe Gex, who founded a support club for Dittli, criticized what he saw as the too little bourgeois course of the party and the Liberal-dominated cantonal government.

Some of these critics mourn the former Liberal Party, which operated to the right of The Liberals and merged with it in 2009. The name of Dittli's new movement also seems to be explicitly aimed at these people. The State Councillor recently told the NZZ that she shared a liberal mindset with Philippe Gex.

## Dittli and Centre Party dispute donations

Only the immediate timing of Dittli's departure seems surprising. The sudden announcement followed new allegations against Dittli in reports by Blick and RTS, which had been published Thursday afternoon. In them, the president of Dittli's former party section said that Dittli had wanted to close or transfer a party account with a balance of around 22,000 Swiss francs.

Dittli denied this Thursday. Thus, it is one statement against another. According to media reports, the account contained donations from Philippe Gex's winery and from individuals, among others. Dittli said this money was intended for her political work, not specifically for her renewed candidacy in the cantonal elections in February 2027. The president of the cantonal Centre Party, Giancarlo Sergi, announced a legal opinion on this question.

Another point of contention between Dittli's supporters and critics these days was who could participate and vote at the delegates' assembly Wednesday. Dittli's supporters accused the party leadership of having changed the rules on short notice to limit support for Dittli. Party president Sergi, however, said he was merely applying the party's statutes, which had been valid for years and which vice president and Dittli supporter Bottlang-Pittet had also helped to draft.

Dittli's opponents in the Centre Party are now likely to breathe a sigh of relief. «We want to close the Dittli chapter,» Sergi had repeatedly said in the past two weeks. When asked, he expressed confidence Friday about the future of his party, adding that the Centre Party would stick to the delegates' assembly next week and would discuss candidacies and strategies for the cantonal elections.

All potential alliance partners of the Centre Party had already stated that they would not be available if Dittli ran. Now the party leadership can carry out its desired break with the previous bourgeois alliance with The Liberals and the Swiss People's Party. Sergi expected to have more chances, together with the Green Liberals and two small centrist parties, to send Centre politicians back to the cantonal parliament. Currently, the Centre Party has not a single parliamentarian.

## Potential suffering for The Liberals and Swiss People's Party under Dittli

For the bourgeois parties in Vaud, however, Dittli's solo move appears fatal. Her candidacy and the Mouvement libéral as a whole could cost The Liberals and the Swiss People's Party crucial votes in the cantonal elections. This would jeopardize the bourgeois majority in the State Council – which is already relative due to the dispute over Dittli. Vaud's left is likely to rub its hands together.

Valérie Dittli has been under strong pressure since March 2025. Several investigative reports accused her, among other things, of abuse of office and the use of public funds in exchange for the withdrawal of a criminal complaint against her. The prosecutor's office is investigating Dittli in both cases.

This now seems to be the greatest danger for Dittli's renewed candidacy for the State Council: Should the investigations gain momentum and the allegations against Dittli be substantiated, this could further darken her political future in Vaud.
