What a feeling it must be to stand on the track below and feel it tremble underfoot when the announcer calls out your name: Audrey Werro. The 22-year-old is currently the greatest talent in Swiss athletics; she has just become European champion, and it seems as if there are no limits for her.

The 800-meter runner has retained some of the joyful urge to move that characterized her as a child. During a training visit in 2023, she recounted how she indulged this urge when she went shopping with her mother: «I grabbed a bag, ran home, placed the Migros bag in front of the garage, ran back to meet my mother, grabbed another bag, and took off.»

## Talent, Pace, and Self-Confidence

Since then, Werro has honed her talent through countless hours of training, but the carefree spirit of her childhood has remained. In her best races, she resolutely takes the initiative at some point and runs at the front, as if she had to get a Migros bag home as quickly as possible. This is how she also won the European Championship title: from the front and with the self-assurance of being able to accelerate at any time. And this, even though she had suffered a bad fall in the semifinal the day before.

Audrey Werro runs like great champions run. This outdoor season, with the exception of the ill-fated European Championship semifinal where she fell, she has won every single race. And she has literally swept the competition along, because it is an unwritten rule: When the Swiss athlete is at the start, it will be a fast race.

This was also the case at Weltklasse Zürich. Ever since Werro ran the fastest time in over forty years in Stockholm in June, she never stepped onto the track without someone talking about the world record. Jarmila Kratochvilova ran it in 1983 with 1:53.28. In Zurich, too, the pacemaker was tasked with setting a pace for the first 500 meters that could bring Werro close to the record.

However, the pacing was a touch too inconsistent, and Werro also had to contend with the Dutchwoman Femke Broeders-Bol at the start of the final bend. But then it was as if the Swiss athlete had an extra gear. She pulled away and stormed to a dominant victory in 1:53.70. Werro has now run a time under 1:54 for the third time this year. That is outstanding.

Broeders-Bol was still world champion in the 400m hurdles a year ago. Now she is completing a training year over the two track laps, running faster in every race than before, and is likely to be Werro's fiercest rival in the future. Both have not yet reached their full potential.

In the recent history of Swiss athletics, there was only one athlete who was as dominant as Werro. Around the turn of the millennium, André Bucher was also the boss on the track over 800 meters. He ran best from the front; in the summer of 2001, he won 11 out of 12 races over the two track laps and stormed to the World Championship title in Edmonton. The world record, however, remained out of reach for him.

The fact that Werro is now being discussed in connection with this ultimate benchmark is remarkable in itself. World records by Swiss athletes are almost as rare as pearls in oysters. In the history of Swiss athletics to date, five athletes have been included in the official lists: Isabell Pfenning (1941) in the high jump, Meta Antenen (1969) in the pentathlon, Werner Günthör (1987) in the shot put indoors, Julien Wanders in the 5K road race (2019), Simon Ehammer (2026) in the heptathlon indoors.

## Behind the successes: a typically Swiss system

It is no coincidence that this year saw golden moments in Swiss sports, such as Ehammer's record or Werro's fast runs. Athletics has been in a permanent upswing since the home European Championships in 2014; medals at European and World Championships are accumulating, and new talents achieve top-3 rankings year after year at junior championships. Never before has there been such a density of international top athletes in this country.

What is still missing from this success story, however, is a medal at the Olympic Games. The Swiss Athletics federation therefore launched a special project called «Next Level» last autumn. It aims to increase the chances of winning medals in Los Angeles in 2028. It is financed through the support of several foundations, outside the federation's budget. Philipp Bandi, Head of High-Performance Sports at Swiss Athletics, says: «It is important to us to invest in the very best without weakening the foundational work.»

The project includes various measures, among them the targeted promotion of eight athletes who are attested to have the potential for an Olympic medal. Audrey Werro is one of them. She also benefits from her coach now being employed as a national coach by the federation. Christiane Berset Nuoffer had previously taken a break from her profession as a teacher; now she can continue to focus entirely on sports.

This is, as it were, the final step in a system that has always worked very well in Switzerland: club coaches grow with their athletes and do not have to hand them over to professionals in the federation at some point, but rather remain within the system. This was already the case with Andy Vögtli, who accompanied André Bucher all the way to the World Championship title. After Bucher's retirement, Vögtli returned to his club TV Beromünster.

Christiane Berset Nuoffer began her continuous education early on, step by step, and recently passed the exams for the highest Swiss coaching diploma. She also continues to train at CA Belfaux, where three young men can be deployed as ideal sparring partners for Werro in tough sessions.

It is also interesting that at the end of 2024, long-time middle-distance coach Louis Heyer separated from Swiss Athletics because he said he believed he could not work professionally enough at the federation. Today, he leads a professional team financed by the outfitter On. For Werro, however, the conditions within the federation are now apparently professional enough.
