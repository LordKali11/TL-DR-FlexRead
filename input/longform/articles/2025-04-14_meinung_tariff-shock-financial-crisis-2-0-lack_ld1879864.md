# Tariff shock and financial crisis 2.0: The lack of respect for America's institutions is dangerous

*Trade isolation is damaging the US. Paradoxically, Trump's supporters will suffer the most. It will be even more costly if trust in the reliability of the US is lost. Europe and Switzerland should seize this opportunity.*

Who would have thought that things could change so fundamentally with the inauguration of an American president?

Just recently, the talk of the stock markets was American exceptionalism, the extraordinary productivity of American (tech) companies. Europe was considered to be economically lagging behind the USA.

Just twelve weeks into Donald Trump's second term, fears of an economic crisis are circulating. Added to this is a looming loss of confidence in the American market and the dollar, which could escalate into a second financial crisis. Signs of this were mounting before Donald Trump yielded to market pressure and suspended his high country-specific tariffs for an initial period of 90 days.

## One economic absurdity after another

Many investors breathed a sigh of relief, and the stock market recouped some of its heavy losses. This temporarily overshadowed the fact that Trump had replaced his «reciprocal» tariffs with an additional 10 percent import duty, that the 25 percent tariffs on steel and aluminum goods and on numerous products from Canada and Mexico remained in place, and that the trade dispute with China had completely escalated. American companies are now likely more dependent on supplies from China than vice versa.

Trump's measures represent a departure from free global trade and cloud the US growth prospects. The only uncertainties are the extent and magnitude of Trump's tariff shock – and how much of it can be negotiated away.

The tariffs, from which Trump hopes for reindustrialization, greater prosperity, and higher revenues, will not have the desired effect. They will make the US poorer. Americans will have to produce more themselves, and at a higher cost. They will be able to buy less relatively cheaply from abroad and instead sell other goods back to those countries at relatively high prices.

Trump's fury against foreign trade surpluses is nonsensical – both in terms of the focus on goods trade and the requirement that trade with each trading partner be balanced individually.

The chronic US trade deficits in goods are not caused by foreign manufacturers exploiting the US by offering their goods at rock-bottom prices. The United States achieves large surpluses in the services balance. The remaining deficits in trade in goods and services arise because Americans want to consume more than they could afford in a closed economy. They save less than they invest. The difference is financed by capital imports from abroad, the reflection of which is the trade deficit. Should foreign companies now be forced to invest en masse in the reindustrialization of the US, this would only exacerbate the deficits – quite apart from the fact that it is entirely unclear where the skilled workers would come from.

## America's strength - America's problem

Such economic absurdity is truly baffling. However, this illogic can at least be partially explained politically.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 9475854b864e28763259aad997990451]

The American economy is characterized by outstanding entrepreneurial spirit, restrained regulation, and great dynamism. This is America's strength; per capita economic output in the US has more than doubled in real terms since 1980. However, this has primarily benefited investors and top earners. The purchasing power parity-adjusted median hourly wage of workers, on the other hand, has stagnated for decades and has grown by a mere tenth since 1980. This is America's weakness.

This unequal development likely explains the widespread frustration among the middle class of feeling shortchanged. Perennial campaigner Donald Trump capitalizes on this, suggesting in a nationalist-populist manner that foreign powers and globalization are to blame. Using the language of construction workers, which he knows intimately, the real estate mogul promises to finally bring about change.

The paradox is that Trump's policies will hit his own constituency the hardest. Low-income earners will be hit the hardest, relative to their disposable income, if Chinese toys and household appliances suddenly cost significantly more, and their stock-invested pension plans lose value.

But Donald Trump and his highly unorthodox economic advisors clearly want to radically transform the US. They seem to be counting on their clientele accepting an economic downturn of estimated one to two percent in the short term, as predicted by equilibrium models. However, they are likely overlooking second-round effects, which are potentially far more disruptive.

## Lack of respect for institutions

The US president's constant announcements on his news service «TruthSocial» continually change the rules. No one seems to know what will happen next. This creates uncertainty and leads to the postponement of investment and production decisions. Uncertainty is poison for the economy.

At the same time, trust in the USA is waning. This is related to statements from Trump's team that question the status of a strong dollar as the world's reserve currency, but the reasons run deeper.

The American president often bases his radical measures on emergency decrees. While these can be challenged in court, it takes time for decisions to become legally binding. At the same time, Trump's Attorney General Pamela Bondi and her entourage are, in an unprecedented manner, questioning the binding nature of judicial rulings. Employees who express criticism face immediate dismissal.

In other respects, too, the self-proclaimed champions of free speech seem unwilling to tolerate dissenting opinions any longer. Companies that don't immediately and publicly renounce all diversity policies are punished and lose contracts. Universities suspected of conducting unwelcome research have their public funding withdrawn. Researchers and students suspected, for whatever reason, of not supporting Trump's policies risk losing their residency permits.

It is still difficult to assess the true extent of these changes. However, they are fueling the impression of arbitrariness and a lack of legal certainty. This has already led to some European managers canceling appointments in the US because they fear arbitrary harassment at the border.

Uncertainty prevails regarding what made the USA what it is today. Until now, the best and most innovative researchers wanted to move to the USA because they felt freest and in the best company there. The most creative startups aspired to relocate to the USA because the opportunities and the capital market there seemed limitless. Should this change, should autocratic tendencies increase, or should respect for the rule of law and the separation of powers decrease, the USA would hardly remain the world's most innovative and dynamic economy for much longer.

It is hoped that American checks and balances will prove resilient, that Trump will return to a degree of pragmatism, and that he will correct his mistakes. It is also hoped that the excesses of the Maga movement will be reined in by the markets and the voters. The American economy is strong and will hardly give up so easily.

These days, investors are using the acronym TINA: «There is no alternative»; there is no alternative to the American markets and the dollar. But investors are relentless, as demonstrated by the Guilt crisis in Great Britain, which forced then-Prime Minister Liz Truss to resign in 2022. Should Trump further undermine market participants' confidence in the creditworthiness and rule of law of the US, this could trigger sharp sell-offs. Global liquidity could dry up, and the trade dispute could escalate into a second financial crisis.

## Now should be Europe's hour strike

Fortunately, it hasn't come to that yet. And despite all the frustration, the Trump shock also presents an opportunity for Europe and Switzerland.

Both sides would be well advised to refrain from countermeasures that would only harm themselves. Liberalization proposals that dismantle remaining tariff and non-tariff barriers, on the other hand, can steer the dynamic in a positive direction. The realization that, independent of the US, more must be invested in national security and the protection of freedom is long overdue.

The realization may be growing in international markets that European countries have their own advantages.

While Europe, in its decentralized diversity, is more bureaucratic and less dynamic than the USA, the developed European states offer a relatively high degree of the rule of law. The separation of powers functions, science is free, and the bureaucracy consists not of sheriffs but of employees who are meant to provide services for citizens and the public. The vast majority of Europeans have access to a generally good education, and innovation is high in many countries. The income gap has not widened as much as in the USA, and social welfare systems ensure (perhaps now too much) that those who lose out from economic developments are compensated.

It is now more important than ever to safeguard these advantages while simultaneously recognizing that Europe benefits from efficient transnational integration. As the Draghi Report shows, the EU single market is not yet complete. It should be deepened. Only an integrated European capital market with common rules could become a viable alternative to the American markets. Cooperation in the defense sector must be expanded, and Switzerland should be formally integrated into it. Furthermore, Europe must adopt American standards in terms of performance orientation, business-friendliness, and deregulation. In this way, the old continent can eventually return to the best of all possible worlds.

However, the worst possible outcome would be if Europe were to follow the negative developments in the US, polarization were to spiral out of control, and European states were to retreat into isolationist nationalism with traits of autocratic arbitrariness. Hopefully, thanks to Trump, Europe's populations and governments will recognize the gravity of the situation and allow the time for an open, integrated, and liberal Europe to arrive.
