# SpaceX will have the biggest IPO in history: These are the ten most important questions and answers

*SpaceX, the aerospace company, is going public, fueling euphoria in the technology sector. Investors are hoping for rising share prices, but the IPO carries risks.*

It is a debut of superlatives: SpaceX goes public on Friday. The company operates in the fields of space travel, satellites and artificial intelligence (AI) and is controlled by Elon Musk. The transaction could raise around $75 billion for SpaceX.

This would make the IPO the largest in the world to date. The current record holder is the 2019 Initial Public Offering (IPO) of the oil company Saudi Aramco, which raised $29.4 billion.

The total value of SpaceX would subsequently be around $1.8 trillion. With the SpaceX IPO, only about 5% of the company's shares will be freely tradable. The company is offering 555.6 million shares at a fixed price of $135 per share. SpaceX is set to be traded on the Nasdaq technology exchange under the ticker symbol «SPCX». These are the most important questions and answers about the IPO.

## 1. What does SpaceX consist of?

SpaceX is a type of conglomerate, a diversified group of companies. Some are successful, others less so. On the one hand, the company includes the rocket division, which was the first to successfully send rockets into space, land parts of them back on Earth under control, and reuse them.

In February of this year, SpaceX also acquired Elon Musk's AI company xAI for $250 billion, which includes the social network X, formerly Twitter. In addition, there is the Connectivity division with its Starlink satellite network. This brings satellite internet to the most remote corners of the world. It is the only profitable division – the rest are operating at a loss.

Investment experts at Liechtenstein's VP Bank commented that instead of a homogeneous business model, SpaceX combines several segments with very different revenue models. They noted that in the past, the largest U.S. technology companies had compelling business models that required little capital and generated high margins. Now, they said, the focus in the sector is shifting «from stable cash flows to uncertain future expectations.»

SpaceX is a prime example of this, the bank's experts said. They see the Starlink satellite internet business as the group's only predictable pillar. The space business, with its rocket launches, is structurally weaker and capital-intensive, they added. Regarding future projects – namely the rocket systems and AI data centers – they noted that high investments would be met with long development cycles and uncertain monetization.

## 2. How should SpaceX's high valuation be interpreted?

SpaceX generated $18.7 billion in revenue and a loss of $4.9 billion last year. In this context, the expected valuation of around $1.8 trillion is extremely high. For comparison, companies in the S&P 500 stock index were valued at roughly three times their annual revenue at the end of 2025 – SpaceX's estimated valuation would be equivalent to 100 times the company's annual revenue.

Investors in SpaceX should be aware that they are buying into a big bet. It could pay off – or it might not.

In its prospectus, SpaceX states astronomically high goals: Among other things, the company wants to produce systems and technologies to make life interplanetary and «carry the light of consciousness to the stars.»

First, a lunar base is to be established, and then Mars is to be colonized. Data centers are also planned for space, which could be used to train and operate artificial intelligence. SpaceX intends to use its rockets for transport into space, while its Starlink division will supply the necessary satellites.

The company sees future markets in areas such as space tourism, space freight, industrial production in space or «asteroid mining» – the extraction of metals such as nickel, cobalt or rare earth elements from minor planets.

## 3. How much control will Elon Musk exercise?

SpaceX has two different share classes. Investors in Class A shares receive one vote per share, while those in Class B shares receive 10 votes per share. Since Elon Musk will receive almost 94% of the Class B shares, he will hold a particularly large amount of voting power and can thereby effectively control the company.

This raises the question of how independently SpaceX can operate. Musk has interwoven many of his companies. For example, xAI's AI is also used for Tesla, and for the past year, xAI has also included the social network X, formerly Twitter. Analysts suspect that Musk could be aiming for a merger of Tesla and SpaceX. This dense corporate web poses a concentration risk, and Musk's consolidation of power could lead to conflicts of interest.

## 4. How rich will Elon Musk become through the IPO?

Musk's stake in SpaceX, based on the targeted offering price of $135 per share, is likely to be worth approximately $866 billion. Since he also holds Tesla shares worth about $350 billion, this could make Musk the world's first dollar trillionaire.

However, over 1 billion of the SpaceX shares issued to Musk are tied to performance targets and subject to a lock-up period: The prospectus states that Musk will receive 1 billion performance-based Class B shares if SpaceX achieves certain market capitalization goals and can establish «a permanent human colony on Mars with at least one million inhabitants.» He will receive a further 302 million shares if, in addition to meeting market value targets, he has completed data centers in space that deliver 100 terawatts of computing power per year.

## 5. What opportunities does the SpaceX IPO offer investors?

According to a report by the Reuters news agency on Tuesday, investor demand for SpaceX shares had already reached $250 billion.

The IPOs of major U.S. technology companies are currently a dominant theme on the stock market. Other tech firms are poised for their own IPOs, including the AI companies Anthropic and OpenAI. The SpaceX IPO could become a litmus test of whether the market can absorb several mega-IPOs within a short period, according to VP Bank.

If SpaceX's IPO is a success, it could boost sentiment on the stock market and open the door to further IPOs.

## 6. What risks do investors face when investing in SpaceX?

Meanwhile, the IPO evokes unpleasant memories of the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s. Investment experts at asset manager Feri commented that companies like SpaceX are currently using the environment «to raise capital on the market at ambitious valuations.» They noted that during the New Economy boom, the mega-IPOs of that era had been harbingers of the bursting of the dot-com bubble.

If the currently planned IPOs take place at the expected valuations, these companies would face the threat of disappointing returns in the medium term, Feri added.

Representatives of the financial institution Bantleon, however, pointed out that the technology and communication sectors now account for almost 50% of the S&P 500, the U.S. blue-chip index. During the dot-com bubble, they noted, the corresponding figure had been «only» 40%. They added that this is undoubtedly a risk factor in the medium term and that even slight doubts about the AI bet could lead to noticeable price declines on the stock markets.

The environment for IPOs is generally favorable when there is a positive mood on the stock market, or ideally even a certain degree of euphoria. However, due to the cyclical nature of the stock market, this can be a poor entry point for investors.

## 7. How can private investors invest in SpaceX?

It is primarily U.S. investment banks that are taking SpaceX public. Swiss or German private investors are generally not clients of these companies. Therefore, it is difficult to participate directly in the IPO.

After the IPO, private investors can buy SpaceX shares on the stock exchange. However, they should be aware of the risks of such an investment. Tax implications should also be considered.

## 8. What impact will the IPO have on the major stock market indices?

Normally, stocks are only included in stock indices after a certain period following their initial public offering (IPO). The index providers aim to monitor the performance of the respective company. The exact timeframes and methodology for inclusion depend on the index regulations of the different providers.

According to investment experts at VP Bank, major index providers have announced they will relax their rules to ensure faster inclusion of SpaceX shares. S&P, however, will reportedly not include SpaceX until at least 12 months after its IPO, forgoing an accelerated index adjustment. Consequently, the path to the S&P 500 benchmark U.S. stock market index could be somewhat longer for SpaceX and the other U.S. tech companies going public.

The shares are also likely to appear in several global stock indices soon. According to an analysis by the consulting firm PPCmetrics, SpaceX shares are expected to be included in the relevant benchmarks of index provider MSCI. These include, for example, the MSCI World and MSCI ACWI global stock indices, and the U.S. benchmark MSCI USA. Large Swiss investors primarily use MSCI indices to track the performance of global stocks.

According to PPCmetrics, the MSCI indices do not take into account the total enterprise value of SpaceX, but only the free float – that is, the shares traded on the stock exchange.

## 9. What does inclusion in the indices mean for investors?

The impact on passive investors – those invested in index funds and ETFs – is manageable for now. SpaceX is expected to represent less than 0.13% of the major MSCI indices at the time of its IPO, according to estimates by PPCmetrics. Musk's company is therefore unlikely to be among the 100 largest companies in the MSCI World stock index. The consulting firm thus sees no need for action on the part of investors whose portfolios are based on MSCI indices.

However, that would only reflect the situation at the time of the IPO. This could change if Musk later releases more SpaceX shares into the free float or if the company's stock price rises significantly.

Meanwhile, strong demand for SpaceX shares from index funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) could drive the stock price higher. According to a Bloomberg report, passive investors could hold around 30% of SpaceX's free-floating shares after 15 trading days.

## 10. What does the IPO mean for the economy?

The prospect of SpaceX launching the largest IPO in history also affects other companies. The IPO is set to absorb a great deal of liquidity on the capital market – capital that will then be unavailable to other firms planning to go public or that are already listed.

In addition, after the mega-IPO from SpaceX, two more major IPOs are expected this year from OpenAI and Anthropic. This wave of IPOs among tech firms suggests that private funding for AI investments is gradually becoming scarce, prompting them to turn to the public markets, where retail investors can also participate.
