In his book «The Intelligent Asset Allocation,» neurologist and financial expert William Bernstein described retirement as «the greatest risk of all.» The aging population is predictably pushing the pay-as-you-go system of the German state pension to its limits. A survey by Postbank shows that roughly a third of Germans are worried about building up private retirement savings. The current draft of the retirement savings account could help more people build up an additional, funded pension, as it combines capital market-based savings with generous government subsidies.

The government's draft legislation promises state subsidies and tax advantages for users of the new retirement savings account. The primary target group of the SPD-led Finance Ministry is people with lower incomes. However, the subsidies and tax advantages could also be attractive to those with higher incomes, for example, as part of an investment strategy to generate an additional monthly income stream in retirement. This is because the lump-sum payment at the start of retirement is limited to 30 percent of the capital.

Carmen Bandt, managing director of Kidron Asset Management in Stuttgart, believes that a retirement savings account is a very good idea. «A politically supported retirement provision is something that every country needs. Especially when it becomes clear that the pay-as-you-go system of the statutory pension will continue to face problems.»

## The idea: Riester reloaded

The retirement savings account is set to replace the Riester pension in January 2027. This form of retirement savings, named after the then Minister of Labor, Walter Riester, has a simple principle: Savers regularly invest money in an approved financial product and receive government subsidies. These products have been certified in advance by the Federal Central Tax Office. The agency does not assess the quality of the savings products, but rather whether they comply with legal requirements and thus meet the criteria for receiving subsidies.

Initially, the Riester pension scheme generated considerable demand, but this has declined more and more in recent years.

The main reasons are high costs and low returns. This is because providers have to guarantee minimum payouts, which reduces their profitability.

The planned retirement savings account aims to improve on this. While security-oriented guaranteed products will likely continue to be available, currently, options with a contribution guarantee of 80 or 100 percent are planned. This means that 80 or 100 percent of the contributions paid in will be available at the start of the payout phase. However, return-oriented versions without guarantees are also planned. A 100 percent equity allocation should be possible. These are significant improvements.

Actively managed funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) tracking national and international, broadly diversified stock indices would therefore be an option. This allows investors to benefit from the fact that, in the long term, as is typical for financial planning for retirement, the performance of stocks tends to compensate for temporary periods of weakness.

## **Consistent deposits, more stable returns**

Furthermore, a standard product is to be offered for the subsidized retirement savings scheme. A specific legal framework is to be established for this purpose. The previous government had designated the German sovereign wealth fund Kenfo for this, which developed an ETF-like investment model for the equity-based pension that was planned at the time.

To prevent costs from significantly impacting returns, fees are to be capped at a maximum of 1.5 percent per year. ETFs should have no problem complying with this limit. Products tracking the MSCI World, for example, cost between 0.1 and 0.5 percent of the invested amount annually, as a study by J.P. Morgan Asset Management, among others, shows. It is hoped that low-cost ETF options will also be available at launch.

It may be somewhat more difficult with actively managed equity funds. Their annual management fees can easily exceed 1.5 percent. However, these fees have fallen in recent years and averaged 1.18 percent in 2024, according to the European Fund and Asset Management Association (EFAMA) . Many fund companies are likely to launch retirement savings products that comply with the fee limit in order to benefit from government-subsidized demand.

The initial costs for retirement savings accounts should be spread over the entire term to prevent double charges in the event of a contract change. Savvy investors can now largely avoid such front-end loads with open ETF savings plans. If this isn't guaranteed for retirement savings accounts, it could significantly reduce their appeal.

## Payout severely restricted

Even during the payout phase, the retirement savings account is intended to offer more flexibility than the Riester pension and grants the saver a range of choices. However, only a maximum of 30 percent of the accumulated capital can be withdrawn at the start of retirement; the remainder is paid out as a monthly pension.

There are two options for monthly payouts. Option one is a lifetime annuity, where a fixed amount is paid out every month until death. Option two provides for a fixed-term payout plan that is intended to run at least until the age of 85. Once the accumulated sum is exhausted, there are no further payments. Any unused assets can also be inherited.

«The fact that the one-time payout is limited to 30 percent is a major disadvantage for investors,» says Rolf Adam, founder of the fee-based advisory firm Adam Investments in Hamburg. He considers the regulation excessive. «Are German citizens really so incapable of making their own decisions that they need to be protected from themselves? I don’t think so.» Despite this drawback, the retirement savings account could still be attractive to many investors because of the subsidies and tax advantages.

## Phased funding planned

For annual contributions of up to €1,200, the government pays 30 cents for every euro invested. From 2029, this will increase to 35 cents. Those wishing to invest more than €1,200 will also receive additional support: contributions between €1,201 and €1,800 will be subsidized by 20 cents for every euro invested. Under the Riester pension scheme, this subsidy was fixed at €175, provided the minimum income-dependent contribution was paid.

In addition, there is a child allowance: It amounts to 25 percent of the sum paid in the contribution year up to a personal contribution of 1200 euros and is therefore capped at 300 euros per child.

Even more interesting is the approach for savers under 25. Those who open a contract at this age receive an additional one-off bonus of 200 euros. Young people will also receive an increased allowance of 35 cents per euro for the first 1200 euros.

Those currently participating in a Riester pension scheme should have the option to switch to the new system. However, it should also be possible to continue saving for retirement under the existing subsidized pension scheme.

## Taxes will be due later

No taxes are due during the savings phase. However, the deposits can be deducted as special expenses, thus reducing the tax liability.

During the payout phase, however, the returns must be taxed at the individual's tax rate. This rate is usually lower in retirement than during one's working life. Because the returns are not taxed as income from capital gains, they are not subject to social security contributions, as is currently being discussed for regular stock market returns. The retirement savings account is disadvantageous at payout for investors whose individual tax rate in retirement is higher than the flat withholding tax rate of 25 percent that applies to unsubsidized capital investments.

With a retirement savings account, investors also benefit from the fact that the compound interest effect, or the return, is not reduced by annual tax payments. In contrast, with actively managed funds and ETFs in conventional accounts, German custodian banks deduct an advance lump sum for the tax office every year, even if the investor has not sold any fund units. A base interest rate of 2.53 percent applies for the calculation in 2025.

For many investors, the tax advantages of a retirement savings account can be attractive. If they contribute €1,800, they can claim this amount, plus the €480 government subsidy, as special expenses on their tax return, totaling €2,280. This could result in several hundred euros in tax savings per year. If investors also invest the saved taxes in a separate ETF savings plan alongside their retirement savings account, this increases the capital that is immediately available at retirement.

If a 47-year-old invests in an equity ETF for twenty years, during which no taxes are levied, they could accumulate well over €50,000 by the time they retire at age 67, assuming an average annual return of around 5 percent and annual costs of 1 percent for the retirement savings account. However, five years before retirement, only half of the capital can be invested in a higher risk category, which reduces the potential returns.

A lump-sum payout of 30 percent would then amount to well over €15,000, before taxes (based on the individual tax rate). The pension until age 85 should then yield more than €150 per month. Those who invested the taxes saved on their retirement savings account through special deductions in an equity ETF should, even after deducting the applicable withholding tax, have significantly more than €5,000 from this freely available savings plan after twenty years.

## Special economic boom for German financial service providers

The reactions from practitioners to the draft are largely positive. «More capital market, less bureaucracy,» says Björn Deyer, Head of Pensions, Insurance, and IFA at the asset management company DWS. He adds that policymakers have finally taken up the reform proposals that have been on the table for some time. In fact, the pension account is based on an idea from the previous government, which was not implemented due to the premature end of the red-green coalition.

Online brokers like Comdirect are closely monitoring developments. «As a bank, we are naturally considering a suitable and attractive product offering that we plan to launch in January 2027,» a spokesperson told «The Market.» They also intend to identify suitable products for standard custody accounts that comply with legal fee regulations. Scalable stated that it is already clear they will offer a product.

The reform is likely to create a boom for fund management companies, brokers, and banks. «The Market» last recommended DWS shares at the end of September and continues to consider them attractive.

## There is a good chance that the draft will become law

In the last cabinet meeting of the year, the draft bill was adopted as a bill , under the name Pension Reform Act . It must now go through the further legislative process.

Experts are optimistic about this future path. Andreas Irion, president of the Federal Association of Pension Consultants, cites two reasons for this. Firstly, the proposals are based on the draft from the previous government, which was well received. Secondly, the Riester pension has become an almost universally negatively perceived form of retirement provision. He therefore expects the draft legislation to be largely passed.

Overall, this approach can help to significantly advance private and capital market-linked retirement savings. While it doesn't offer the same freedom of disposal as a conventional, open-ended savings plan, it does provide substantial subsidies and tax advantages.
